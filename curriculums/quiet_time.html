Being Still In a Busy World

Picture this.

Alarms are blaring. People are rushing around the house. Everyone’s late. Everyone’s stressed. Doors slam, cars speed off, and the day explodes into chaos.

That’s life for most of us.

Everything moves fast, and because the world never slows down, we start to believe we’re not allowed to either. We stay busy even when we don’t need to be busy. And if we’re not careful, days go by where we do everything except spend time with Jesus.

But Jesus lived a busy life too.

Crowds followed Him. People demanded His time. Needs never stopped. If anyone knew pressure and pace, it was Him. So what did He do?

He got alone with the Father.

Mark 1:35 says that very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus left the house, went to a solitary place, and prayed. Not because it was convenient. Not because He had free time. But because He needed that time with God.

Think about that.

The Savior of the world began His day with silence, solitude, and prayer. If Jesus needed a quiet time, how much more do we?

This isn’t optional. It’s essential. Quiet time is the fuel for your faith, the anchor in your chaos, the place where your heart lines up with God before the world starts pulling at it.

So today we’re going to break this down using what Jesus modeled in Mark 1:35. We’re going to walk through the Power P’s of Quiet Time, and we'll add a few more things so we understand not just when and where, but how to meet with God in a way that actually changes you.

1. Priority

We make time for what matters to us. If we have no time at all to spend time with the Lord, it shows us really how much we care about that relationship with Him.

“Watch and pray that you may not enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.””

Matthew 26:41 ESV

Keep watch and what? Pray!

This is a command! Pray!

The only way God can speak to us is through his word. God speaks through his word and we communicate to him through prayer.

It's obvious that we don't all have it together, but He does. We need His help.

If we're not reading then how do we hear?

If we don't pray, then how is He hearing from us?

If we don't make it a priority, then none of it's happening.

Matthew 6:33- "But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you.”

It clearly teaches order: God first, everything else follows.

“O God, you are my God; earnestly I seek you; my soul thirsts for you; my flesh faints for you, as in a dry and weary land where there is no water.”

2. Place

“But when you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret. And your Father who sees in secret will reward you.”

Matthew 6:6 ESV

Make a place! Where's yours?

It doesn't need to be your bed. You'll be counting sheep instead of talking to the Sheppard. Don't let it be so comfortable that you'll fall asleep.

Make sure it's away from distractions.

I think it's impossible to have a quiet time in a place that ain't quiet.

8. Purpose

There's a great purpose for you meeting with God.

It is not primarily for all the things you can receive from Him, but for us to be able to meet with him.

Piper- God is most glorified in us when we're most satisfied in him

Analogy- A young Christian came to his pastor and said, “Pastor, I read my Bible, I pray, I am serious about it, but I don’t seem to be getting anything out of it; what is wrong with me?”

The pastor quietly prayed and then said, “See that wicker basket on my desk? Empty it.” The boy did. Then the pastor said, “Now go fill it with water from the fountain and bring it back.”

The boy protested, “Pastor, that basket won’t hold water!” The pastor said, “Do it anyway.” He went, filled it, and of course all the water spilled through; when he returned, the pastor said, “Yes, the basket won’t hold water, but look, it is a lot cleaner, isn’t it?”

The point is, don’t worry about what you are getting out of your quiet time; just show up, obey, and pour yourself out. Like any relationship, growth is gradual; quiet time cultivates your relationship with God, even when you don’t see immediate results.

9. Protection

In the Old Testament times, the high priest was put in place to protect the place of God

“But he took his stand in the midst of the plot and defended it and struck down the Philistines, and the Lord worked a great victory.”

2 Samuel 23:12 ESV

Shammah held his ground in the middle of the field and beat back the Philistines

We have to hold our ground and guard our quiet time.

There are hundreds of things that can compete with our time.

Guard it with your life.

I've asked the Lord to make my day miserable until I spend time with Him

I don't care what's going on in our lives. We have to remove or add whatever needs to happen to make sure it gets done.

⁃ Remove the distractions

⁃ Add time to the schedule

“Hold your ground; no distraction, no excuse, no compromise.
“If it’s important to God, it should be non‑negotiable in your schedule.”

In a world that never slows down, our quiet time is where we stop, show up, and pour ourselves out to God, wholeheartedly, intentionally, and without compromise. Because, it’s in that daily rhythm of priority, place, prayer, praise, and protection that our souls are strengthened, our hearts are aligned, our souls are fed, and God is glorified.”

And then we can go tackle our day.
