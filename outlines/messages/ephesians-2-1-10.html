Ephesians 2:1–10
From Death to Life

1. Who You Were — Dead in Sin (Ephesians 2:1–3)

“And you were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked…”
Paul begins this passage by reminding us who we were before Christ.

What is the difference between “trespasses” and “sins”?

Sin is to miss the mark. Imagine an archer aiming at a target but failing to hit the bullseye. Sin is falling short of the perfect standard of God. We have all missed the mark.

We have said things we should not have said, done things we should not have done, and thought things we should not have thought.

If you think you have never missed the mark, have you ever crossed a line against another person? Have you ever said something you should not have said, done something you should not have done, thought something you should not have thought, or treated someone in a way you knew was wrong?

You missed the mark.

Trespasses are crossing a known boundary. It is the idea of stepping over a line that has been established. You know where the line is, but you cross it anyway. You know what is right, but you choose what is wrong.

Therefore, when Paul uses both “trespasses” and “sins,” he is emphasizing the complete nature of our rebellion against God. We have missed the mark of a holy and perfect God, crossed boundaries He has established, failed Him through our sin, and rebelled against Him through our disobedience.

When we sin against other people, we are ultimately sinning against God because those people were created in His image. When you cross a line against another person, you have also violated God's standard.

Who Were You?

Paul says that we were dead in sin, misdirected, misguided, blinded to the truth, slaves to sin, lovers of darkness, and children of wrath.

What Does It Mean to Be Spiritually Dead?

Think back to Genesis. Adam and Eve disobeyed God. They were deceived, disregarded the Lord's command, and sin entered the world.

Because of Adam's sin, death entered humanity.

Every person is born physically alive but spiritually separated from God.

Death does not mean extinction or non-existence. Death means separation. Physical death is the separation of the body from physical life. Spiritual death is separation from God.

The Bible describes the spiritually dead person as someone enslaved to sin, loving darkness, and blinded to the truth.

2 Corinthians 4:4 (ESV):
“In their case the god of this world has blinded the minds of the unbelievers…”

Satan has blinded the minds of unbelievers. This does not mean they have physically lost their ability to see. It means they are spiritually unable to see the truth of the gospel apart from the work of God.

Satan has influenced the thoughts, reasoning, opinions, feelings, and beliefs of those who are spiritually dead.

This is why humanity needs a Savior. By our own strength, we are doomed to fall short of the perfection of God.

Children of Wrath

Ephesians 2:3 says that we were: “By nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind.”

This is a difficult truth. We rightfully deserve God's judgment because we have sinned against Him.

James 4:4 (ESV): “Friendship with the world is enmity with God.”

John 8:44 (ESV): “You are of your father the devil, and your will is to do your father's desires.”

Romans 2:5 (ESV): “You are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath when God's righteous judgment will be revealed.”

The Dam Illustration

Imagine a dam. Every sin is like another drop of water being added: one lie, one act of hatred, one lustful thought, one act of disobedience, one moment of pride.

The dam continues to fill.

God's righteous judgment is building against sin.

And the frightening reality is this:

We deserve God's wrath.

But this does not make God cruel or evil. God is perfectly righteous and just.

The same God who judges sin is the God who provided a way for us to be saved from that judgment.

He sent His Son, Jesus Christ.

What Do You Think About When You Think of Salvation?

When you think about salvation, you may think about heaven, forgiveness, eternal life, or being saved from sin.

But how often do you think about being saved from the wrath of God that we rightly deserve? Salvation is not merely being saved from a bad life.

Salvation is being rescued from the judgment that our sin deserves.

And God Himself provided the rescue.

2. But God — Made Alive by Grace (Ephesians 2:4–7)

The first three verses are terrifying.

Paul begins by telling us that we were dead, disobedient, enslaved, blinded, and children of wrath.

Then verse 4 changes everything.

“But God…”

Those may be two of the most beautiful words in the Bible.

We were dead—but God.

We were enslaved—but God.

We were children of wrath—but God.

We were unable to save ourselves—but God.

Why did God save us?

“Because of the great love with which he loved us.”

And because of His rich mercy.

Mercy is withholding what someone deserves.

We deserve judgment, but God shows mercy.

Grace is giving what someone does not deserve.

We do not deserve salvation, but God gives us salvation through Jesus Christ. Because of Christ, we are made alive, saved, covered, protected, and loved. Paul says that God:

“Made us alive together with Christ.”

The spiritually dead person has been given life. The person separated from God has been brought near. The person who could not save himself has been saved by God.

You Do Not Have to Live Dead Anymore

Notice the wording in verse 2:

“In which you once walked…”

You once walked in those sins.

You once lived that way.

You once followed the course of this world.

But you do not have to walk that way anymore.

You are not dead anymore.

You have been made alive.

And if you have been made alive, your life should look different.

You walk differently.

You think differently.

You speak differently.

You act differently.

You no longer live like the person you used to be.

Why Do Some People Who Have Been Made Alive Continue Living Dead?

Christ has done the work to make us alive. He accomplished what we could never accomplish on our own.

Yet some people who claim to be alive in Christ continue to live as if they are dead. Why?

Perhaps they still love the world. Perhaps they have not truly surrendered. Perhaps they want the benefits of Christ without the lordship of Christ. Perhaps they have forgotten what Christ saved them from.

We should all ask ourselves:

Why would I return to the grave after Christ has given me life?

My Testimony:

There was a time when I had been made alive, but I went back to living like I was dead. I had been given life, but I returned to my coffin.

I went back to the same things, the same sins, the same ways of thinking, and the same lifestyle.

I was living like someone who had never been made alive.

But God used people like Bryant and Lilly to help me awaken again.

Through that time, I began to understand the love of God in a deeper way.

I began to understand that God did not merely save me so that I could continue living the same dead life.

Christ gave me life so that I could live.

3. Saved by Grace Through Faith (Ephesians 2:8–10)

This is the main point of the passage.

“For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God…”

Salvation is by grace. Grace means that God gives us what we do not deserve. Salvation is through faith. Faith is trusting in what Christ has done.

Salvation is not of your own doing. You did not save yourself.

Salvation is the gift of God. You cannot earn a gift. You receive it.

Paul continues:

“Not a result of works, so that no one may boast.”

You cannot stand before God and say, “I was good enough,” “I did enough,” or “I earned my way into heaven.”

Salvation is not about what you have accomplished. Salvation is about what Christ HAS accomplished.

You were dead.

You could not make yourself alive.

A dead person cannot revive himself.

The dead cannot save themselves.

But God made you alive through Christ.

Therefore, salvation is by grace through faith—not because of your works.

Final Thought

Ephesians 2:1–10 takes us through the entire story of salvation.

Who were you? Dead in trespasses and sins.

What did you deserve? The righteous wrath of God.

What did God do? He showed mercy because of His great love. What did Christ accomplish? He made the dead alive.

How are you saved? By grace through faith.

What are you now? God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works. You were not saved by good works.

But you were saved for good works.

You were not saved because you were alive.

You were made alive so that you could live.

The question is not simply: “Have I been saved?”

The question is also: “Now that I have been made alive, am I still living like I am dead?”
