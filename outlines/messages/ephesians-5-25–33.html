Ephesians 5:25–33
Husbands

Paul has just addressed the wives and their responsibility to submit to their husbands. Now he turns his attention to the husbands.

The wife's role can be summed up in the word submit.

The husband's role can be summed up in the word love.

But the kind of love Paul commands husbands to have is not ordinary love. It is the kind of love Christ has for the church.

1. Husbands Are Called to Sacrificial Love

Verse 25

“Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her.”

This comparison of wives to the church shows the weight of the analogy.

The same way the church is submissive unto Christ shows the submission the wife takes on for her husband.

Likewise, the love the husband has for his wife is meant to display the love that Christ has for the church.

Men, you are responsible for your family.

That is a task that carries more weight than we can bear on our own. The only way we can accomplish this responsibility is through the power of the Holy Spirit guiding us.

The wives have been given a difficult command: to submit to their husbands.

Now the husbands are given a difficult command: to love their wives as Christ loved the church. What a task!

Christ is the Savior of the church, and Paul compares the husband's responsibility to the way Christ cares for the church.

We are to be a picture of Christ to our wives.

The wife’s role can be summed up by submission.

The husband’s role can be summed up by love.

But this is not simply emotional love.

The Greek word agape describes a love that is a decision over feelings and the mind over emotion.

It is sacrificial love.

It is life-changing love.

It is a love that gives itself for another person.

Christ's love for us was demonstrated through action. He willingly gave Himself up for us.

That same quality of love is now possible for believers because the very essence of that love lives in the believer through the Spirit of Christ.

This is a different kind of love than the butterflies-in-the-stomach kind of puppy love. This is sacrificial love.

This love changes things.

For those of you who are not married, I would encourage you to look deeply into your relationship and into yourself and ask whether you are truly ready for the responsibilities and the love that marriage requires.

No wife would mind submitting to a husband who loves her as much as Christ loved the church. I know it is a difficult task, but we have two options.

We can say:

“I can't do that.”

Or we can say:

“I can try.”

That is where we are to be: willing, ready, and committed to loving our wives with everything we have.

A loveless marriage does not please Christ.

Husbands, love your wives.

Leadership is not tyranny.

The husband is not called to control his wife.

He is called to love her.

2. Husbands Are Called to Lead Their Wives Toward Christ

Verse 26

“That he might sanctify her, having cleansed her by the washing of water with the word.”

Paul teaches us about this agape love through an illustration.

In these verses, we can see the past, present, and future work of Christ:

● Past — Justification
● Present — Sanctification
● Future — Glorification

Paul first says that Christ gave Himself up for the church.

1. Christ gave Himself up for her — Redemption

This points to the past.

Christ gave Himself for the church.

Men, it is no longer about you.

Most of us are naturally selfish, but husbands are called to be selfless. Our love must be sacrificial in the way we live and in the way we treat our wives. 2. Christ sanctifies and cleanses her — Sanctification

To sanctify means to set apart as holy.

To cleanse means to take something filthy and make it clean.

The husband has a responsibility to lead his wife toward holiness.

This does not mean that a husband is responsible for saving his wife. Only Christ saves.

But a husband is responsible for the spiritual leadership of his home.

DK explains:

“You gonna look at your wife and see problems and see faults and you are not gonna call her out on every little thing. You are gonna cleanse her through the word and your leadership guiding her through sanctification.”

Romans 10:17 says:

“So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ.” First things first, she needs to be in God's Word.

She needs to be learning and growing in understanding.

But the husband needs to be doing the same.

If you do not understand the importance of Scripture, and if you are not growing in your own understanding, you will not be able to lead your wife spiritually.

Men, it is your duty to lead in this way.

You have the responsibility of leading your wife.

If she is not what you think she should be, you need to look at how you are leading her. It may be that you are not leading her like Christ leads the church.

We need to understand that Christ's goal is not simply to save us from hell.

Christ's goal is also to purify us and set us apart as people who once lived in sin but are now being made holy.

That should be the goal of a married couple.

The husband and wife should work together to become more like Christ and display Christ to the world.

3. Christ perfects her — Glorification

Christ is ultimately preparing the church to be presented in glory.

The husband should desire to help his wife grow in holiness and become more like Christ. The goal is not for the wife to become more like the husband.

The goal is for both husband and wife to become more like Christ.

3. Husbands Are Called to Present Their Wives in Beauty

Verse 27

“So that he might present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that she might be holy and without blemish.”

Paul uses the imagery of holiness and purity.

The high priest, especially in the Day of Atonement, would bring an offering that was without blemish.

The sacrifice had to be pure.

Paul says that Christ desires to present the church in splendor.

That word speaks of beauty and glory.

The LMW Commentary explains:

The husband's love involves having no spot or wrinkle or any such thing. Spots would mean the mistakes that tarnish one's life and marriage, mistakes so serious

that they are very difficult to wash off one's body and out of one's mind. They would include such things as mistreatment and abuse, loose and immoral behavior, and withdrawal and avoidance.

Wrinkles would mean things that cause friction and rattle the nerves and that need to be ironed out. They would include such things as temper and reaction, broken promises and serious neglect, severe selfishness and rejection.

The husband's love involves being holy and without blemish.

The word holy means to be separate and untouched by evil.

A husband's love, if it is genuine, should stir him toward holiness and should help stir his wife toward holiness as well.

The process of ironing things out is significant.

If you run the iron over a wrinkle too quickly, it will not straighten the wrinkle. That is like a husband who simply goes with the flow and refuses to lead his wife. The wrinkles never get addressed.

But if you hold the iron on the wrinkle too long, you will burn through the material. That is like a husband who picks apart his wife over every little thing.

That is not leading out of love.

That is leading out of authority.

A husband must lead with wisdom and love.

Song of Solomon 4:7 says:

“You are altogether beautiful, my love; there is no flaw in you.”

A husband must care for every realm of his wife's life:

1. Physically

2. Mentally

3. Emotionally

4. Spiritually

5. Sexually

The husband is called to care for his wife and help her grow.

She should look more like Christ than when he married her.

The husband needs to look in the mirror.

If he is concerned about the condition of his wife, he should also examine the way he is leading her.

4. Husbands Are Called to Love Their Wives as Their Own Bodies

Verse 28

“In the same way husbands should love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself.”

Paul says that husbands should love their wives as their own bodies.

This is not talking about selfishness or self-esteem.

It is speaking about the natural way we care for our own lives.

Most people do not intentionally do things that are dangerous or destructive to themselves because they value their own lives.

Paul says that the husband should value his wife in the same way.

The way you care for your own body should be the way you care for your wife. You should not intentionally harm yourself.

You should not intentionally harm your wife.

You should protect her, care for her, and value her.

5. Husbands Are Called to Nourish and Cherish Their Wives

Verse 29

“For no one ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just as Christ does the church.”

No matter who you are, you are going to feed yourself.

A man has to eat.

Paul says that just as we nourish our own bodies, husbands are to nourish their wives. The word nourish means to bring someone to maturity.

It means to nurture, raise, and provide for someone.

We nourish our bodies, sometimes too much.

But we must nourish our wives in every area of life.

A husband should provide nourishment:

● Spiritually
● Mentally
● Physically
● Emotionally
● Sexually

The word cherish originally carried the idea of warming or keeping warm with body heat. It came to describe tender love and care.

A husband is supposed to create a place where his wife feels secure and warm through the way he acts and through the way he provides.

How often do we stop and think about who we are cherishing while we are so busy with everyday life?

We need to slow down and focus on the people we cherish.

Paul teaches that just as Jesus cherishes the church, husbands are to cherish their wives. Christ supplies the needs of His body, the church.

Husbands are called to do the same for their wives.

This does not mean providing every want.

It means providing for every need.

The husband is the provider, protector, and preserver.

Husbands miss the mark when they view their wives as objects:

● A cook
● A babysitter
● A house cleaner
● A sexual partner

She is God's gift and is to be continually cherished and nourished.

6. Husbands and Wives Are One Flesh

Verse 30

“Because we are members of his body.”

Paul is building the comparison between Christ and the church.

Christ and His church are united.

The union between Christ and His people is mysterious.

It can be illustrated, but it cannot be fully explained.

Paul compares this mysterious union to the relationship between a husband and wife. Marriage is a union of two people becoming one flesh.

This union includes:

● Body and spirit
● Mind and thoughts
● Objective and purpose
● Behavior and activity

The husband is to care for his wife as he cares for himself.

7. Marriage Requires Leaving and Cleaving

Verse 31

“Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and hold fast to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.”

Genesis 2:24 says:

“Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.”

The old expression is leaving and cleaving.

A man leaves his father and mother and holds fast to his wife.

The word carries the idea of holding tightly to something or being joined together. Marriage is the joining of two people into one.

Two people could never become closer.

This is complete union and oneness:

● Of body and spirit
● Of mind and thoughts
● Of objective and purpose
● Of behavior and activity

The husband is to care for his wife as his own body.

John Piper writes:

“In other words, the covenant involved in leaving mother and father and holding fast to a spouse and becoming one flesh is a portrayal of the covenant between Christ and his church. Marriage exists most ultimately to display the covenant-keeping love between Christ and his church.”

Marriage is ultimately a picture of the gospel.

8. Marriage Is a Mystery That Points to Christ

Verse 32

“This mystery is profound, and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church.” Constable writes:
“The relationship that exists between a husband and his wife is the same as the one that exists between Christ and His church. The church has as close a tie to Christ spiritually as a wife has to her husband spiritually.”

Why is marriage called a mystery?

It is not simply about living happily ever after.

Marriage is ultimately pointing toward something greater.

It points back to the Lord.

Marriage exists to display the relationship between Christ and His church. The church submits to Christ.

Christ loves the church.

Christ sacrifices Himself for the church.

Christ nourishes and cherishes the church.

Christ sanctifies the church.

Christ is preparing the church for glory.

And marriage is meant to be a picture of that relationship.

Verse 33 — The Final Application

“However, let each one of you love his wife as himself, and let the wife see that she respects her husband.”

The husband is called to love his wife as himself.

The wife is called to respect her husband.

The entire passage comes together here.

The wife submits.

The husband loves.

The wife respects.

The husband sacrifices.

The wife follows.

The husband leads.

And both are called to submit themselves to Christ.

Main Point

The Lord allows us to marry our spouses so that our marriages can represent the love Christ has for the church and the submission and respect the church has for the Lord.

The husband is called to love his wife as Christ loved the church.

That means:

● Sacrificing for her
● Leading her toward Christ
● Nourishing her
● Cherishing her
● Protecting her
● Providing for her
● Caring for her
● Helping her grow in holiness

The wife is called to respect and submit to her husband as the church submits to Christ.

Marriage is not ultimately about the husband getting his way.

Marriage is not ultimately about the wife getting her way.

Marriage is about Christ being glorified.

So the question is:

Would you submit to yourself?

If the answer is no, then husbands must ask whether they are truly leading their wives in a way that is worthy of submission.

And wives must ask whether they are willing to respect and submit to a husband who is trying to lead under the authority of Christ.

The goal of Christian marriage is not for the husband and wife to become more like each other. The goal is for both of them to become more like Christ.

And when a husband loves his wife like Christ loves the church, and a wife submits to her husband as the church submits to Christ, marriage becomes a living picture of the gospel.
