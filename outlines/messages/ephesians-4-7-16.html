Ephesians 4:7–16
A Gifted Walk

Introduction

In Ephesians 4:1–6, Paul emphasized the unity of the body of Christ. There is:

● One body.
● One Spirit.
● One hope.
● One Lord.
● One faith.
● One baptism.
● One God and Father.

But beginning in verse 7, Paul introduces an important truth:

We are united, but we are not identical.

There is unity in the body, but there is also diversity within the body. We are one body with many members.

We are one church with different gifts.

1. Grace Given to Each Believer

Ephesians 4:7

“But grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ's gift.” The first important word is “but.”

Paul has spent the entire letter emphasizing unity.

The mystery of God's plan was revealed in Christ.

Jews and Gentiles have been united through Christ.

We are one body.

We have one Spirit.

We are to seek the unity of Christ.

But grace was given to each one of us.

Every believer is different.

Every believer has been gifted differently.

The spiritual gifts given to believers are not merely natural talents. They are gifts given by God for the purpose of accomplishing His will. This grace is different from the grace mentioned in Ephesians 2:8. Ephesians 2:8 focuses on the grace by which we are saved.

Ephesians 4:7 focuses on grace given to believers for service.

God has given each believer grace for ministry.

This means that every believer has value in the body of Christ.

No spiritual gift is unnecessary.

No believer is useless.

God has given you something to be used for His purpose.

2. Christ Gave Gifts to Men

Ephesians 4:8–10

Paul writes:

“When he ascended on high he led a host of captives, and he gave gifts to men.”

This is a quotation from Psalm 68:18.

Psalm 68 is a victory hymn celebrating God's triumph.

The picture is of a victorious king ascending after conquering his enemies and distributing the spoils of victory.

Paul applies this picture to Jesus.

Jesus descended.

Jesus conquered.

Jesus ascended.

And Jesus gave gifts to His people.

What Does “He Descended” Mean?

There has been debate over what Paul means by the lower regions of the earth. Some believe it refers to Christ descending into the grave or Hades.

Others understand it as referring to the incarnation—Jesus leaving heaven and coming to earth. The passage may simply be emphasizing the contrast:

He descended to earth, and He ascended far above the heavens.

Either way, the main point is clear:

Jesus descended.

Jesus rose.

Jesus ascended.

And after His ascension, He gave gifts to His church.

Through the Holy Spirit, Christ equipped His people for ministry.

The gifts of the Spirit are ultimately gifts from the victorious, resurrected, and ascended Christ.

3. Christ Bestowed Spiritual Gifts

Ephesians 4:11–12

“And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ.”

These gifts were given by Christ.

No one earned them.

No one can boast in them.

They were given by grace.

A. Apostles

The word refers to a special ambassador or one who is sent.

The apostles were foundational to the establishment of the church.

They were witnesses of Christ and were commissioned to teach His message. The apostles helped lay the foundation of the church.

There are no new foundational apostles today in the same sense as the original apostles. The apostles taught:

● Jesus.
● Faith.
● Grace.
● The gospel.
● The future hope of Christ's return.

B. Prophets

A prophet is not primarily someone who predicts the future.

A prophet is a forth-teller before he is a fore-teller.

A prophet is:

● A truth-sayer.
● A herald.
● A proclaimer.
● A testifier.

The prophet speaks what God has revealed.

The purpose of prophecy is not entertainment or speculation.

It is to proclaim the truth of God.

Jesus wants His church to hear from God and proclaim what He has said to others.

C. Evangelists

Evangelists are the proclaimers of the good news.

They are gifted to spread the gospel.

The word evangelist means someone who announces good news.

The gospel is the good news of Jesus Christ:

● We are sinners.
● We are separated from God.
● Jesus died for our sins.
● Jesus rose from the grave.
● Salvation is by grace through faith.
● We can be reconciled to God through Christ.

Evangelism can happen in many different ways.

It can happen:

● On the street.
● One-on-one.
● Through writing.
● Through preaching.
● Through personal relationships.

The same gift can operate through different methods.

D. Shepherds and Teachers

A pastor is a shepherd.

A shepherd leads, protects, feeds, and cares for the sheep.

A teacher explains the Word of God and helps believers understand truth. Preaching is teaching.

But teaching is not always preaching.

The pastor-teacher is called to lead the people of God and feed them the Word. A leader must live a life that supports what he teaches.

The church is intended to be:

A truth-telling, Word-disseminating, spiritually feeding fellowship. The roles of these gifts may overlap.

Paul himself is an excellent example.

He was an apostle.

He taught.

He evangelized.

He preached.

He planted churches.

He proclaimed God's truth.

4. The Purpose of the Gifts Ephesians 4:12

The spiritual gifts were given:

“To equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ.” This is extremely important.

The purpose of church leadership is not to do all the ministry themselves. The purpose is to equip the saints to do ministry.

Every believer is called to serve.

Every believer is a minister.

Every believer is a servant.

Every believer has a role in the body of Christ.

Every believer has received grace for ministry.

Therefore, spiritual gifts must be:

● Identified.
● Developed.
● Practiced.
● Used for God's glory.

Leadership must grow out of discipleship.

Equipping the Saints

The word equipping carries the idea of:

● Perfecting.
● Fully furnishing.
● Outfitting.
● Preparing.

The Greek word is katartismos.

It carries the idea of making something complete and useful.

The word can be associated with a skilled craftsman.

God does not save people merely to sit in a church building.

He saves people and equips them to serve.

The church should be continually preparing believers to:

● Understand the Word.
● Live by the Word.
● Minister with the Word.
● Lead the lost to Christ.
● Make disciples.
● Multiply the kingdom.

Building Up the Body of Christ

The church needs equipped believers.

Too many churches have a small number of people doing all the work while everyone else remains unequipped.

Pastors should not be expected to solve every problem.

The pastor's responsibility is to devote himself to prayer and the ministry of the Word while equipping the saints for ministry.

We see an example of this in Acts 6.

A need arose in the church.

The apostles said they should not abandon the preaching of the Word to wait on tables. Instead, qualified men were selected to serve.

This allowed the apostles to focus on prayer and the ministry of the Word while other believers served in other necessary areas.

This is the body of Christ functioning properly.

Different people have different responsibilities.

Everyone serves.

Everyone contributes.

Everyone has a role.

5. Growth in the Body of Christ

Ephesians 4:13–16

The purpose of equipping the saints is spiritual maturity.

Paul says that we are to continue growing:

“Until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of God's Son, growing into maturity with a stature measured by Christ's fullness.”

The goal is Christ.

We are not trying to become like another Christian.

We are trying to become like Jesus.

Spiritual Maturity

A Christian must grow.

We are either growing or dying.

There is no neutral position.

We begin the Christian life as spiritual children.

But children are supposed to grow.

We are to grow through:

● The Word.
● The Spirit.
● Prayer.
● Discipleship.
● Obedience.
● Service.

Eventually, we are supposed to become disciple-makers ourselves.

We should be discipled until we are mature enough to disciple others.

Knowing Your Place in the Body

One of the purposes of spiritual maturity is understanding where you belong in the body of Christ.

Paul uses the illustration of a body.

A body has different parts.

A foot is not a hand.

An eye is not an ear.

Every part has a different purpose.

Imagine a foot trying to perform the job of a hand.

It would be extremely ineffective.

The same is true in the church.

God has gifted every believer differently.

If you are called to be a foot, be the best foot you can be.

If you are called to be a hand, be the best hand you can be.

The body needs every part functioning in the way God designed it.

You do not need to become someone else.

You need to become who God created you to be.

Truth Protects Us from Deception Paul says that spiritual maturity protects us from being:

“Tossed by the waves and blown around by every wind of teaching.” Children are easily deceived.

They are gullible because they lack maturity and understanding. The same is true spiritually.

Immature Christians can be easily influenced by:

● False teaching.
● Clever arguments.
● Manipulation.
● Deception.
● Human wisdom.

The only way to defeat deception is with truth.

That is why discipleship is so important.

You must be discipled by people who know the truth.

But even truth can be presented incorrectly.

You can teach the truth pridefully.

You can teach the truth hatefully.

You can teach the truth arrogantly.

But the Bible says we must speak the truth in love.

Truth without love can become cruelty.

Love without truth can become compromise.

The Christian must have both.

Truth in love.

6. The Body Must Grow Together Ephesians 4:15–16

Christ is the Head.

The entire body receives direction from Him.

The body is:

● Joined together.
● Held together.
● Supported by every part.
● Growing through the proper work of each individual member. This gives us three important truths.

1. The Body of Christ Must Function Harmoniously Every part must work together.

The church is not a collection of individuals doing their own thing. We are a body.

The body functions when the parts cooperate.

2. The Body of Christ Must Function Helpfully Every part contributes to the growth of the body.

The church grows when believers serve one another.

Every believer has something to contribute.

3. The Body of Christ Must Function Humbly The body is built up in love.

No individual member is the entire body.

No gift is more valuable because it is more visible.

We all need one another.

The power to produce mature believers does not come from human effort alone. The power comes from Christ, the Head of the church.

Healthy biblical church growth occurs when:

● Every believer uses his spiritual gift.
● Every believer submits to the Holy Spirit.
● Every believer cooperates with other believers.
● Every believer serves the body in love.

Main Point

The primary purpose of the Christian life is not merely to be saved and sit still. Christ saves us, equips us, matures us, and sends us.

The purpose of the church is to:

1. Disciple believers.

2. Mature believers.

3. Equip believers.

4. Send believers into ministry.

5. Make more disciples who do the same.

We are a gifted body.

We are different in our gifts.

But we are united in Christ.

The church is healthiest when every member understands his role and uses his gift. The goal is not to draw attention to ourselves.

The goal is to build up the body.

The goal is not our glory.

The goal is Christ.

We are equipped to serve, matured to grow, and sent to multiply the gospel until the entire body grows into the fullness of Jesus Christ.
