Ephesians 5:1–14
Imitators

Introduction

There was a father who was loading up in his truck one day with his son. He got him buckled in and ready to go. They drove through town and got hungry and so he stopped at Mcdonalds. He grabbed his son a happy meal and kept on driving.

As he was driving a green traffic light turned straight to red, the father slammed on his brakes and mumbled under his breath. The son's fries went flying and his orange drink covered his lap. Then his 4 year old said a 4 letter word . He said son , Where did you learn to talk like that?

Then Rodney Adkins' son said , I've been watching Dad ain't that cool. I'm your buckaroo, I wanna be like you and eat all my food and grow as strong as you are.

Children imitate what they see.

The question for us is:

Who are we imitating?

1. Believers Live to Imitate Christ

Ephesians 5:1–2

“Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children. And walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us…”

Paul begins with the word “therefore.”

This connects us to everything he has already taught.

Ephesians 4:32 says:

“Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you.”

This is what it means to imitate Christ.

We forgive because Christ forgave us.

We love because Christ loved us.

We show mercy because Christ showed mercy to us.

1 Corinthians 11:1 says:

“Be imitators of me, as I am of Christ.”

Paul is not telling people to imitate him as if he is perfect.

He is saying:

Imitate me because I am imitating Christ.

The Connection to Being Born Again

There is an important analogy throughout the Christian life.

We are born again.

We are born as spiritual infants.

Then we grow.

Jesus said:

“Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.”

— John 3:3

You must be born again into the family of God.

You are made new.

You are made holy.

You are made righteous through Christ.

Then, as a child of God, you are called to grow.

Jesus said:

“Unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.”

— Matthew 18:3

The Christian is called to have a childlike faith.

Childlike Faith

A child trusts.

A child may not understand everything, but he trusts the person who does. We trust the Lord even though we cannot see Him.

We trust His love even though we cannot physically see Him.

Childlike Innocence

This does not mean Christians are sinless.

It means our minds should not be controlled by the corruption and darkness of the world. We should focus on the truth of God.

Childlike Obedience

Children who love and trust their parents desire to please them.

In the same way, our love for God should produce obedience.

We obey God because we love Him.

Walk in Love

Paul says:

“Walk in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us.”

This is how we walk like Christ.

Christ gave Himself for us.

He did not merely speak about love.

He demonstrated love through sacrifice.

The cross was the greatest act of love in history.

One writer described Christ's sacrifice as:

“A love so measureless, so reckless of cost, for those who were naturally so unworthy of it…”

Think about the person you refuse to forgive.

You may say:

“They do not deserve forgiveness because of what they did.” But did you deserve the forgiveness Christ gave you?

And yet He gave it anyway.

Christ's love was a fragrant offering.

It was:

● Sweet.
● Pleasing.
● Sufficient.
● Accepted by God.

The sacrifice of Christ was a pleasing aroma before the Father. And now, as followers of Christ, our lives should also be a pleasing offering to God.

2. Believers Should Not Live in Darkness

Ephesians 5:3–7

Paul begins with a contrast:

Walk in love.

But do not walk in darkness.

The Christian life is not only about what we do.

It is also about what we refuse to participate in.

Sexual Immorality

Paul begins with sexual sin.

This includes any sexual activity outside God's design for marriage.

The world often says that sexual sin promotes love.

But Scripture teaches the opposite.

The world says:

“This will bring you closer together.”

But God's Word teaches that sexual sin damages the proper expression of love. Sexual immorality does not create genuine love.

It often uses people for selfish desire.

Impurity

Impurity is broader.

It includes the things we allow into our minds and hearts.

This can include:

● Pornography.
● Impure movies.
● Impure television.
● Impure books.
● Impure music.
● Impure social media.
● Impure conversations.

The question is not only:

“What am I doing?”

The question is also:

“What am I allowing to influence me?”

Covetousness

The Greek word pleonexia carries the idea of greed and wanting more. It is the desire to possess what you do not have.

Covetousness is not limited to money.

You can covet:

● Possessions.
● Relationships.
● Status.
● Attention.
● Power.
● Other people's lives.

It is the constant desire for more.

Paul places covetousness alongside sexual immorality because both are rooted in selfish desire.

Both say:

“I want what I want.”

“Not Even Be Named Among You”

Paul uses strong language.

These things should not characterize God's people.

This does not mean Christians will never struggle with sin.

It means sin should not become the identity of the believer. A Christian may fall into sin.

But a Christian should not make peace with sin.

There is a difference between:

Falling into sin

and

Living comfortably in sin.

Foolish Talk and Crude Joking

Paul also addresses our speech.

He mentions:

● Filthiness.
● Foolish talk.
● Crude joking.

This is a matter of the heart.

Jesus said:

“Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.”

What comes out of your mouth reveals something about what is inside of you. Crude speech can be:

● Evil.
● Hateful.
● Hurtful.
● Sexual.
● Disrespectful.

The question is:

Does my speech reflect Christ?

Do Not Be Deceived

Ephesians 5:6

Paul warns that empty words can deceive.

People can use clever arguments to make sin sound acceptable. They can call evil good.

They can call darkness light.

Isaiah 5:20 says:

“Woe to those who call evil good and good evil…”

We must be careful.

The world will try to redefine sin.

It will tell us:

● Sin is freedom.
● Purity is oppression.
● Holiness is judgmental.
● Truth is hateful.
● Darkness is light.

But God's Word does not change.

How Does Deception Work?

Deception often begins with trust.

You are not normally deceived by someone you completely distrust. You are deceived by someone who has your ear.

Someone who has your time.

Someone whose words you accept.

Deception means being led astray.

You are walking in one direction.

Then someone speaks into your ear.

You trust what they say.

And slowly, you begin turning in another direction.

That is how deception works.

You must be careful who influences you.

Do Not Be Partners with Darkness

Ephesians 5:7

Paul says not to become partners with the sons of disobedience. This does not mean Christians must completely abandon unbelievers. Jesus ate with sinners.

Jesus spoke with sinners.

Jesus served sinners.

Jesus washed the feet of sinners.

We are not called to isolate ourselves from unbelievers.

We are called to avoid participating in their sin.

There is a difference between:

Being around darkness

and

Becoming part of the darkness.

We work with unbelievers.

We may live with unbelievers.

We may have unbelieving family members.

But we should not participate in the sins that separate them from God.

3. Believers Are to Expose Darkness with Light

Ephesians 5:8–14

“For at one time you were darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Walk as children of light.”

Notice what Paul says.

He does not merely say:

“You were in darkness.”

He says:

“You were darkness.”

But now:

“You are light in the Lord.”

You have been transformed.

You Are Not Who You Used to Be

The Christian life is not about pretending to be new.

The Christian life is about actually being made new.

One famous statement says:

“The grace that does not change my life will not save my soul.”

If you have been made new by the Spirit of God, you cannot remain comfortable in darkness forever.

A true believer may fall into sin.

A true believer may struggle.

A true believer may wander.

But the Holy Spirit will not allow them to remain at peace with sin forever. Something inside of them will begin to grieve.

The believer must evaluate the grace they claim if they are completely comfortable living in habitual, unrepentant sin.

Be Who You Are

You have been made new.

So live new.

You are no longer who you were.

Do not continue living according to the identity that Christ killed.

You were once darkness.

Now you are light in the Lord.

Walk like it.

Live to Please the Lord

We do not determine what pleases God based on:

● Our feelings.
● The world's opinions.
● The opinions of other Christians.
● Our own conscience.

Our conscience can be wrongly informed.

We learn what pleases the Lord through His Word.

The believer's question should be:

“Does this please the Lord who loved me and gave Himself for me?” That question should guide:

● What we watch.
● What we listen to.
● What we say.
● What we do.
● Where we go.
● Who influences us.

Expose the Works of Darkness

Ephesians 5:11

Paul says:

“Take no part in the unfruitful works of darkness, but instead expose them.” Notice the difference between verse 7 and verse 11.

Verse 7: Identity

Do not be partners with darkness.

Do not become part of it.

Verse 11: Action

Expose darkness.

The Christian is not called merely to avoid darkness.

The Christian is called to shine light into darkness.

But remember:

Paul says to expose unfruitful works of darkness, not unfruitful people. People are not our enemies.

People are blinded.

We were once in darkness too.

We are not better than them.

We are saved by grace.

Therefore, we should approach unbelievers with humility. We are called to:

● Live among them.
● Love them.
● Serve them.
● Speak truth to them.
● Guide them toward Christ.

But we must not participate in their darkness.

You Will Imitate Something Everyone is imitating something.

The question is:

What are you imitating?

You will imitate:

● Christ.
● The world.
● Your friends.
● Your culture.
● Your desires.
● Your influencers.
● Your family.

But you will imitate something.

Paul's command is clear:

Be imitators of God as beloved children.

Main Point

Paul is calling believers to live as people who have been transformed. We were once darkness.

Now we are light in the Lord.

Therefore:

● Imitate Christ.
● Walk in love.
● Reject the darkness.
● Do not be deceived.
● Do not participate in sin.
● Expose darkness with the light of Christ.
● Live to please the Lord.

The question is not whether you will imitate something. The question is:

Who are you imitating?

The world or Christ?

Darkness or light?

Your desires or God's Word?

You are going to be an imitator of something. Choose Christ.
