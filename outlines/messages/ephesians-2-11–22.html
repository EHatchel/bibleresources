Ephesians 2:11–22
A Worldwide Segregation

Ayden Williams

Introduction

What day of the week do you think is the most segregated?

For many people, the answer would be Sunday.

People often separate themselves based on race, culture, background, social status, political beliefs, income, and countless other differences.

But Ephesians 2 shows us that Jesus Christ came to destroy the greatest division of all: the division between Jews and Gentiles.

1. The Two Who Were Divided

Ephesians 2:11–12

“Therefore remember that at one time you Gentiles in the flesh… were at that time separated from Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world.”

Paul begins by telling the Gentiles to remember who they once were.

The Gentiles were called “the uncircumcision” by the Jews.

Circumcision was a sign of being part of God's covenant people. The Jews were circumcised on the eighth day, and anyone outside of that covenant was considered unclean and separated.

The Gentiles were:

● Separated from Christ.
● Alienated from the commonwealth of Israel.
● Strangers to the covenants of promise.
● Without hope.
● Without God in the world.

Do You Feel Welcomed?

The Jews were God's chosen people.

They were chosen for a specific lineage and a specific purpose: to be the people through whom God would reveal Himself and bring His promises into the world.

They were meant to be the face of God and at the forefront of God's mission. But instead of fulfilling the mission of God, many became prideful and arrogant. They forgot that being chosen was not about being better than everyone else. They began to use their identity as a wall.

If you were not Jewish, you were simply a Gentile.

There was no in-between.

The people who were supposed to lead the nations to God became a barrier keeping people away from God.

Religion became a blockade for the Gentiles.

This is why relationship with God is more important than religion.

Religion, when corrupted, can become its own blockade between a person and God. Being separated from God brings a sense of hopelessness.

Have you ever had a time in your life when you felt like you had no hope? What was the cure?

2. The “But Now”

Ephesians 2:13

“But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.”

Paul says:

But now.

The Gentiles were once far away.

But now they have been brought near.

Christ brings us near to God.

Jesus Christ unites people to Himself.

The people who were once separated are now brought as near to God as Christ Himself. They are no longer outsiders.

They are no longer strangers.

They are no longer far away.

They have been brought near through the blood of Jesus Christ.

The world may reject you.

But in Christ, you are accepted by God.

Rejected on Earth, Accepted in Heaven

Remember:

● Jesus was rejected by people but chosen by God and precious to Him. ● Jesus warned that His followers would be rejected, hated, and opposed for His sake. ● Satan wants to discourage us and tempt us to give up.
● We must submit ourselves to God and resist the devil.
● God's grace is sufficient for us, and His power is made perfect in weakness. ● God may allow us to experience rejection so that we learn not to rely on ourselves but on Him.
● The Lord will never reject His followers, forsake us, or condemn us.
● Nothing can separate us from the love of God.
● Even when everything goes wrong on earth, we can rejoice because our names are written in heaven.
● Because God has accepted us freely and graciously, we should accept others as Christ accepted us.

The only reason these things are possible is because of what Jesus did.

3. The Two Made One

Ephesians 2:14–18

“For he himself is our peace, who has made us both one and has broken down in his flesh the dividing wall of hostility…”

There was a physical wall in the temple separating Gentiles from the Jews. An inscription warned:

“No foreigner may enter within the barricade which surrounds the sanctuary and enclosure. Anyone who is caught doing so will have himself to blame for his ensuing death.”

The message was clear:

You cannot come any further.

You are not one of us.

You are separated.

You are outside.

But then something happened that changed everything.

The cross.

The cross called for remembrance.

The cross called for salvation.

The cross called for forgiveness and grace.

And the cross destroyed the division between two groups of people.

“That he might create in himself one new man in place of the two, so making peace.”

The two groups—Jew and Gentile—were no longer two separate groups. They became one people of God through Jesus Christ.

Humanity has always focused on what makes us different.

Then we use those differences to decide who we want to be around and who we want to avoid. But Jesus did not come to create multiple groups of people.

He came to create one people in Himself.

The Church Should Not Become Another Wall

While writing this message, I began thinking about the modern church.

The ancient temple had walls and signs that communicated:

You cannot come any further.

Then I think about the modern church.

Sometimes we create walls and signs that communicate:

You are not like us.

You do not belong here.

You need to clean yourself up before you come in.

What is the difference?

Jesus did not tell people to clean themselves up before coming to Him.

People came to Jesus with:

● Anger.
● Lust.
● Addiction.
● Hatred.
● Racism.
● Pride.
● Brokenness.
● Sin.

And Jesus led them to salvation.

The Shower Illustration

When someone wants to become better, we often use this example: Do you clean yourself before you get in the shower?

Or do you get in the shower to get clean?

You get in the shower because you are dirty.

The same is true spiritually.

People do not clean themselves up before coming to Christ.

They come to Christ because they need to be cleansed.

We should not focus first on cleaning up someone's sins.

We should focus first on whether they have experienced salvation.

Sanctification comes after salvation.

We should guide people to Jesus.

We should not create a standard that says:

“Fix yourself first, then come to Christ.”

Because none of us could have come to Christ if we had to fix ourselves first.

4. Members of the Household of God

Ephesians 2:19–22

“So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God…”

Paul summarizes what has happened.

The Gentiles were once:

● Far away.
● Strangers.
● Aliens.
● Without hope.
● Without God.

But now they are:

● Fellow citizens.
● Saints.
● Members of God's household.
● A dwelling place for God.

Have you ever been at home and felt like a foreigner?

Have you ever been around people you were close to but still felt like a stranger? Ephesians tells us that in Christ, you are no longer a stranger.

You belong to the household of God.

Hebrews 2:11 says:

“For the one who sanctifies and those who are sanctified all have one Father. That is why Jesus is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters.”

Jesus is not ashamed to call us family.

The Man Outside the Temple

Imagine a man standing outside the temple.

He looks at the temple and asks an Israelite:

“Can I go in there?”

The Israelite responds:

“Sure. Any Israelite man can go in there.”

The man says:

“Well, I am not an Israelite.”
“Then you cannot go in.”
“What would I have to do to get in?”
“You would have to be born an Israelite.”

The man thinks:

“I wish I had been born an Israelite.”

Then he sees a priest carrying sacrifices.

The priest washes his hands, offers sacrifices, burns incense, and enters the Holy Place. The man asks:

“What is in that room?”

The Israelite explains that the Holy Place is only for priests.

The man says:

“I wish I had been born into the tribe of Levi and the family of Aaron.”

Then he sees the veil separating the Holy Place from the Holy of Holies. He asks what is behind it.

He is told that the Holy of Holies is where the presence of God dwells and that only the high priest can enter.

But even the high priest could only enter once a year.

The man walks away thinking:

“I will never be able to enter the presence of God.”

He has no hope.

He is separated.

He is outside.

But then Jesus died on the cross.

And the veil of the temple was torn in two.

God's invitation to His presence was opened.

The bridge was made.

The separation was destroyed.

You are no longer far away.

You are no longer a stranger.

You are no longer an outsider.

You are now a member of the household of God.

Main Point

Jesus Is For All.

The cross destroyed the dividing wall.

The cross brought the far-off near.

The cross made two groups into one.

The cross opened the way into God's presence.

The cross made strangers into family.

And the cross reminds us that the church should never become a wall keeping people away from Jesus.

Jesus is for all.

There is no race, culture, background, sin, or difference that can keep someone from the grace of Jesus Christ when they come to Him in faith.
