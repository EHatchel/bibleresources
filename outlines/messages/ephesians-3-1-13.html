Ephesians 1:1-7
Inheritance of the Believer

Introduction/Greetings (vv. 1-2)

Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ
Written to the saints / believers

Point 1: God's Position for the Believer (vv. 3-6)

Blessed Be — We Are Already Blessed
We are already blessed. He has blessed us. Believers are already blessed immediately, from the moment of salvation. We are always blessed. There shouldn’t be a hope of blessing like you are without.

The statement from Paul points out that the believer already has blessings. A lead-up to the rest of the letter of our blessing provided by Christ.

He says “us,” Jews and Gentiles of the whole church of Ephesus. Not excluding any believer.
With this pointed out, I want to give you a thought from Spurgeon:

“We are not sitting here and groaning and crying and fretting, and worrying and questioning our own salvation. He has blessed us and therefore we will bless him. If you think little of what God has done for you you will do very little for him, but if you have a great idea of his great mercy to you, you will be greatly grateful to your gracious God.”

Your insurance from God should bring about a great confidence. Do you remember a few weeks ago at Lincoln’s house when we talked about confidence in the Lord? Where do you think confidence comes from? What makes you confident about something? Perhaps you’re very knowledgeable about a certain topic, from either experience or head knowledge and learning. Or a promise you’ve been given from a trustworthy source. Or a trial or mistake that you made it through and learned and grew from. That can make you pretty confident. Or finally, you know the absolute truth about it.

Doesn’t God provide you with all these things?

You’ve experienced God. You’ve learned and become knowledgeable about His Word. You’ve been given promises and blessings from Him who is very trustworthy, who has never led you wrong or lied about a promise, who knows what’s best for you. When you have been put through a trial or a difficult time, He comforted you and guided you. You grew from that experience. And because of these things, you know that this is the absolute truth of all things. This should give you some confidence in life and death.

Every Spiritual Blessing in the Heavenly Places

Paul continues with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places. Every spiritual blessing comes from Jesus Christ. And the blessings granted are heavenly. This is important because they aren’t earthly blessings as part of the inheritance.

Colossians 3:2 (NKJV) -

“Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth.”

Set your mind on things above. Speaks of a mindset of a believer to focus on your Father, focus on your home, and focus on your inheritance. Rather than blessing your earthly life with things that will pass away, God gifts you blessings that are eternal. Are you experiencing every spiritual blessing that God has for you?

Chosen in Him Before the Foundation of the World — (v. 4)

Genesis 1:26–29 (NKJV)

Then God said, “Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness; let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over the cattle, over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.” So God created man in His image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them. Then God blessed them, and God said to them, “Be fruitful and multiply; fill the earth and subdue it; have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over every living thing that moves on the earth.” And God said, “See, I have given you every herb that yields seed which is on the face of all the earth, and every tree whose fruit yields seed; to you it shall be for food.”

We were and are God’s chosen creature, to have dominion over the earth and all the things that live there. And chosen to be made in His image and likeness. He chose us in Him. We did not do anything nor deserve anything.
That’s where grace comes from. Not deserving but given. It is in the person of Jesus Christ and the work of Him on the earth and cross that we have this grace. He chose us and commands us to be holy and blameless. This points out the notation that He did not choose us likewise. Meaning to be unholy or blameful. He did not choose us to be lost.

The BBC states:

“When Paul describes the elect, he speaks of them as ‘vessels of mercy which he had prepared beforehand for glory’ (Romans 9:23); but when he turns to the lost, he simply says, ‘vessels of wrath prepared for destruction’ (Romans 9:22). God prepares vessels of mercy for glory, but He does not prepare men for destruction: they do this for themselves by their own unbelief.”

This is called the doctrine of election. God is sovereign and He reigns over all. With this doctrine, the Bible also teaches the responsibilities of human-kind. God all throughout Scripture offers salvation to all people. Most famously, John 3:16.

So this takes away the mindset of only following the doctrine of, “Well, if He didn’t choose me then I’m eternally damned,” or thinking, “I’m chosen and other people are eternally damned,” because He offers salvation to all who believe in His Son.

Holy — Meaning set apart.
Blameless — Meaning free from sin.
A.K.A. perfect. This is a gospel verse.

Think about how it was written. You can’t be holy and blameless by yourself. He adds in Him. Him — Jesus Christ — the only way to be holy and blameless in the eyes of God.
We pray that our faith in Jesus will make us righteous in the eyes of God. This is the gospel message Paul is writing here.

Predestination — (v. 5)

Predestination as used here is to appoint adoption beforehand.
When we talk about sovereignty, God could have done anything He wanted. He could have done away with human-kind in the flood. There could have been no redemption. Or even before then, we could have just been another creature that roams the earth only to reproduce and multiply.

But instead, in His sovereignty, He chose us before the foundations of the world to be His image and likeness. To have a soul and a mind of God. With common sense and logic. Through our words and actions showing God’s glory and goodness.
We are His image on earth. Which is why we look to Jesus and follow His example.

His Glorious Grace — (v. 6)

All of this is possible because of verse 6. His glorious grace poured out on us. Poured, not measured.
There’s not a certain amount of grace when it comes to salvation of Jesus. Imagine just for a moment God put a measure to grace. “Unfortunately, you have gone over the measure of grace, so there is no longer salvation for you.” This is partially why work-based salvation doesn’t make sense.
You work and fail and work and fail. There is no amount of work you can do for salvation. So God has poured out an endless amount of grace and made a bridge to Him in Jesus Christ.

Point 2: God’s Plan for the Believer — (v. 7)

We know now from the first point that our position is in Christ. Now we learn from Paul that His plan for us is to purchase our freedom.

We have been captivated by sin. We are slaves to it. God sent His Son to pay for man’s freedom. We can’t free ourselves, but we need freedom, so God made a blood sacrifice of a perfect lamb.

Predestination determines that God knew we would sin. This raises questions like, “Why then were we created?” So brings of the point again of God choosing to have a plan and purpose ahead. There was a plan from the beginning. And that plan was diverted because of us. The Bible makes it clear that we have totally and arrogantly screwed up that plan and purpose.

Think about the present circumstances for a moment. How many of the people around you, the people you spend your time with, will be confined to hell for eternity? And if they don’t find forgiveness with the Lord and a faith in Jesus, their eternal reality will come to pass sooner than their next breath.

This highlights the importance of the Lord’s Prayer where it states, “Forgive us of our trespasses.” With that in mind, it is no longer the meaningless prayer we think of often, but a crucial role in our lives now. With your life being a mist. With your family and friends’ lives being a morning fog.

Your action tomorrow could be someone’s eternal damnation today.

Act now.

Forgiven of All Our Sins

Forgiven of all our sins over and over is the reminder of how much grace God has

Me and Thomas play ping pong every day for lunch at work. A common courtesy in ping pong is if you hit the net and win the point, you’re supposed to say sorry. Tuesday morning, Thomas hit the net so many times and I heard “sorry” so much that I was annoyed and didn’t want to hear sorry anymore.

I immediately responded with, “How does God have so much grace?” It was a small reminder of how much we tell God sorry and ask for forgiveness, and God every time forgives.

This is why Paul gives the perfect wording for God’s grace:

The riches of His grace.

Main Point:

God predestined human-kind to be His image and His glory. Uniting all to Him through Jesus Christ, promising to show His glory, and to attain eternity with God.

Application:

In Christ:
1. Be Holy
2. Be Blameless
3. Be Confident

Ephesians 3:1–13

August 5, 2026

The Mystery Made Known

Greek Phrase: ho mystērion gnōrizō

"To make known the mystery."

1. The Mystery Is Made Known

Ephesians 3:1–6

"For this reason I, Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles..." Paul begins by calling himself:

A prisoner of Jesus Christ.

Paul had many names:

  • Saul of Tarsus.
  • Paulus.
  • Brother Saul.
  • Saint Paul.
  • The chief of sinners.
  • The apostle to the Gentiles.

But when Paul describes himself, he brings every title down to nothing and gives everything to Christ.

Prisoner of Christ.

Bondservant of Christ.

Paul is essentially saying:

"I would rather be a prisoner of Jesus than a king of the world."

Paul's identity was no longer found in his heritage, accomplishments, status, or reputation. His identity was found in Christ.

What Is the Mystery?

Paul says that a mystery was made known to him.

To understand this mystery, we need to understand three things:

  • What was hidden?
  • What was revealed?
  • What does the mystery mean?

The mystery Paul is talking about is connected to what we studied in Ephesians 2. The Jews were God's chosen people.

But for much of the Old Testament, the full understanding that Gentiles would be saved by grace through faith had not been fully revealed.

The Gentiles were viewed as outsiders.

They were separated from the covenant.

They were separated from the people of God.

But God's plan was always bigger than one nation.

Rahab

Consider Rahab.

Rahab was a citizen of a wicked city under God's judgment.

She was part of a corrupt, pagan culture.

She had not been raised under the godly leadership of Moses or Joshua. But she had heard about the God of Israel.

She had heard about:

  • Israel's escape from Egypt.
  • The crossing of the Red Sea.
  • Their wandering in the wilderness.
  • Their victories over their enemies.

And she came to the correct conclusion:

"For the Lord your God is God in heaven above and on the earth beneath." — Joshua 2:11

Rahab's faith led her to action, and God saved her and her family.

Faith Was Always a Factor

We can also look at the New Testament.

In Luke 5, a paralyzed man was brought to Jesus by his friends.

The crowd was so large that they could not get through the door.

So they climbed onto the roof, opened it, and lowered the man down in front of Jesus. Luke says:

"And when he saw their faith, he said, 'Man, your sins are forgiven you.'" Faith was always a factor.

Old Testament.

New Testament.

God's grace was always present.

Remember Ephesians 2:4:

"But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us..." God's grace was not a new idea.

But the full mystery of God's plan was now being revealed.

When Was the Mystery Revealed to Paul? From Genesis 12 through Acts 8, the story primarily focuses on the Jewish people. God chose Israel and worked through them to reveal Himself to the world.

But then the mystery was revealed in Acts 9.

Jesus gave Ananias a message concerning Paul:

Go, for he is a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel.

— Acts 9:15

Paul was specifically chosen to carry the gospel to the Gentiles.

"To Make Known"

The word Paul uses for making known is connected to the Greek word ginōskō. It carries the idea of:

  • Learning to know.
  • Coming to know.
  • Gaining knowledge.
  • Perceiving.
  • Understanding.

God was making known something that had previously been hidden. And what was the mystery?

The Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel.

The Gentiles were now:

  • Fellow heirs.
  • Members of the same body.
  • Partakers of the promise.

The two groups had become one body.

This was unheard of.

It was like mixing:

  • The rich and the poor.
  • The sick and the healthy.
  • The clean and the unclean.
  • The Jews and the Gentiles.

The Gentiles had been outsiders.

But now they were equal in Christ.

They were invited to share in the glory of God's love and the promise of eternity.

2. The Mission of the Gospel

Ephesians 3:7–10

Paul says that he became a minister of the gospel according to the gift of God's grace. Service

Paul was once a traitor to the church.

He was called by God to turn from his ways, stand up, and live out the will of his Father in heaven.

Paul had once persecuted the people of God.

He was a Jew, born in Tarsus and educated under Gamaliel.

I am a Jew, born in Tarsus in Cilicia, but brought up in this city, educated at the feet of Gamaliel according to the strict manner of the law of our fathers, being zealous for God...

— Acts 22:3

Paul had every reason to boast in his heritage.

He was:

  • A Hebrew.
  • An Israelite.
  • A descendant of Abraham.
  • A Pharisee.
  • Highly educated.
  • A man with an impressive Jewish lineage.

He had status.

He had reputation.

He had heritage.

But he counted it all as rubbish compared to knowing Christ.

Everything that once made Paul important became meaningless compared to Christ. Paul's life became about one thing:

Being a messenger of Jesus Christ.

Grace

Paul understood grace.

God's grace is sufficient.

God's grace is so great that we cannot fully understand it.

We have only a microscopic understanding of the amount of mercy and grace God provides. Think about the grace God has shown you throughout your life.

Now think about the grace God gave you before time began.

Can you understand the depth of that?

Neither can I.

Who saved us, and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, which was given us in Christ Jesus before times eternal.

— 2 Timothy 1:9

Paul was not chosen because he was worthy.

He was chosen because God was gracious.

Humility

Paul says:

"To me, though I am the very least of all the saints, this grace was given..." Paul calls himself the least of all the saints.

This shows humility.

But Paul's humility was not fake humility.

There is a difference between true humility and pretending to be bad at something you are actually good at just so other people will think you are humble.

Paul's humility was deeper than that.

Paul was haunted by the people he had persecuted.

He had forced people to blaspheme Jesus.

He had tried to destroy the church.

Even after all of that, Paul says:

Grace was still given to me.

Paul never forgot what he had been saved from.

His humility was not about saying nice words about himself.

His humility came from understanding the greatness of Christ and the depth of his own sin. Humility is the enemy of pride and selfishness.

It is one of the clearest fruits of surrender to God.

Ask yourself:

If someone who knows me well had to describe me, would they describe me as humble?

The Mystery Was Hidden

The Jews did not fully understand that salvation was for all people through faith in Jesus Christ. This truth was hidden until God revealed it.

But Ephesians 3:10 takes this even further.

Paul says that God's wisdom is now being made known to:

The rulers and authorities in the heavenly places.

This appears to refer to the spiritual realm.

Even the heavenly authorities were being shown the wisdom of God's eternal plan.

God's plan of salvation was so great that He was revealing His wisdom through the church to the spiritual realm.

The mystery was not simply about Jews and Gentiles.

It was about God's eternal plan to unite all things in Christ.

3. Move Toward Courage

Ephesians 3:11–13

Paul says that this was according to the eternal purpose that God has realized in Christ Jesus. The plan of God was not an accident.

It was not a backup plan.

It was God's eternal purpose.

God's plan was established before the world began.

And because it is God's purpose, its fulfillment is certain.

The Purpose Is Accomplished

Paul says that God's purpose has been accomplished in Christ.

The work of Jesus is certain.

God's plan will be fulfilled.

Nothing can stop it.

And because of this, Paul tells the Ephesians not to lose heart.

Paul was in prison.

He was suffering.

He had been rejected.

He had been beaten.

He had been persecuted.

But Paul did not see his suffering as evidence that God's plan had failed. He understood that he was suffering for the sake of the gospel. Therefore, he says:

Do not lose heart.

Boldness in Christ

Because of Jesus, we have boldness.

Because of Jesus, we have confidence.

Because of Jesus, we have access to God through faith. We do not have to approach God as strangers.

We do not have to approach God as outsiders.

We do not have to approach God wondering whether He will accept us. In Christ, we have confidence.

The mystery has been revealed.

The gospel is for all.

God's plan is certain.

And therefore:

Do not lose heart.

Main Point

The mystery of God has been revealed:

Salvation through Jesus Christ is for all people—Jews and Gentiles alike—through faith in Christ.

Paul's response to this mystery was:

  • Service.
  • Grace.
  • Humility.
  • Courage.

And our response should be the same.

We have been saved by grace, called to serve, humbled by the mercy of God, and strengthened to move forward without losing heart.
