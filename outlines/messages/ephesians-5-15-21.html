Ephesians 5:15–21
Be Careful Fool

1. Walk Carefully and Wisely — V. 15–17

Carefully — Blepo: to discern, observe, and consider.

Paul commands believers to look carefully at how they walk. Wisdom is not simply intelligence or experience. Wisdom comes from God.

“If you seek it like silver and search for it as for hidden treasures, then you will understand the fear of the Lord and find the knowledge of God...” — Proverbs 2:4–10

Wisdom is given through learning and understanding. We must always be growing in our understanding of God.

What makes an unwise man unwise?

An unwise man does not acknowledge God's righteous decrees. He acknowledges only his own.

Romans 1:29–32 describes people who are filled with unrighteousness, evil, covetousness, malice, envy, murder, strife, deceit, gossip, slander, pride, disobedience, foolishness, faithlessness, heartlessness, and ruthlessness. They know God's righteous decree but still practice sin and approve of others who do the same.

Secondly, an unwise man often does not even know that he is unwise.

James 4:13–17 reminds us that we do not know what tomorrow will bring. Our lives are like a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes. Instead of boasting about our plans, we should say, “If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that.”

This is why we must be careful with our lives. We will give an account for how we have used the time and life God has given us.

How do we make the best use of our time?

Romans 6:23 — “For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

James 1:17 — Every good and perfect gift is from above.

Psalm 139:14 — “I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made.”

Your life is a gift from God. We are borrowing this life. If that is true, why would we want to live for ourselves when our lives do not ultimately belong to us?

We should live to please the Creator.

We constantly ask God for things when we are afraid or sad. We ask for money, help, and direction. But why not ask Him about everything?

Where is my next step? Where should I go? Who should I help? Who needs guidance? Where should I place my feet?

Ephesians 5:17 tells us to understand what the will of the Lord is.

Therefore, look carefully at how you walk. Do not waste your life away. Be wise with the time God has given you. Ask Him for wisdom and continue growing in understanding.

2. Be Filled with the Spirit — V. 18–21

Do not be controlled by excess — V. 18

Debauchery — Asotia: reckless or wasteful living, especially in the use of resources. Paul contrasts being drunk with wine and being filled with the Spirit.

Proverbs 20:1 — “Wine is a mocker, strong drink a brawler, and whoever is led astray by it is not wise.”

Dr. Wayne Barber says:

“What fills a man controls a man.”

This applies to alcohol, but it also applies to anything we allow to control our lives. Whatever fills you will influence your thoughts, decisions, priorities, and actions. Five principles of a Spirit-filled believer — V. 19–21

Paul gives us five results of being filled with the Spirit:

1. Speaking — addressing one another in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs. 2. Singing — expressing joy in salvation.

3. Making melody — worshiping the Lord from the heart.

4. Giving thanks — giving thanks always and for everything.

5. Submitting — submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ.

A Spirit-filled believer is not simply someone who claims to have the Spirit. The Spirit produces visible evidence in how we speak, worship, give thanks, and treat others.

Speaking, Singing, and Making Melody — V. 19

Wuest explains that this passage is not describing a Christian simply singing to himself. Believers are to speak to one another through psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs.

Our joy in salvation should overflow into the lives of others.

Charles Swindoll describes this kind of surrender:

“This is your day, Lord. I want to be at Your disposal... From this moment on throughout this day, I’m Yours, Lord.”

This is what it means to be available to God.

Giving Thanks — V. 20

Paul says we are to give thanks always and for everything to God the Father.

A Spirit-filled believer is not someone who never experiences difficulty. A Spirit-filled believer is someone who learns to see God even in the difficulty.

Submitting to One Another — V. 21

Reverence: deep respect for someone or something.

Submit: to accept or yield to the authority or will of another.

Submission is an act of humility. You do not always have to win the argument. You do not always have to get your way.

Somewhere along the line, if you constantly have to win, you may have stopped submitting to one another.

Dr. Wayne Barber explains that submission is ultimately an act of faith. We trust God to direct our lives and accomplish His purposes in His timing.

There is a danger in submitting to others because people can take advantage of us. But when we are truly submitted to God and desire to serve others, we will not use submission as an opportunity to take advantage of people.

Main Point

A wise Christian carefully walks through life, seeking the will of God, and remains continually filled with the Holy Spirit. The Spirit-filled believer is marked by worship, thanksgiving, and humble submission to others for the sake of Christ.
