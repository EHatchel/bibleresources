The Cross is the Cost
Matthew 16:24-27

This text is written in a very meaningful color , the color Red. Which if you didn’t know is the words of Jesus Christ . So the writer of the passage is Mathew , but the words written are the words of God almighty .

Most people don’t mind following Jesus—

they just don’t want it to cost them anything.

We like a Savior who forgives our sins,

but not always a Lord who demands our lives.

We want eternal life, but we want to keep control of this one.

And the question Jesus asks in Matthew 16 is not,

“Do you believe in Me?”

but, “What are you willing to lose for Me?”

Because somewhere along the way, we’ve convinced ourselves that following Christ should add to our lives— when Jesus says it will first strip us of everything.

Tonight, Jesus speaks with unsettling clarity.

He tells us discipleship has a cost, that a soul has infinite value, and that a day is coming when He will return in glory, and repay every life exactly as it was lived.

So the question before us is simple—but terrifying:

Is anything you’re holding onto worth more than your soul, and worth more than standing before Christ when He returns?

Main point:

The cost of the cross is eminent death tossing aside pride and self value. The price of a soul is immeasurable and the eternal reward for the faithful is certain.

Point 1- The Cost to Follow the Cross (v24-25)

If anyone would come after me. 2 commandments

Deny yourself - what does it mean to deny yourself?

To bring this closer to an easier understanding I think of Philippians 3:7-8 “But whatever gain I had, I counted as a loss for the sake of Christ. Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ”

Denying yourself includes overcoming the persistent fleshly demands of the body, also known as the carnal self or the natural man, and bringing them into submission to God’s Word so that you don’t give in to sin: “And those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires”

Jesus calls on those who wish to be his followers to reject own selfishness in order to grow in commitment towards him and righteousness in God.

Take up his cross -

When a person carried a cross in Jesus’ day, no one thought of it as a persistent annoyance or symbolic burden. To a person in the first century, the cross meant one thing and one thing only: death by crucifixion. To carry a cross was to face the most painful and humiliating means of death human beings could develop.

Two thousand years later, Christians view the cross as a cherished symbol of atonement, forgiveness, grace, and love. But in Jesus’ day the cross represented a torturous death. The Romans forced convicted criminals to carry their own crosses to the place of crucifixion. Bearing a cross meant one was about to die, and that one would face ridicule and disgrace along the way.

What is the cost ? A payment , Christmas shopping: gift, foods, decorations, you have to choose what to buy. Every payment or cost , you choose to pay the cost . Jesus chose to pay the cost of the cross . Now he is asking us to make a payment of his desires over our own . The everyday choice. The inherent choice . Am I going to pay today ?

Neglecting the magnitude of this choice because Jesus has paid is overdone . What are you paying for ? You have to ask your father for his money everyday. His spirit , his power ,

the renewal of your mind, and the constant growth in sanctification to look less like you and more like him .

This is what it means to carry his cross . Brutally, this is a call to self-sacrifice. Knowing death is the price that was paid for your eternity. One must be willing to die in order to follow Jesus. Dying to self is an absolute surrender to God.

Point 2- The Cost of a Soul (v26)

To gain the whole world is to gain all the comfort, pleasure, power, prestige. This causes you to forfeit your soul, which is to die without a right relationship with Christ and spend an eternity in the lake of fire.

Notice the phrasing of forfeit and not Death . The soul cannot die but only can be lost . A sole is eternal and must go somewhere .

This is the heed he is presenting . Warning! Pay attention ! The soul has to go somewhere, where will yours end up. Rejecting Christ might gain you some earthly temporary gain at the expense of the worse price.

Jesus says that we must give up everything, a full surrender, drop the defenses and wave the white flag of our own selfish ways and declare him king and lord of our lives. Only then will we gain our soul an eternal inheritance.

If you choose to embrace the world instead of heaven you have forfeited your soul . Choosing anything over Christ and rejecting him is the poorest decision one could ever make.

Once a man has lost his soul, it is lost. It cannot be bought back. The man forfeits and suffers the loss of it forever. Imagine! Even if a man possessed all the wealth of the world, he would not be able to buy back his soul. Why? Because it is gone; it has passed on forever. The man will never return to earth, not even for one day. He is gone forever.

Point 3- The Reward Worth The Cost(v27)

“According to what he has done” Inheritance

At first glance, this verse may seem straightforward—a statement about judgment. But when we unpack it, it carries a weighty reminder for every believer about how we live our lives here and now.

This truth connects directly to what Jesus has been teaching in the verses leading up to this moment. First, we see the cost of following the cross. In verses 24–25, Jesus tells us to deny ourselves, take up our cross, and follow Him. This isn’t a casual invitation; it is a call to total surrender. It may cost our comfort, our ambitions, even our reputation in this world—but it is the path of true discipleship.

Then, in verse 26, He reminds us of the value of the soul. ‘What good will it be for someone to gain the whole world, yet forfeit their soul?’ Here, Jesus shows that no earthly gain can compare to the worth of what is eternal. Our efforts, sacrifices, and

sufferings for Christ are never wasted because they touch something far greater than this temporary life—the eternal destiny of our soul.

Verse 27 calls us to recognize the seriousness of our daily choices. Every decision, every action, every attitude matters because one day it will all be accounted for. The good we do in secret, the faith we live out even when no one sees, the sacrifices we make to follow Christ faithfully—these are the things that will receive reward. Conversely, those who ignore God’s call, who live only for themselves, will also face the full weight of their choices. There is no escaping God’s justice, and there is no favor for the casual follower.

Jesus reminds us that the way we live today has eternal consequences. Every person will one day stand before Him and receive what they deserve for the choices they have made—whether they have followed Him faithfully or turned from His call. This truth should motivate us to count the cost of discipleship, value the soul above all else, and persevere in faith, knowing that the reward Christ promises is not only certain but far greater than any sacrifice we make for Him now.

Application

Are you ready to take up your cross and follow Jesus? Consider these questions: – Are you willing to follow Jesus if it means losing your closest friends?

Are you willing to follow Jesus if it means alienation from your family? – Are you willing to follow Jesus if it means losing your reputation? – Are you willing to follow Jesus if it means losing your job? – Are you willing to follow Jesus if it means losing your life?
