Ephesians 4:17–24
The Right Kind of Walking

We’re in chapter 4, if you remember last time, the first 3 chapters (1,2,3) of Ephesians are considered doctrinal where the Ephesians are learning and being reminded. Now in the last 3 (4,5,6) is more applicational, calling the Ephesians to do, to act.

Skip Heitzig approaches it from three separate sections,

The Wealth, The Walk, and the Warfare.

In chapter 4 we’re in the Walking section.

For sake of us understanding what Paul is addressing as we move forward,

Walk, in the context of this section, translates to peripateō (per-ee-pat-eh'-o)

- to walk
- to make one's way, progress; to make due use of opportunities
-* Hebrew * for, to live

to regulate one's life

to conduct one's self

to pass one's life

That being said, my first point is:

1. The Gentile Walk

17 Now this I say and testify in the Lord, that you must no longer walk as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their minds. 18 They are darkened in their understanding, alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance that is in them, due to their hardness of heart. 19 They have become callous and have given themselves up to sensuality, greedy to practice every kind of impurity.

The first thing we see in 17a is Paul “testifying” that we “must”. Not “shouldn’t” not Paul recommending we don’t, but we MUST no longer walk as the Gentiles do. It’s a requirement.

Many treat it as a suggestion, like it’s optional, a side quest even.

They try to still “do them” while claiming to be letting God “do him” in their lives. They hold on to parts of themselves for their own reasons (to fit in, pleasure, pride)

But just as Paul wrote in Galatians 2:20 it says,

“20 I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.”

It is no longer YOU but CHRIST. And The life you live NOW, as opposed to before. There’s a contrast.

If you remember the first thing we covered when we started extraget preaching, Jesus taught this same concept with Salt and Light (Matt 5:13-16).

As a believer you're supposed to contrast the world, if you fit in you may or may not be what you think you are.

17b, Paul begins to address how the Gentiles (or in related applicable terms “non-believers”) walk.

They walk in Futility of their minds.

The translation of Futility is mataiotēs (mat-ah-yot'-ace)

what is devoid of truth and appropriateness

perverseness, depravity

frailty, want of vigour

Others summarize it as pointlessness, aimless.

Gentile/Unbelievers lack true purpose due to being void of the truth in Christ Jesus.

If you’ve ever watched Cliffe Knechtle argue with unbelievers, this is something he brings up often. Without God, life has no meaning. He elaborates that life is just one big accident therefore justifying us to do whatever we want without God.

This is what Paul is addressing.

Along with aimlessness without purpose, 18a Paul tells us they are blinded.

It’s difficult to really paint the picture of this level of ignorant unconcerned nature… because it’s literally blindness.

Proverbs 4:19 put’s it this way:

The way of the wicked is like deep darkness;

they do not know over what they stumble.

If you remember the you before Christ, unconcerned with what the Bible says, though hearing and knowing of its existence, this is what this is addressing. Unconcerned, going through life giving yourself to things of this world that in the end destroy you.

I also associated this with the conversation we had a few weeks ago in regards to knowing someone’s saved before addressing sanctification, prayer, and other aspects.

You can show them all the truth in the world, a Christophany like we see in the old testament could appear before them, but being literally blinded in that regard could they even see it? Would they even want to?

‘Wanting to’ ties into 18b where Paul says they’re alienated due to their hardness (other translations double down on ‘Blindness’) of heart.

The LMW commentary addresses that this isn’t God doing. The Bible makes it clear God wants everyone to come to know him. It’s their own wilful ignorance and intentional “hardness” of their heart. If you’ve ever tried to speak to someone about Jesus and received hostile rebuke or exile over it, this is what this portion is addressing. Again, they know “The Bible” is there and see the facts adding up to Jesus being who he says he is. They just choose to not believe it and think little or indifferent of you as a result of your faithfulness. Why? They were alienated. Cut off. Detached. Unassociated with the Love in the Bible they never chose to pick up and read. When you receive backlash from speaking of Jesus from someone, and see yourself asking “How can someone be so cruel?” Remember they’re alienated from the Love you’re preaching of.

Jesus addressed this in the, commonly overlooked, back half of John 3:16

John 3:16-19

16 “For God so loved the world,[] that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life. 17 For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him. 18 Whoever believes in him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already, because he has not believed in the name of the only Son of God. 19 And this is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil.

19a We read Callous.

If you ever bench pressed, deadlift, or back squat, I’m sure you remember the pain from holding the bar in your hands and fingers or the top of your back for squatting. Your skin may have torn, I’ve seen some people even start bleeding. It hurts. But if you do it enough times often enough, the area will become a hard layer of compressed skin that your body (flesh) makes to help prevent the uncomfortable skin damage allowing you to continue on without feeling the pain of your exercise.

In the same way our flesh makes a callous to block the pain of an exercise, our flesh will callous our heart and soul to block out the conviction of the sin in our lives. We feel bad the first few times, but if we do it enough times we won’t feel the pain, maybe won’t feel anything at all when it’s through with you.

Acts 28:27

“For this people's heart has grown dull, and with their ears they can barely hear, and their eyes they have closed; lest they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears and understand with their heart and turn, and I would heal them.”

I don’t want to spend much on this since this isn’t what we’re here to go over today, but in the later half of that verse it addresses that it’s not that God has rejected the callused. If that was the case we all, or at least I, would have been doomed. It’s not a matter of CAN they come back, it’s an issue of IF they ever even want to though their calloused heart.

(kinda) 19b “have given themselves up to sensuality, greedy to practice every kind of impurity.”

Greek for Sensuality

unbridled lust, excess, licentiousness, lasciviousness, wantonness, outrageousness, shamelessness, insolence

(Whatever those all mean)

For standard definition sake…

the enjoyment, expression, or pursuit of physical, especially sexual, pleasure.

the condition of being pleasing or fulfilling to the senses.

They do what feels good, not so much what is good. It’s often a quick fix to a more terminal problem. It’s bandaids on bullet holes. Yet, as we spoke before, it’s often the same thing killing them to begin with. Only the more they feed into it, the more it feeds on them.

Nonetheless, the Bible says they’re “greedy to practice.”

Q: What comes to mind?

A: For me, it’s premarital sex. You do it once, you're nervous, if you were saved prior to doing it, just like the text implies you probably felt a level of conviction after. Yet, temptation drags you by the ankle to slip again. And again. And again.

Difference is, do you give in or put your faith in God to desire what his word says? Do you “greed” for it? Or do you desire to oppose it out of love for Jesus?

To shortly summarize, Paul describes gentiles (unbelievers) walk as void of truth, blinded by their hardness of heart, spiritually callused by their greed for their pleasure-full sensuality.

Joke: Sound about right?

2. How Christ Walked

20 But that is not the way you learned Christ!— 21 assuming that you have heard about him and were taught in him, as the truth is in Jesus,

It’s not. Whether you read this text as that's not how Jesus was on earth with us or that the behavior we just went over was not the kind that drew you into fellowship with the Lord, that is not how you learned Christ.

Jesus lived a perfect life and died on a cross out of Love to be in relationship with us. He who knew no sin, became Sin (2 Cor 5:21). Not abolishing the law, but fulfilling it (Matt 5:17). He was tempted as we were, but never failed. And so much more. But nowhere in there did he make walking both roads a choice. He came to give us new life (John 10:10). But we had to leave the old one behind. Matthew 16:24-26:

“24 Then Jesus told his disciples, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross and follow me. 25 For whoever would save his life[] will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake will find it. 26 For what will it profit a man if he gains the whole world and forfeits his soul? Or what shall a man give in return for his soul?”

You must die so Christ may live. John 12:24-25:

“24 Truly, truly, I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit. 25 Whoever loves his life loses it, and whoever hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life.”

Jesus taught it was one way, not both.

In addition that’s probably not what drew you in. Least I would imagine not. Church hurt is more common than not. People not walking right gives Christ a bad wrap and pushes people away doing more harm than good.

I started this new job and that’s been nearly every conversation I’ve had with my co-workers. Someone claiming Christ but living another pushed them away. It’s sad but true. If you’re here by relation or recommendation you most likely experienced someone walking the right way, a new and better way. Now it’s your turn.

3. Your New Walk

22 to put off your old self,[] which belongs to your former manner of life and is corrupt through deceitful desires, 23 and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds, 24 and to put on the new self, created after the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness.

Simply put, do the opposite of what we went over in 17-19.

22 Paul is saying to not be that way anymore. Don’t be alienated from the Lord, void of truth, blind, hardness of heart, greeting over what makes you feel good in the moment that’s killing the soul.

23 be renewed.

Q: What do you believe it means to be renewed?

A: It ties into sanctification, where it takes time. We learned Ephes. 2:3 that we’re born children of wrath. It’s something we must continuously work on. Not for salvation that's given to us through faith alone, but towards sanctification and even then and in the end it takes the Lord to really change our lives if you let him. It won’t be over night, but like we’ve said before you’re either growing or dying. There is no just staying the same. You put your faith in him, and believe to the point you desire to get better and learn every day.

24 You live Christ like. Not necessarily “Christian” as it’s seen today. But as a true follower of Christ. In a manner that displays the change that has taken place in your life. Love God with all your heart (Matt 22:37), Love your neighbor (Matt 22:39), flee from temptation (1 Cor 6:18), care for your body as a temple of the holy spirit inside of you (1 Cor 6:19), with the fruits (love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control)(Gal 5:22), and the rest… I’ll let you read for yourselves. To learn and to grow. Desire to be more like him everyday till the day he returns where we’re completely sanctified in the end.

If I had to summarize this entire text into a single verse for you to memorize and store in your hearts, it would be the same verse you’ve heard time and time again in here, it was even the first one I used for this message, Galatians 2:20:

20 I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.

Main Point:

Paul tells the church of Ephesius to walk, not as the gentiles are blinded and calloused by their lustful desires, but in the way Christ showed them how to walk. Put off their former manor of life so that they may be made new in likeness to Christ.

Application:

Die to yourself everyday so Christ may live, Galatians 2:20
