Justice is Coming - Nahum 1

Imagine living next door to someone who terrorizes the entire neighborhood.

Every night there are sirens. Every week someone else’s house gets vandalized. People whisper about what’s happening, but nobody stops it. The bully just keeps getting away with it.

At first, everyone hoped it would stop. Then they started to wonder, “Why hasn’t anyone done something?” Eventually the whole neighborhood began to feel helpless… like justice would never come.

But one day, police cars pulled up. Officers stepped out with authority. The one who had caused fear for so long is finally confronted. The neighborhood watches as justice finally arrives. And suddenly what looked like silence wasn’t really silence at all. The authorities had been watching the whole time—waiting for the right moment to act.

In many ways, that’s the world Nahum was speaking into.

For years the brutal empire of Assyria had terrorized nations. Its capital city, Nineveh, was known for cruelty, violence, and oppression. People wondered if anyone could ever stop them.

But Nahum steps onto the scene with a message that changes everything: God has not ignored evil. God has not forgotten the suffering. And when the time is right… His justice will come.

That’s how Nahum chapter 1 begins—declaring that the Lord is both patient and powerful, slow to anger but certain in justice.

Point 1 - God’s Character vv.1-3

Thomas Constable: “This book claims to be an oracle (1:1, an uplifting and or threatening prophecy). While most of the book threatens Nineveh with destruction, there are also words of comfort for the people of Judah (1:12, 15; 2:2). Nahum revealed that

Yahweh would destroy Nineveh as punishment for the Assyrians' cruelty to many nations, including the Northern Kingdom of Israel in 722 B.C. and Judah. This was a comforting message for the remaining Jews who were presently living under Assyria's shadow in Judah. Assyria had destroyed many Judean cities and had even besieged Jerusalem, unsuccessfully, in 701 B.C. The purpose of Nahum's book, then, was to announce Nineveh's fall and thereby comfort the Judean Jews with the assurance that their God was indeed sovereign and just.”

God begins by revealing His character. He is jealous, avenging, and slow to anger. His jealousy is not sinful envy but a holy passion for His glory and justice. The Lord will not allow wickedness to go unanswered forever.

Jealous:

In 2 Corinthians 11:2, Paul writes, “I am jealous for you with a godly jealousy. I promised you to one husband, to Christ, so that I might present you as a pure virgin to him.” We normally think of jealousy as being a negative trait or even a sin, akin to envy. Yet jealousy can also be a godly trait. There are times when jealousy is appropriate. God is described as being provoked to jealousy over idolatry (Exodus 20:4–5). God is jealous when someone takes something that rightly belongs to Him and keeps it for himself or gives it to another. God alone deserves our worship and praise.

Think about it like this, if someone you loved deeply gave their time, love, and loyalty to someone else, you wouldn’t just shrug it off. You’d care. That’s the kind of jealousy God has. It’s not selfish, it’s protective and loving. He knows nothing else will truly satisfy us like He can.

God’s jealousy isn’t about Him needing us, it’s about Him loving us. He knows that when we chase other things instead of Him, it actually hurts us. So His jealousy is really His way of saying, ‘I want what’s best for you, and that’s Me. God’s jealousy isn’t a flaw, it’s proof of His love. He’s not jealous of you; He’s jealous for you.

God’s jealousy is tied to covenant. Just like in marriage, there’s a commitment, ‘you are mine, and I am yours.’ So when we turn to other things, it’s like spiritual unfaithfulness. His jealousy is His holy response to protect that relationship.

Avenging:

The Bible has a great deal to say about revenge. Both the Hebrew and Greek words translated “vengeance,” “revenge,” and “avenge” have as their root meaning the idea of punishment. This is crucial in understanding why God reserves for Himself the right to avenge.

This is the same reasoning we are commanded not to judge. He says that he is the only judge that will cast judgment. God is the only perfect one so he is the only one who can cast perfect judgement. He also made morals and righteousness, so he knows what makes a man righteous. We are to allow Him to judge rightly and pour out His divine retribution against His enemies as He sees fit.

Point 2: God’s Power vv.4-6

Nahum describes God’s overwhelming power through nature—seas dry up, mountains melt, and the earth trembles before Him. The point is clear: if all creation responds to His authority, then no empire, no matter how powerful, can stand against Him.

Walter C. Kaiser, Jr.: “The book, then, is not about Nahum’s personal hatred for Assyria or about Judah’s lust for vengeance. It is about God’s almighty rule over all peoples, nations and cultures. It is another affirmation that, since God created all persons in His image, a person cannot treat others inhumanely and hope to escape God’s punishment. It also affirms that the law of God is based on His nature and personal character; therefore, holiness, righteousness, and justice are expected of all rulers, potentates, and nations, regardless of whether they believe in the Living God.”

We could go through and break down each one of these instances, but the examples are all pointing to one conclusion. God is the authoritative of life. God is supreme and holds all the power in his hand. In this instance he will use this power to protect Israel and to destroy Nineveh.

What we think is stable, God can shake with a sentence. If mountains melt before Him, empires don’t stand a chance. God’s wrath doesn’t warn forever—it eventually falls.

He ends with a question in verse 6, “Who can stand?” Obviously no one can, but i want to ask you a question, Where do you stand with God? Because you found out on your own that you can't stand without Him, so now where do you stand with Him?

Right in the middle of this message of judgment comes a beautiful contrast: “The Lord is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble.” For those who trust Him, God is refuge. But for the wicked, like the oppressive empire centered in Nineveh of Assyria, His justice is certain.

When I began to look at verse 7 something so critical caught my attention. The wording says, “ A stronghold in the day of trouble.” Notice not from trouble, but in trouble. We know God doesn't always remove the storm, he becomes your shelter in it. The christian life wont surpass problems, but there is a God that will lift you up with his righteous right hand. You don’t have to escape the storm when you’re hidden in the Stronghold.

The “overwhelming flood” shows total, decisive judgment. Darkness pursuing His enemies = no escape, no hiding. Even when the wicked seem tangled and thriving, God will cut them down fully. God doesn’t manage evil, He wipes it out.

Nineveh then plots evil against God's people, but it was in Vain. You cannot out think God. We know in Hebrews 4:13 it talks about no one is hidden from the site of God. It is pointless to think you are witty enough to play God a fool. I feel the same way about your excuses, God doesn't buy them. You think you are the first person ever to come up with why that sin is actually not a sin. Even then God will say “ Oh yeah he's absolutely right it is sin no longer.” Absolutely not. Your excuses are vain and you cannot plot against an all knowing God. Nineveh would find out consequently. You can fool people, but you cannot outthink God.

This is both national (Israel) and personal (our sin, bondage, burdens). Jesus is like this today, “Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28.
