EPHESIANS OVERVIEW

Who wrote it

Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus (Eph. 1:1)

• His Jewish name before being a

Christian was Saul of Tarsus. (Acts 13:9).

• Paul was known as Saul. Before becoming a Christian, he killed Christians and brought them to prison (Acts 9).
• He was a man that hated Christians and became a man who led Christians.
• Paul had a ministry from 33 AD-68 AD. The Bible records 3 times that he was arrested for his faith, but there could have been more not recorded in Acts.

When was it written ?

Estimated around 61 AD.

• Written while in Prison (3:1), (4:1), and (6:20).
• There’s disagreements on if this was the time he was imprisoned in Rome (Acts 28:30) or Caesarea (Acts 24:22).
• It was most likely when he was imprisoned in Rome in a guarded rental quarters (Acts 28:30) (House Arrest).
• He most likely wrote Ephesians, Colossians, Philemon, and Philippians during this time or around this time. All “prison epistles”

All of acts chapter 19 is his time in Ephesus. We learn a few things about his time there . He was spreading the word of God mightily there and it bothered some people .

The city was known for magic and the first story (Acts 19:13-16) is a Jewish exorcist who tried to heal in the name of Jesus ,but was unsuccessful.

Later converted magicians ended up burning there books on the magic they studied (Acts 19:19)

Some theologians believed that Paul was actually imprisoned in Ephesus. Why was it written ?

It is intended that all who long for Christlike maturity would receive this writing.

• The aim of this epistle is to confirm and to equip a maturing church. It presents a balanced view of the body of Christ and its importance in God’s economy.
• Ephesians will help to fortify and to establish the believer so he can fulfill the purpose and calling God has given.

What is the structure ?

It is an epistle: a book of the New Testament in the form of a letter from an Apostle. (Pauline)

• Chapters 1-3 introduce God’s lavish grace and divine purpose for the new humanity. And chapters 4-6 exhort readers to live according to God’s gift and offer a challenge for unity in the new humanity.
