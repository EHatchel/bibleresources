A God of Second Chances - Jonah 3

Main Point: Jonah submits to God's will, reaching Nineveh and driving them to repentance from their wicked ways. Submitting all they have to God in fear and trembling.

1. Second Chances (vv. 1-2)

Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, 2 "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you." Second chances.. It's rare today to see many of those.

In today's world it doesn't take much for people to rule you out or make up their mind about you. You accidentally cut someone off in traffic? You just don't know how to drive. You made a bad call at work? You don't know how to do your job. You fail a single test in school? Grade is final, no re-tries. You're young and get in trouble with the law? Society will punish you for it till you die, jail or not.

But God… Thankfully God, abounding in grace, knows our imperfection and chooses to call out to people and use them no matter how hard they run or mess up. He's a God of second chances.

We saw this even before Jonah with Moses, Not only Moses fleeing Egypt after killing the guard, did God call on him in the desert yet again (Exodus 2:11-15, Acts 7:23-30), he most notably smashed God's 10 commandments on the ground in a fit of anger over the golden calf. (Deuteronomy 9:13-21)

Did God do away with the Israelites and Moses then? Go find new and more obedient people? No. He gave them a second chance to hold the covenant. No different grace than what Jesus taught us with the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11-32).

How wonderful it is, that like the 1 lost from the 99, that God's faithful to chase after us though we run from obedience to him. Not to leave us dead, abstained from his call. Plenty of people on their way to nineveh that day! But he chose Jonah, and was gonna give him every chance to make it right all while saving people on the way.

2. It'll only take a Second. (vv. 3-5)

3 So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh, according to the word of the LORD. Now Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth. 4 Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey. And he called out, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" 5 And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them.

Jonah, 100% on the fence between going to Nineveh and going back to the fish, knows he has a long journey ahead of him. Not only did he have to travel, mostly on foot, to even reach the city; He acknowledged the great size of this wicked city God has sent him to.

Three days of journey just to get around the inside of the city, let alone reach every single wicked and evil person along the way. This is assuming the same people he's sent to reach already don't wanna kill him let alone listen to him. The task ahead of Jonah only seemed to get bigger and much scarier as he went. Yet, somewhere between obedience and the mild desire to just die, he persisted. He even took a days journey into the city, probably fighting his demons to not run again. And finally, after his days of travel for a task he'd rather die than do, he gets to his spot in the city.

I imagine he sat his stuff down, wondering why such wicked people deserve this grace, maybe even preparing to die a miserable death God had sent him to. He takes a deep breath and says "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!". …and the people, v5, did what? Stoned him? Hung him? Cast him back into the sea? A God of Second Chances. The Bible tells us "and the people of Nineveh believed God".

William MacDonald commented that it's theorized that the people of Nineveh heard of what happened to Jonah and the fish. Their worship of the fish god Dagon, had prompted them to believe no man could survive such an event without their skin being deformed from the digestive fluid of the animal.

In that moment they realized their "god" wasn't the same as Jonah's God. And while what I believe to be Jonah's short one liner of what God actually gave him wasn't the whole thing, it was enough (backed by his testimony with the fish) to strike fear and awe inspiring worship into the people of Ninivah. Leaving them to believe and repent.

Again, what a comedian God is to have Jonah think his rebellious actions were the opposite of what God willed only for Him to use in the end all along. Not saying our rebellious nature is God's fault, just how blessed we are as people that God uses our worst intentions for his greatest outcomes.

All I could think when writing this was Jonah was playing checkers while God was playing chess, always 10+ steps ahead. As for the sack cloth and fasting, GotQuestions writes, "Sackcloth and ashes were used in Old Testament times as a symbol of debasement, mourning, and/or repentance. Someone wanting to show his repentant heart would often wear sackcloth, sit in ashes, and put ashes on top of his head. Sackcloth was a God of Second Chances coarse material usually made of black goat's hair, making it quite uncomfortable to wear. The ashes signified desolation and ruin."

In regard to Jonah they write, "Sackcloth and ashes were also used as a public sign of repentance and humility before God. When Jonah declared to the people of Nineveh that God was going to destroy them for their wickedness, everyone from the king on down responded with repentance, fasting, and sackcloth and ashes" I'd like to say thankfully we don't have that tradition today, but honestly we may need it. The people of Ninavah though so wicked and ungodly, knew repentance meant acknowledging their wrong, humility, and most importantly change. They were brutal and proud people. Like war mongers of their day, they obsessed with idol worship, power, dominance, torture, and violence. And almost in an instant, their in-counter with God left them hungry, cold, and humbled in the skin of something as dead as they were in sin. The feared became afraid, the proud became humbled, and the wealthy became poor in spirit. They repented.

What about us? How do you repent? Do you turn from your wicked ways as a decision then and there? Or do you tell God, "I'll give it up in a week, I just need time", "I need to wean myself off, I can't go cold turkey", "I'll act right when they act right", or "I probably should work on that one day when I have time". Is your repentance turning away from sin toward obedience in that moment or just another chore you put off till it gets bad enough?

No one's saying it's not hard, but easy's found on the broad path, not the narrow one. A God of Second Chances Maybe we need to bust out the sackcloths again in regard to how abrupt and socially absurd it can be to repent but how important it is for us to follow God and use his abounding grace as an opportunity to be obedient to the one who saved us.

2 Peter 3:9 "9 The Lord is not slow in keeping his promise, as some understand slowness. Instead he is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance."

Nineveh was given with 40 days and nights, yet none of us are promised tomorrow. Maybe we need the sackcloths.

3. Second Chance Revival (vv. 6-9)

6 The word reached the king of Nineveh, and he arose from his throne, removed his robe, covered himself with sackcloth, and sat in ashes. 7 And he issued a proclamation and published through Nineveh, "By the decree of the king and his nobles: Let neither man nor beast, herd nor flock, taste anything. Let them not feed or drink water, 8 but let man and beast be covered with sackcloth, and let them call out mightily to God. Let everyone turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands. 9 Who knows? God may turn and relent and turn from his fierce anger, so that we may not perish."

Billy Graham often referred to this section of scripture as one of if not the greatest instances of revival ever. From king to farm animal, all turned to God in humility and fast at the wickedness of their nature. The word reached even the King, that's why who we have leadership matters. Both of a country/city, but in every aspect of our lives. A God of Second Chances

I find it hilarious that this wicked king/governor of Nineveh, who lived so abstained from the Lord and his will, almost in an instant receives him. Not only that, but leads his entire city (3 days travel in length) to repentance and fear of the Lord. Meanwhile, Israel runs through Kings during this time trying to find any of them to be faithful and lead God's people.

Nevertheless, we know leadership is appointed by God. Not only in Romans 13:1-2. 13 Let everyone be subject to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established. The authorities that exist have been established by God.

But also in this time, Daniel 2:21 "He changes times and seasons; he deposes kings and raises up others. He gives wisdom to the wise and knowledge to the discerning." It's clear, both for God's will for Nineveh and the king's own soul, that God's in control. Something we need to remember even today, no matter who runs for office, God raises up kings and he's liable to dispose of them as well.

But regardless of who the king is, just as for Jonah's situation, God calls us to be faithful. Faithful to reaching people, walking out Matthew 28:16-20. God's not concerned with who we cast our votes for or who has the better campaign, he's concerned with whos more bought in to his campaign. His will, not our idea of what we believe should happen.

Now more than ever, it's important to be faithful even if the world leaders around us are not. Who knows, A God of Second Chances maybe like Jonah you share the right message that drives belief and repentance to our leaders' hearts. As for the animals being fasted and sackclothed,

Q: why do we think this is? It's as simple as it's put in v9, 9 Who knows? God may turn and relent and turn from his fierce anger, so that we may not perish." The "Who Knows?" one could say almost comes off sarcastic.

In reality… it's desperate. The king is so desperate for Nineveh repentance that he wants everything in the kingdom to be humbled before God seeking mercy, even their possessions like livestock.

Q: How often do we make sure our possessions and what we hold valuable are obedient to the Lord? Not that the Lord is seeking belief and repentance from our PS5s, but out of submission is our belongings used in obedience to God?

It could be said for vehicles, do we own nice vehicles to pick up chicks to do God only knows what in the Sonic parking lot? Or do we use it as a conversation starter to advance the Gospel? Maybe to pick others up to bring them to group and serve our Church?

Sackclothing and fasting the animals wasn't so they could have cows in heaven, it was to show full submission, surrender, and humility before God. Something Christians struggle with even today.

4. God's Second Chance to Nineveh (v. 10)

10 When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, God relented of the disaster that he had said he would do to them, and he did not do it. If I had to summarize it would be the same verse I shared earlier.

2 Peter 3:9 "9 The Lord is not slow in keeping his promise, as some understand slowness. Instead he is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance."

Jonah completed the command God gave him in the beginning, Nineveh was reached, they believed, they repented, and they were spared. Spared from what? "The wages of sin is death" - Roman 6:23

Matthew 10:28 (ESV): Jesus warns to "fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell". A God of Second Chances Revelation 21:8 (ESV): "But as for the cowardly, the faithless, the detestable, as for murderers, the sexually immoral, sorcerers, idolaters, and all liars, their portion will be in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur, which is the second death". He gave Nineveh 40 days, but he would've been righteous to take them out immediately. But His goal isn't punishment, it's relationship. He was patient with Nineveh, he's patient with us. Desiring us to turn away from our wickedness, repent from our sins, and live in obedience to his word.

Application

Live a life of repentance in full submission to the Lord.
