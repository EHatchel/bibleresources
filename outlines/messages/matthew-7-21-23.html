Matthew 7:21-23
The Will Over the Works

“Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven. On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’ And then will I declare to them, ‘I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of lawlessness.’”

Introduction

Beginning the study of the words of Jesus Christ our Lord is no easy feat. To be vulnerable with you all, every time I get the opportunity to teach a text I am immediately nervous. It is a heavy call to break down a text to help people better understand it and pull application so we may be able to apply the doctrines of God.

In order for me, a Tipton County graduate, to break down a divine and alive book, inspired through men to write by an all-knowing, all-mighty, many-mysteries God, is an overwhelming task not to be taken lightly.

Not only this, but also we see the red text and my heart skips a beat. My Savior’s written word has the power to raise the dead, and for apparent reason I was called to be a teacher of His word.

This is an honor that cannot be described by any means. This is the reason I had to learn a valuable lesson: I have to rely on His strength for everything.

I rely on Him to write my messages and relay His words through my voice.

I rely on His strength to get up every day and go to work heartily.

I rely on His strength to make me strong.

I rely on His strength to get me to take a step.

I rely on His strength to even take a breath.

This is what it means to be in full surrender to Him and His will.

Father, lend me Your strength through everything I do and let Your will be done always. My will is prideful and wicked, but Yours is holy and perfected.

For the ones who don’t understand this, Jesus speaks to them directly toward the end of the Sermon on the Mount.

In this passage today…

1. The Design — The Father’s Will

Not everyone who professes Christ possesses Christ.

“Not everyone” is the beginning of this passage, and as you see we find ourselves in the aroma of heart posture. This poses an important note to contemplate: if not everyone, then who?

Let’s begin with who will not depart from Him, as this is the latter example.

There are people who did and will do what is listed in this passage. There will be some who prophesy, cast out demons, and do mighty works. These people do these works out of humility and selflessness. They have no selfish ambition and rely on the Father for everything they may do.

Colossians 3:23–24

23 Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men, 24 knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ.

They work heartily and hope to gain nothing. The work is the byproduct of faith.

I’m not going to dive off into this, especially because I know y’all have talked through James when he writes, “Faith without works is dead.” It especially is relayed here though.

Why does Jesus use the title “Lord, Lord”?

Why does He use the double tetragrammaton?

There could be a couple reasons:

Reverence or high urgency message.

To label two different titles (divine and human).

Have you ever had to ask your parents for something you were nervous about?

Before doing so you might compliment your mom’s hair or how nice her makeup looks. This is strictly out of selfish ambition, as you can see. Whether or not I thought her hair was nice or not, I would still say it in order that I might be accepted.

I did this many times with my cousin Cohen. We would go to my great-grandmother’s for a family get-together and immediately make plans to hang out.

My parents didn’t mind me hanging out with people, but they did mind asking without planning ahead and it being last minute. So very often I would go to my mom—“because she was kinder”—and would compliment her in order that she would allow me to go without much restraint.

In my desire for being allowed to go, I would do everything outwardly that had nothing to do with my parents but rather everything to do with me.

Luke 22:41-42

’And he withdrew from them about a stone’s throw, and knelt down and prayed, saying, “Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done.”

Jesus prayed for the fathers will to be done and not his own. Why do we not do the same? Why do we continuously go through the supplication portion of our prayer and only pray for what we will?

Is he not the author of the world and mankind? Is he not the creator of all good things holy and righteous? Is he not the perfected king of all eternity? What better example to follow than the Kings of Kings or better in this case Lord of Lords?

His will is simple, Will- thelēma meaning of the purpose of God to bless mankind through Christ. Now let me ask you, not you outwardly, but let me ask your heart, Are you living by His Will?

Is my relationship with Jesus built on surrender or convenience?

2. The Display

Their defense before Christ: “Did we not…?”

Religious activity can become a substitute for genuine submission to the Father’s will. When we are proven wrong or caught in a lie we immediately go into a defensive posture.

Just as they thought the divine double title would get them accepted, now also they think works will do the same.

I can remember plenty of times I would want to go hang out with my friend Chase early in the day, but I would also have a ton of responsibilities to do around the yard and house.

I would do one or two of them hoping it would be enough to accept me doing the remaining ones the next day because I “worked so hard.” I began to put on an act toward my father.

I didn’t care about how he felt.

I didn’t care about how hard he works.

I didn’t care about his house.

This is the same thing as written in these scriptures. They outwardly show false concern for Him. Inwardly they are for their own gain and do not in all actuality care about Him or His house.

With this in mind, remember what we have just learned.

They do not care about the Father, only outwardly try and get their way, while inwardly try and prove what they do not believe to be true.

They want a lamp without a genie.

They want a shell without the turtle.

They want heaven without God.

They try so urgently to prove their non-existent faith by their works, they repeat their phrasing three times.

Pay attention!

“On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’”

In the show Young Sheldon, Sheldon read a book on how to make friends. In the book it talked about effective communication. The book wrote that repetition of someone’s name would produce care and attentive listening.

He went home to his father and started talking to him:

“Hey George, how are you George? How was work George?”

He thought that this would work to find favor with him and any future friends, just as the people that Jesus is talking about here.

Also, if you’re wondering about how it went for Sheldon, this tactic didn’t help him make a friend. They will also not make friends with God. In actuality, we see the response of our Lord.

These are on the more extreme side of the examples, but let me simplify it for you.

I go to church

I wear a cross necklace where ever i go

I pray every meal

I have my favorite verse in my social media (John 3:16)

My grandma is the most holy person i know

All of these are just some of the many “Actions” people will use to try and prove their faith in God.

Would Jesus describe me as someone who knows Him, or someone who only knows about Him?

Are my works flowing from faith, or am I using them to try to prove my faith?

3. The Destination

Then - According to what was previous stated.

I will declare - (homologeō) - to promise, to confess

Those who trust in their works rather than Christ hear the most terrifying words in Scripture: “I never knew you.” This quite simply means Separation from Christ.

GotQuestions writes, “This speaks of many people, those He “never” knew them, nor did they ever truly know Him. They weren’t Christians who lost their salvation.set apart and sanctified by the Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:11; 2 Timothy 2:21), and justified by faith (Ephesians 2:8-9). The ones Christ puts away from Himself with harsh words of condemnation, calling them doers of evil, never came to Him in faith, despite their outward good works and miracles.”

This begs the question, How can people who are not saved, how do we explain their abilities to perform miracles? Scholars promote 2 different answers, any guesses?

One is that some miracles are done by the power of Satan and his demonic host.

It’s possible that God, in His sovereign will and for His purposes, will empower unbelievers to perform miraculous deeds. An example is Judas Iscariot who, along with the other disciples, preached the gospel and we assume healed the sick and performed other miracles. There is nothing to indicate that Judas didn’t have the same power as the other eleven, although he was never a true disciple of Christ. He was a deceiver and the “son of perdition” (John 17:12). If Judas did perform miracles, it was only because God saw fit to use him for His own glory, despite the condition of Judas’s heart.

Jesus goes on to describe those who will be able to call upon His name on the day of judgment. It will be those who hear His words and put them into practice, the same ones referred to in verse 21 as those who obediently do the will of the Father in heaven.

This is the hardest part to explain to anyone at anytime. Obedience without love is obligation. On the other hand, WITH LOVE is devotion. I’m devoted to my savior, I do not work out of obligation to get into heaven. Those who believe that obligation is a key to the gate do not even know the true key. He will declare he doesn’t know them either.

True believers are the good trees that produce good fruit (Galatians 5:22-23), the true sheep who look to Christ, depend on Him, commit themselves to Him, trust in Him, and believe on Him for righteousness, salvation, and eternal life. These are the ones who will enter into the kingdom of heaven.

Am I truly known by Christ, or am I only trying to prove to Christ that I know Him?

If Christ examined my heart today, would He find surrender, or simply religious works?

Depart, workers of lawlessness.

Lawlessness - anomia - contempt and violation of law, iniquity, wickedness

They work through wickedness, Workers of wickedness and not workers of Christ. They formed a facade of a label, Christ corrected there title.

I can put on a vest and walk into a facility and might make it pretty far, but when the owner sees me he will know i do not belong to him. Just in the same way Christ knows as well. Your act or costume might fool some but not the Originator.

While they worked so hard to “Prove themselves” they couldnt do the only work of God that counts: “to have faith in the one he sent” (John 6:29, CEV). And so Jesus, the Righteous Judge, condemns them to eternal separation from Him.
