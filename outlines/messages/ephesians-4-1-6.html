Ephesians 4:1–6
The Walk

1. Godly Walking for the Body of Christ

Ephesians 4:1

“I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called…”

The word “walk” is used seven times in Ephesians:

● Ephesians 2:2
● Ephesians 2:10
● Ephesians 4:1
● Ephesians 4:17
● Ephesians 5:2
● Ephesians 5:8
● Ephesians 5:15

A walk is a picture of growth.

Human life is a walk.

Spiritual life is a walk.

For the first three chapters, Paul has been teaching doctrine and explaining what it means to be a follower of Christ.

Now he says:

Because you know these things, live them out.

Walk in a manner worthy of your calling.

A worthy walk is a walk worthy of Jesus.

And the only way to know what a walk worthy of Jesus looks like is through Scripture.

If you do not read the Word, you cannot know how to live a worthy walk. We must walk according to the spiritual knowledge we have received.

Walking Takes Work

Walking with Christ is not a situation where salvation happens and Jesus moves your legs for you.

You have a responsibility to walk.

One writer said:

“Acceptance of the gospel requires daily application. Getting in the faith by saving faith requires accepting the challenge to live out our faith.”

Too many Christians say:

“If God wants me to do it, He will do it for me.”

But God has equipped you with physical and spiritual resources to accomplish His will. We still have to walk.

We still have to work.

We still have to obey.

The Christian life is not passive.

Walking in Unity

Paul has spent the first three chapters explaining what God has done in Christ. Now he begins explaining how believers should walk.

This section focuses on unity.

So how do you not only walk with Christ but walk in unity with other believers? 1 John 1:7 says:

“But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another…”

That is the perfect combination:

Walking in the light produces fellowship with one another.

The Greek word for walk, peripateō, means:

● To walk.
● To make one's way.
● To progress.

Imagine that you decide to go to the gym and walk every day.

What would be required to continue?

Discipline and endurance.

The same is true of your spiritual walk.

If you are called to sing, you must be disciplined enough to attend practice and improve your voice.

If you are called to teach, you must study.

You must work.

You must manage your time.

You must prioritize the calling God has given you.

We often want discipleship without discipline.

Many people want someone to disciple them but do not want to work for their spiritual growth. But without discipline, your walk will begin to die.

You are either growing or dying.

There is no neutral position.

Where your priorities lack, your discipline will also lack.

God has provided the light.

You must walk in the path He has illuminated.

Remember Who You Are

Paul reminds us that we have been called by God.

This calling is:

1. A Humble Calling

Not everyone has received it.

And no one received it because they were worthy of it.

We are saved by grace.

2. A Heavenly Calling

“Holy brothers, you who share in a heavenly calling…”

— Hebrews 3:1

This calling comes from God.

God gives life to dead spirits and gives ears to hear.

3. A High Calling

“I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus.” — Philippians 3:14

4. A Holy Calling

“Who saved us and called us to a holy calling…”

— 2 Timothy 1:9

5. A Hope-Filled Calling

“One hope that belongs to your call.”

— Ephesians 4:4

All of these callings belong to the unified body of believers.

God's grace gives us a common unity in Christ.

How Do We Walk in Unity?

Ephesians 4:2–3

Paul gives us the characteristics required for unity.

Lowliness

Humility.

A person who looks to someone greater than himself—God—for direction. A person who understands that he is dependent on God.

Meekness

A humble acknowledgment of our dependence on the goodness and grace of God. Meekness is not weakness.

It is strength under control.

Long-Suffering

Patience.

Endurance.

A slowness to avenge wrongs.

A refusal to be quick to anger or judgment.

Love

The two greatest commandments:

Love God.

Love your neighbor.

Bearing With One Another

We must be willing to put up with one another.

We are not all the same.

We will disagree.

We will frustrate each other.

But unity requires patience, humility, and love.

Maintain the Unity

Ephesians 4:3

“Eager to maintain the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.” The word peace means harmony and concord.

Paul does not say:

“Create the unity of the Spirit.”

He says:

“Maintain the unity of the Spirit.”

Why?

Because believers are already united through Christ. Jesus has already created the unity.

Our responsibility is to protect it.

When you combine:

● Humility.
● Meekness.
● Patience.
● Love.
● Forbearance.

You get the characteristics necessary to maintain unity.

If believers could consistently practice these qualities toward one another, we would be able to endure one another for the sake of unity.

2. Growing Into Unity for the Body of Christ

Ephesians 4:4–6

Paul now gives us seven things beginning with the word “one.”

These seven things unite the body of Christ.

1. One Body

We share a common existence in the church of Christ.

We are diverse in background, culture, experience, and gifting.

But we are one body.

The church is called the Body of Christ because:

● We are joined to Christ in salvation.
● Christ is our Head.
● We represent Christ to the world.
● We are indwelt by the Holy Spirit.
● We have different spiritual gifts.
● We share a common bond with all believers.
● We belong to one another.
● We share in Christ's death and resurrection.
● We share Christ's inheritance.
● We receive Christ's righteousness.

The body is made of many parts.

But it is still one body.

2. One Spirit

We share a common origin in the work of the Holy Spirit. The Spirit creates unity and empowers us to maintain it. Every believer has the Spirit of God.

The Spirit allows us to know God and follow Him. This alone should unite us.

3. One Hope

We share one common hope in Christ.

Ephesians 2:12 says that we were once:

“Having no hope and without God in the world.”

But now we have hope.

Therefore, we should live in a manner worthy of our calling.

4. One Lord

We all confess:

Jesus Christ is Lord.

When early Christians said, “Jesus is Lord,” they were saying: “Caesar is not Lord.”

This was not an empty statement.

It could cost them their lives.

For Jewish Christians, calling Jesus Lord was also identifying Him with the God of the Hebrew Scriptures.

The church is united under one Lord.

Jesus Christ.

5. One Faith

We share the essential truths of Christianity.

We are not called to live without faith.

We are not called to have separate faiths.

We are united by the truth of the gospel.

6. One Baptism

We share the common experience of being spiritually united with Christ. Water baptism pictures this spiritual reality.

The external act points to the spiritual reality of being united with Christ.

7. One God and Father

As adopted children, we share the same Father.

He is God over all.

He is Father to all His children.

Regardless of ethnicity, background, culture, or nationality, we are one spiritually reborn and legally adopted family.

Unity in a Divided World

If we individually work on growing in our walk with Christ, we will be better able to live in unity with one another.

We live in a time of great division.

But our response should not be anger.

Remember:

● Be slow to anger.
● Be quick to love.
● Be quick to forgive.
● Be eager to maintain unity.

We have learned what unifies us.

Now we have to live it.

This does not mean that every Christian must agree on every subject.

The saints must agree on the essential truths of the faith:

● God.
● The Trinity.
● The deity of Christ.
● The atonement.
● The resurrection.
● The return of Jesus.

There are secondary issues that Christians can debate without dividing. For example:

Should baptism be by immersion or sprinkling?

We may disagree.

But does that disagreement change the fact that Jesus gave His life for us, died for our sins, rose from the grave, and defeated death?

The smaller categories of Christianity should not separate us into hatred. It is good for Christians to be different in certain areas.

Different churches may reach different people.

Different traditions may connect with different communities.

But we should all reach the same conclusion:

Jesus is the Son of God who died for our sins, rose from the grave, defeated death, and makes us holy and righteous before God.

There Are No Denominations in Heaven

There is no Baptist section of heaven.

There is no Pentecostal section.

There is no Methodist section.

There is no Presbyterian section.

There are only the saints of God and the presence of God.

We may disagree on certain issues here.

But we should not allow secondary issues to destroy the unity Christ purchased with His blood.

When God Redeems Division

In Acts, Paul and Barnabas had a serious disagreement over John Mark. The disagreement became so strong that they separated.

But even that division was redeemed by God.

Instead of one missionary team, there were now two.

God used the division to send more people to more places.

The disagreement did not stop the gospel.

God used it to multiply the message of Christ.

Sometimes we may disagree.

Sometimes we may even separate.

But the gospel must continue.

And God is powerful enough to redeem even our disagreements.

Main Point

Paul calls us to walk worthy of our calling.

That walk requires:

● Humility.
● Meekness.
● Patience.
● Love.
● Forbearance.

And it produces unity.

We are united because we have:

One body.

One Spirit.

One hope.

One Lord.

One faith.

One baptism.

One God and Father.

We are not united because we are all the same.

We are united because we all belong to the same Christ.

The walk of the Christian is not only about walking with Christ. It is also about walking with the body of Christ.
