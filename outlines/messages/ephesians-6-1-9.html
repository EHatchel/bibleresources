Ephesians 6:1–9
The Great Submission

Children, Fathers, Servants, and Masters

Introduction: The Household Ladder of Submission If you thought, “Surely we’re done speaking on submission after two weeks,” you’re mistaken.

As we continue through the practical “walk” section of Ephesians, Paul continues climbing down the household ladder of God’s design:

God → Husband → Wife → Children → Servants → Masters

We have already discussed the submission of wives to their husbands. Now we are met with how children are to submit to their parents. Later, Paul will address how servants are to submit to their masters and how masters are to exercise authority.

The entire section is showing us that every person is under authority, and every person is accountable to God.

1. Children: Submit to Your Parents

Verses 1–3

“Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right. ‘Honor your father and mother’ (this is the first commandment with a promise), ‘that it may go well with you and that you may live long in the land.’”

A Story of Submission

A twelve-year-old boy and his parents would travel into the big city every year for the Feast of Passover.

One year, after the feast had ended, the parents began traveling home, assuming their child was with them. However, halfway through the journey, they realized their twelve-year-old son was missing.

The parents returned to Jerusalem and searched for three days.

Eventually, they found him in the temple, listening to the teachers.

His mother ran up, relieved to see her child safe but also frustrated.

She asked:

“Why did you do this to us? Your father and I have been searching for you! We were worried!”

The child responded:

“Why were you looking for me? Did you not know that I must be in my Father’s house?”

However, instead of believing he was right and refusing to submit to his parents, the child went home with them.

Who was this child?

Jesus Christ.

Luke 2:51 says:

“And he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them.” Even though Jesus was the Son of God, He submitted Himself to His earthly parents. That is the example we are given.

“Children”

The word “children” refers to those who are descendants of a parent.

Paul begins with a direct command:

Obey your parents.

The Greek word translated “obey” is hypakouō.

It carries the idea of listening, hearing, harkening to a command, obeying, and submitting.

The word could even be used of someone who heard a knock at the door and went to find out who was there.

The idea is that a child is not merely hearing the words of a parent.

They are listening with the intention of responding.

“In the Lord”

Paul does not simply say:

“Children, obey your parents.”

He says:

“Children, obey your parents in the Lord.”

This is an important qualification.

Just as a wife submits to her husband as unto the Lord, children are to obey their parents in the Lord.

This means that children should obey their parents because, in doing so, they are honoring the Lord.

It also means that children are not required to obey commands that directly contradict the will of God.

William MacDonald writes:

“The command to obey in the Lord means first children should obey with the attitude that in doing so they are obeying the Lord: their obedience should be as if unto Him. Second, it means they should obey in all matters that are in accordance with the will of God. If their parents order them to sin, they should not be expected to comply. In such cases, they should courteously refuse and suffer the

consequences meekly and without retaliation. However in all other cases they must be obedient.”

The principle is simple:

Obey your parents in everything that is in the Lord.

Submission Is Also Respect

Children should not submit to their parents merely because they are forced to. Submission should also come from respect.

A parent makes enormous sacrifices to raise a child.

Consider the difference between life before children and life after children.

Before children, you may have more free time, more money, more freedom, more opportunities to travel, more time with friends, and more time for hobbies.

Then one day, God makes you responsible for a child.

Suddenly, your bank account may look like it was robbed. One parent may have to stop working. Sleep becomes difficult. Free time disappears. Vacations become more complicated. Vehicles change. Priorities change.

Camaros become Civics.

Mustangs become minivans.

And you can laugh at yourself with tears when you say the words “free time.” Why would someone willingly make that decision?

Look in the mirror.

You are the reason your parents made those sacrifices.

Parents sacrifice their time, money, freedom, comfort, and often their own desires so that their children can have an opportunity to grow and become something.

That is why honor should come without constant rebuke.

Your parents sacrificed much so that you could have a chance at life.

Of course, God works in ways beyond our understanding.

Jeremiah 1:5 reminds us that God knew us before we were born.

Some people never have children even though they desire them. Some people have children when they never planned to. Some children die young. Some families experience circumstances that no one understands.

Ultimately, we must trust God's plan.

But regardless of our circumstances, Scripture still commands children to honor their parents.

The Difference Between Wives and Children

There is one major difference between submitting to your husband and submitting to your parents:

You choose your husband. You do not choose your parents.

So what happens when a child has a parent who is addicted to drugs, addicted to alcohol, abusive, mentally unstable, or leading them into sin?

The answer is simple:

You do not submit to the sinful or abusive parts of their authority.

You obey in the Lord.

You are not required to obey sin.

You are not required to submit to abuse.

You are not required to participate in evil.

But outside of those things, children are called to obey their parents.

Jesus as the Example

Luke 2:51–52 says:

“And he went down with them and came to Nazareth and was submissive to them. And his mother treasured up all these things in her heart. And Jesus increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God and man.”

Jesus Christ submitted to His earthly parents.

He was wiser than them.

He was greater than them.

He was God in the flesh.

And yet He willingly submitted Himself to their authority.

That is a powerful example of humility.

Submission is not weakness.

Submission is obedience to God's design.

Honor Your Father and Mother

Paul then quotes the fifth commandment:

“Honor your father and mother.”

This command comes from Exodus 20:12 and Deuteronomy 5:16.

Jesus also affirmed this commandment in Matthew 19:17–19.

“If you would enter life, keep the commandments.”

When Jesus was asked which commandments, He included:

“Honor your father and mother.”

Paul then points out that this is:

“The first commandment with a promise.”

The promise is:

“That it may go well with you and that you may live long in the land.”

There are several possible ways to understand this promise.

1. General Well-Being and Wisdom

Children have limited experience.

They may believe they understand the world, but they have not yet experienced everything the world contains.

Parents have more experience.

They understand dangers children do not yet see.

When children rebel against their parents, they often miss out on the wisdom their parents possess.

That rebellion can lead them down paths that take them away from God and toward destruction. 2. Physical Inheritance

Another possibility is that honoring one's parents allows a person to maintain the physical blessings and inheritance passed down through a family.

This can be compared to the pattern seen throughout Israel's history.

As later generations turned away from God and rejected the wisdom of those who came before them, they often lost the blessings God had given their ancestors.

3. Spiritual Inheritance

The promise may also refer to spiritual life and blessing.

The child who learns to humble himself and submit to godly authority is learning the same pattern of submission that is necessary in his relationship with God.

My understanding is that this promise may involve all three: general well-being, physical blessings and inheritance, and spiritual growth.

When a person humbles himself and honors his parents within the will of God, he grows in wisdom and learns how to submit to authority.

What Makes a Good Child in God's Eyes?

A good child is submissive and humble.

2. Fathers: Lead Without Provoking

Verse 4

“Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.”

Paul now turns to fathers.

Why Does Paul Specifically Address Fathers?

My opinion is that this connects directly to the structure Paul has already established in Ephesians 5.

The husband and father has been given the responsibility to lead his household under God. This does not mean the mother has no role.

Proverbs 1:8 says:

“Hear, my son, your father’s instruction, and do not forsake your mother’s teaching.” Both parents teach.

However, the father has been given the responsibility to lead his home spiritually. The father is called to lead his wife and children toward the Lord.

Do Not Provoke Your Children

The Greek word translated “provoke” is parorgizō.

It means to rouse to wrath, provoke, exasperate, and anger.

Paul is not saying:

“Fathers, never discipline your children.”

Discipline is necessary.

It is better for a father to discipline his child on earth than for his child to face the consequences of rebellion later in life.

The issue is the kind of anger being produced.

A father should not discipline out of uncontrolled anger, selfish frustration, cruelty, inconsistency, abuse, or a desire to control.

A father should discipline in a way that reflects the character of God.

During the time Paul wrote Ephesians, fathers had enormous authority over their children.

Roman fathers could exercise extreme control over their families.

Paul's command was therefore radically different from the culture around him. Fathers were not merely given authority over their children.

They were given responsibility for their children.

The Danger of Fatherlessness

The absence of a father can have devastating consequences.

Children need fathers who are present, involved, and spiritually responsible. A father is called to provide more than money.

He is called to provide physical care, emotional care, spiritual leadership, discipline, instruction, encouragement, and time.

Jesus warned strongly against causing children to stumble.

Matthew 18:5–6 says:

“Whoever receives one such child in my name receives me, but whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him to have a great millstone fastened around his neck and to be drowned in the depth of the sea.”

A father must remember that his leadership affects the spiritual direction of his family.

Discipline and Instruction of the Lord

Paul says:

“Bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.”

Notice the connection.

Children are commanded to obey their parents in the Lord.

Fathers are commanded to raise their children in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

God is working at both ends.

Without obedient surrender, disciplined instruction is not received.

Without disciplined instruction, there is little for children to gain from spiritually obedient surrender.

What Makes a Good Father in God's Eyes?

A good father leads his children to depend on the Lord rather than himself, provides for his family physically, emotionally, and spiritually, makes time for his family, understands that God is still working on him, teaches his family the Word of God, and encourages his family toward Christ.

A father does not have to be perfect.

If God required perfection before allowing someone to lead, no one would ever be qualified.

Instead, the father must learn to depend on God rather than his own strength and understanding.

The responsibility of leading a family should drive a man toward the Lord.

3. Servants and Workers: Serve as to Christ

Verses 5–7

“Bondservants, obey your earthly masters with fear and trembling, with a sincere heart, as you would Christ, not by the way of eye-service, as people-pleasers, but as bondservants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart, rendering service with a good will as to the Lord and not to man.”

When we hear the word “slave,” we often think only of modern slavery.

However, we must understand the historical context of Ephesians.

During the first century, many different types of workers were considered slaves or bondservants.

Doctors, teachers, secretaries, and other highly respected professions could be classified within the servant system of the Roman Empire.

There were also people who were forced into brutal labor and slavery.

The point is that the word applied to a wide range of people who served others.

In a modern application, anyone who works for another person should recognize the principle of serving faithfully under authority.

“With Fear and Trembling”

Why does Paul say servants should obey with fear and trembling?

The answer is not that Christians should live in constant terror of their employers. Rather, it speaks of taking our responsibilities seriously.

We are called to be faithful wherever God has placed us.

Jesus demonstrated this principle when He said:

“Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's.”

Our ultimate freedom is not found in our earthly position.

Our ultimate freedom is found in Christ.

What Is Eye-Service?

Eye-service is work that is performed only when someone is watching.

It is essentially:

Working when the boss is present and loafing when the boss leaves. It is service performed only for appearance.

The modern phrase might be:

“Brown-nosing.”

A Christian should not work hard merely to impress people.

A Christian should work faithfully because God sees everything.

What Is People-Pleasing?

People-pleasing is doing something merely to gain the approval of others. It can even mean sacrificing principle in order to please someone with authority. A Christian should not butter up the boss.

A Christian should not pretend to work hard only when someone is watching. The Christian should work faithfully because the true Master is Christ.

Serve from the Heart

Paul says:

“As bondservants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart.”

Every person is a servant of something.

The question is:

What are you serving?

A bondservant had been purchased by a price.

Christ has purchased us with a price that we could never repay.

If we genuinely understand what Christ has done for us, we should desire to serve Him.

Jesus spent His earthly ministry serving others by washing feet, healing the sick, comforting the brokenhearted, and guiding the lost.

Faithful work, wherever God has placed you, can become part of your service to Him.

4. The Reward of Faithful Submission

Verse 8

“Knowing that whatever good anyone does, this he will receive back from the Lord, whether he is a bondservant or is free.”

God sees everything.

He sees what happens in public.

He sees what happens in secret.

He sees the actions no one else sees.

He sees our motives.

He sees our attitudes.

He sees the reason behind what we do.

Psalm 139:2 reminds us that God knows our thoughts from afar.

Proverbs 16:3 says:

“Commit your work to the Lord, and your plans will be established.” So what does it mean that we will “receive back” from the Lord?

There are several possibilities.

1. Heavenly Reward

Faithful work on earth may result in reward from the Lord in eternity. Colossians 3:24 says:

“Knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward.” 2. Earthly Blessings

This does not mean that everyone who works faithfully will receive an F-250 Platinum. The promise is not that faithful service guarantees material wealth.

However, God may use our faithfulness to create new opportunities for ministry and service. 3. Advancement for the Kingdom

Joseph is a good example.

Joseph served faithfully even in terrible circumstances.

God eventually elevated him to a position of authority and used him to save many lives. Faithfulness in difficult circumstances does not go unnoticed by God.

Perhaps the reward includes all of these things.

Regardless, the principle is clear:

God sees faithful submission.

5. Masters and Bosses: Authority Comes with Accountability

Verse 9

“Masters, do the same to them, and stop your threatening, knowing that he who is both their Master and yours is in heaven, and that there is no partiality with him.”

Servants receive several verses of instruction.

Masters receive instruction as well.

The message is clear:

All authority comes with accountability.

A master or leader is simply a servant with greater responsibility.

If God places you over His people, do not make Him regret giving you that responsibility. A position of authority does not make you more valuable than the people beneath you. It makes you more accountable.

This principle appears throughout Scripture.

Teachers receive stricter judgment.

Husbands are responsible for leading their wives.

Fathers are responsible for leading their children.

Masters are responsible for how they treat their servants.

A job title means absolutely nothing when we stand before God.

What matters is what we did with the position God gave us.

You can bring your resume before God and show Him all your credentials. But God already knows the real credentials.

The question is not:

“What position did you have?”

The question is:

“How did you use the position God gave you?”

The Great Submission

Children submit to parents.

Wives submit to husbands.

Husbands submit to Christ.

Servants submit to masters.

Masters are accountable to God.

And ultimately, every person will submit to the true Master.

Philippians 2:10 says:

“So that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth.”

Everyone will bow: husbands, wives, fathers, mothers, children, masters, servants, believers, and unbelievers.

The question is not whether you will submit.

The question is who you will submit to now.

Main Point

God has designed every relationship with authority and submission, and every person—whether child, parent, servant, master, employee, employer, husband, or wife—is ultimately called to submit to the authority of Christ.

The world says:

“No one can tell me what to do.”

The Christian says:

“I belong to Christ.”

And if we truly belong to Christ, then we should be willing to submit wherever God has placed us.

Not because submission makes us less valuable.

But because submission to God is the path to becoming more like Christ.
