Colossians 1:7-14
Prayer to Walk Worthy

The Source – v. 7-9a

Paul introduces Epaphras, who is with him. Calling him a faithful follower. He labels him this because He was a part of the Colossian Church. He was realizing all of the division in the church from a bunch of different beliefs and knew he needed guidance. He left his home and church to travel over 1300 Miles to seek guidance from Paul. With Paul hearing all that had been happening, I guess he decided it was letter-worthy.

Spurgeon - Epaphras told them of Paul’s prayers for them; and when he came back from Colosse, he told Paul of their great love in the Spirit....I like to read of these godly men speaking well of one another. Nowadays, it is thought to be a distinguishing mark of faithfulness to be able to pick holes in the coats of our fellow-Christians. Now, we cannot help perceiving their defects, and sometimes it is our duty to speak of them, and to speak of them faithfully, but let us also observe all the virtues that are to be found in them; otherwise we may despise the work of the Holy Spirit, and rob him of his glory. How kindly Paul speaks of Epaphras, and how kindly Epaphras speaks of the church at Colossae!

did not simply lead the Colossians to Christ and then abandon them. He taught them the Word and sought to establish their faith. In short, he "discipled" them.

Every church needs an "Epaphras" to disciple the new converts, lest they "be carried away by varied and strange teachings"

Are you praying for your disciples? Do you have any disciples? Are you disobeying Jesus' great command to make disciples?

Epaphras meets with Paul for prayer over the Colossians.

They begin to pray without ceasing.

What does prayer without ceasing look like?

Keathley - The faithful person is a person who is also full of faith. That which makes a person faithful is his or her trust and faith in the Lord and His sovereignty, love, provision, and support. A man or woman of faith is faithful because he or she is resting in God’s sovereignty and knows their work is never fruitless

On...behalf of (huper) means in place of or as a substitute for. [Epaphras]() was Paul’s representative at Colossae, backed by his authority and that of the Lord Jesus. While Paul was imprisoned, unable to go to the Colossians, Epaphras ministered to the Colossians on Paul’s behalf. This pattern reminds one of Paul's exhortation to Timothy

2 Timothy 2:2
The things which you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses, entrust these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also.

Praying for their understanding of the will of the Lord. Again, as a refresher, we spoke about knowing the will of the Father, to know his desires. In order to know the will, we must know the Father. Once we know him, then we can begin to discern what his will is for our life.

V.9b-11

Paul and Epaphras continue in their continual prayer for the church of Colossians. In fact, they prayed so boldly they prayed for all wisdom and knowledge would be revealed to them.

Wisdom and understanding - the first great request is to know God's will. But note exactly what Scripture says: we are to be filled with the knowledge of God's will. It is not enough to just know God's will; we are to be filled with the knowledge of His will.

Worthy walk pleasing to God - This brings to my attention Ephesians 4:1 1I therefore, a prisoner for the Lord, urge you to walk in a manner worthy of the calling to which you have been called. Walk worthy to which you have been called; now walk worthy of the calling to please the lord.

The list of how to walk pleasing to him. Bear fruit from good works. What does bearing fruit look like? Sanctified in the lord. Good works for the kingdom of God through the Holy Spirit produce love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, and self-control. Reliance on him because we cannot be good without him.

Increasing in the knowledge of the Lord. Studying Scripture, prayer for wisdom and understanding.

Possess the Power of God - Many in the world believe that man has the strength within himself to become spiritually strong; that it is a matter of the will and discipline; that man can apply himself and conquer the circumstances of life. And, to some degree, this is true. But man's flesh fails in three critical areas. James 1:5

2 Questions about Man and God's Power from LMW Commentary.

What good is it if the believer knows God's will, but he does not have the power to do God's will?

How can the believer walk worthy of Christ if he does not have the power to walk worthy?

Man’s flesh cannot be perfect- our flesh will always be unacceptable to God. By knowing what God's word says. God is perfect, without blemish. So, in that we know he can only accept perfection.

Man's flesh cannot conquer death. Man can try anything to live longer: exercise, diets, medicines, etc., but none of them will permit man to conquer death.

It is for these three reasons that we need the power of God. Man's power can overcome some of the problems of life, but his power cannot overcome all the problems, especially the three mentioned.

Only God's power can make us acceptable (perfect) before Him.

Only God's power can conquer death, that is, raise us up to live eternally.

God's power can only be secure through his graciousness that he pours out on us and our prayers. God's power gives us a spirit of patience (hupomonēn): endurance, fortitude, steadfastness, constancy, perseverance. The word patience is not passive; it is active. It is not the spirit that just sits back and puts up with the trials of life, taking whatever may come. Rather, it is the spirit that stands up and faces the trials of life, that actively goes about conquering and overcoming them. Paul knew this truth so intimately. He would be in prayer always without ceasing. Now here he is praying to a church he has never met, but yet loves them so much to ask God to provide his power to man.

Precept Austin writes:

“When we become ungrateful, we begin to complain and look at people and circumstances with a critical spirit. That's never happened to you, has it? As someone has well said, "Don't complain about thorns among the roses! Be grateful for roses among the thorns!" Paul said it even better exhorting us to “In everything give thanks; for this is God's will for you in Christ Jesus” (-). Note that Paul is not exhorting us to give thanks for everything but in everything. For example, Job could give thanks even after losing all his possessions and even his children (,,). V.13

God Has Given Us an Inheritance

Domain of darkness - Domain is a territory owned by a ruler. We have chosen to be roommates with the enemy while we are here on earth. Trusting in Christ gives us hope for a future where we will be delivered to the kingdom of his beloved son. The bridge was made through him on the cross. He ransomed his life so we may live in eternity with him.

Matthew 6:19-20

We must be prepared for the inheritance.

“Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal, but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves do not break in and steal.'

You get what you gave. As far as salvation and God giving grace, there is no work needed to be done. The work you did faithfully for the kingdom is the inheritance and treasure you will receive in Glory. On an unrelated note, your job in heaven depends on this as well. This does not depend on the magnitude of the gift. If your pastor hates his gift and slouches his calling, yet you sweep and do so full of joy and faithfulness, you used your gift greater than his and in return would store up more treasure; Scripture points to it, but does not say this directly.

We must share our inheritance with fellow saints.

Remember, saints (hagios) simply means those who have set their lives apart to live for God (see note, pt.2-Col. 1:2). God is Light; therefore, when a person turns his life over to God, he is turning his life over to Light. He is to live and move and have his being in light, that is, in God. He is to walk in the light of God Himself, so much so that he, the believer, is called the light of the world. This is what is meant by "saints in light." They are people who have committed their lives to walk in the light of God. Therefore, they are saints, persons set apart in the light of God.

1 Peter 1:4: to an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you,

Colossians 3:24: knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ.
