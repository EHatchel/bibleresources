1 Timothy 4:6-10
The Training of a Godly Servant

Point 1: The Training of the Servant (v.6–7)

“These things”

These things? Good servant — Matthew 25:21.

We are saved by grace through faith (Ephesians 2:8–9), but we are saved “to do good works” (Ephesians 2:10). Jesus spoke of laying up treasures in heaven (Matthew 6:20), and His parable of the talents hints at various rewards for those who faithfully serve Him in this world.

To hear those words, “Well done, good and faithful servant,” from Jesus, first make sure you are saved. The unbelieving will never hear those words, for “without faith it is impossible to please God” (Hebrews 11:6). And recognize that Jesus is not only your Savior; He is also your Lord (see Luke 6:46). “Serve the LORD with gladness!” (Psalm 100:2, ESV).

“Being trained”

What does training look life in our life ?

Sanctification is not a one time step, it is not a weekend course and you get the certificate. It is training your mind and your spiritual mind everyday for all your life.

There are a few training types in this that Paul highlights:

The words of the faith —

This refers to the truths of the Christian faith—the message Timothy has received and is teaching to the church. Timothy is to be continually nourished by the truth of God's Word.

The servant of Christ cannot effectively feed others if he isn't continually feeding himself on the truth.

How often are you feeding yourself ? When we try and go out and feed others with empty hands. We are unfruitful and ineffective.

The good doctrine —

“Good doctrine” is sound, healthy teaching. Paul is contrasting this with the false teaching and myths mentioned in the surrounding verses.

Doctrine isn't merely information we collect. Good doctrine produces good living. What we believe shapes how we live.

That you have followed —

Timothy had already been following this doctrine. Paul is emphasizing that Timothy's teaching wasn't supposed to be disconnected from his life.

He was to teach what he believed and live what he taught.

Learn it → Follow it → Teach it

Verse 7

“What silly myths is Paul referring to?”

Paul is referring to worthless religious speculation and fabricated stories that were distracting people from the truth of the gospel.

Have you ever heard something or had a conversation that was a silly myth or fabricated stories? ( church or spiritual situation)

(Book of Enoch)

The important point isn't necessarily identifying every specific myth. Paul doesn't spend time explaining them because they're not worth the attention.

Don't waste your spiritual energy on worthless things.

“Then, Rather, Another training — Godliness”

Instead:

Train yourself for godliness.

The picture is of deliberate, disciplined training.

Just as an athlete doesn't become strong by accident, a Christian doesn't become godly by accident.

Spiritual growth requires intentional participation:

Prayer. God's Word. Obedience. Discipline. Endurance. Consistency.

Sanctification is a lifelong process of becoming more like Christ.

Where in the Bible was the term Christian coined ?

Point 2: The Value of the Training (v.8–9)

With this training of the mind, Paul must bring up training of the body. So it is not neglected, Paul brings up its importance.

“Bodily training is of some value”

Paul isn't condemning physical exercise. He actually acknowledges that it has some value. The problem is that its value is temporary and limited. You can train your body for years, but eventually your physical body will pass away.

Your body is a temple — 1 Corinthians 3:16-17 ' Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you? If anyone destroys God’s temple, God will destroy him. For God’s temple is holy, and you are that temple. '

“Godliness is of value in every way”

Godliness benefits the believer in every way because it affects both this life and eternity. Physical training has temporary benefits.

Godliness has eternal benefits.

This doesn't mean godliness guarantees health, wealth, comfort, or an easy life. It means that living according to God's will has value that extends beyond our temporary circumstances.

Value definition —

The idea is a benefit, usefulness, or advantage.

Paul's point is that godliness isn't merely something that makes you look religious. It produces genuine benefit in every aspect of life and continues to have value beyond this life.

“Promise”

The word carries the idea of an announcement or assurance of something beneficial that is to come.

Paul's point is that godliness isn't empty effort. God has attached His promises to the life of faithfulness.

Present and future — valuable promise —

There are benefits to godliness now and eternally.

Present life:

A closer relationship with God. Wisdom for living. Spiritual maturity. Greater usefulness to God. The ability to withstand temptation. A life that reflects Christ.

Life to come:

Eternal life. Eternal reward. Being with Christ forever.

So Paul's comparison is:

Physical training → temporary benefit

Godliness → present benefit + eternal benefit

Verse 9

“The saying is trustworthy and deserving of full acceptance.”

Remember Paul saying this previously to provide emphasis of truth. Paul uses this phrase to emphasize the reliability and importance of what he has just said. This isn't just an interesting thought. This is a truth you can trust and fully accept.

The truth being emphasized is that godliness has value both in this present life and in the life to come.

Not just for you as well , but who you influence around you and who is watching you.

Can anyone share a time in their life when they realized someone was watching their godliness without them knowing?

Point 3: The Hope Behind the Training (v.10)

“For to this end we toil and strive, because we have our hope set on the living God, who is the Savior of all people, especially of those who believe.”

“To this end”

To what end?

To the purpose of pursuing godliness and living according to the truth Paul has just described. Their work has a purpose. They aren't simply working hard for the sake of working hard.

“Toil and strive”

Toil carries the idea of laboring to the point of weariness. Strive carries the idea of struggling, exerting effort, or putting forth great effort. Paul is showing that following Christ isn't passive. There is labor involved in the Christian life.

“Because we have our hope set on the living God”

Where does our toil derive from ? Does it derive from worldly toil or spiritual toil?

This is the motivation behind the toil.

Paul doesn't say:

We toil and strive so that we can earn God's love.

He says:

We toil and strive because our hope is already set on God.

Hope produces endurance.

Because they know who God is and what He has promised, they're willing to labor now.

“The Savior of all people, especially of those who believe”

This doesn't mean that every person will automatically receive eternal salvation.

The phrase “especially of those who believe” makes the distinction clear. God is the Savior and source of salvation for humanity, but those who experience salvation are those who believe in Him. The emphasis is that salvation is found in the living God, and believers have placed their hope in Him.

Toil — Ecclesiastes 1

All man will toil all the days of their life.

Ecclesiastes describes the continual labor of mankind. People work, labor, strive, build, acquire, and pursue things throughout their lives.

But Solomon repeatedly asks what lasting value those things have when they are pursued apart from God.

There is a way that it will not be vanity.

Everyone toils.

Ecclesiastes shows the emptiness of labor when life is lived under the sun without God.

Paul shows us labor that isn't ultimately empty because our hope is set on the living God.

Everyone will spend their life striving for something. The Christian's labor is not vanity because his hope is not in what he accomplishes, but in the living God.

The Progression: Be nourished by the truth → reject worthless teaching → train yourself for godliness → recognize its eternal value → toil and strive → because your hope is in the living God.

Main Point -

Paul teaches the church that godliness isn’t fulfilled instantly but rather takes training. Bodily training is important but is temporary while Godly training is eternal and promised for those who put their hope in God.
