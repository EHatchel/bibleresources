Ephesians 6:16–24
The Battle Intensifies

1. Suit Up — Verses 16–17

The Shield of Faith — Verse 16

“In all circumstances take up the shield of faith, with which you can extinguish all the flaming darts of the evil one.”

Take Up

We have to choose to lift it up.

It is us who have to make the choice to use the faith that was given to us. Make the choice to be obedient in faith.

The Shield of Faith

The shield is used to protect and extinguish.

It protects against the flaming darts of the evil one.

The Roman Shield — Literal

The Roman shield was a large, oblong, four-cornered shield. It was approximately four feet by two and a half feet and was sometimes curved on the inner side.

It was large enough to cover the other pieces of armor and allowed the soldier to stand behind it fully protected.

The Christian soldier's shield is faith—a faith in God's trustworthy Word and in the One who is named Faithful and True, the Lord Jesus.

A faith firmly grounded on the Rock provides a sure defense against the fiery missiles of the evil one.

Before battle, Roman soldiers would wet the leather covering of their shields with water so that the flaming arrows could be extinguished.

The Roman legionaries could also close ranks with their shields. The first row would hold their shields edge to edge in front of them, while the rows behind would hold their shields above their heads.

In this formation, they were practically invulnerable to arrows, rocks, and even spears. The Shield of Faith — Spiritual

Faith is the believer's expression of trust in God's promises and truth.

In other words:

Faith is belief in God and His Word.

The attitude of obedience is the faith that protects you against the enemy. The faith that we possess is a key piece in protecting our whole body.

The flaming arrows cover the skies in an all-out attack.

The enemy wants to take out, distract, and discourage the believer.

Faith keeps us grounded.

Faith keeps us in the fight.

Faith relieves our worries because we know we are fully covered.

Faith Protects the Whole Man

Faith protects the mind from deception.

Let Satan attack the head.

Let him try to deceive us with unsettled ideas.

Let him tempt us to doubt the things we have received.

A full faith in Christ protects us against dangerous heresies and enables us to hold fast to the truth of God.

Unsettledness generally springs from a weakness of faith.

A man who has strong faith in Christ has a grip on the doctrines of grace that cannot easily be broken.

He knows what he has believed.

He understands what he has received.

He will not give up what he knows to be the truth of God.

Faith also guards the heart.

When the temptation to love the world comes, faith holds up the thoughts of the future and the confidence of the reward that awaits the people of God.

Faith protects the whole man.

The Helmet of Salvation — Verse 17

“And take the helmet of salvation...”

The Roman Helmet — Literal

Roman military helmets were made from leather or metal.

The helmet had a band to protect the forehead, plates for the cheeks, and protection extending down the back to protect the neck.

When strapped in place, it exposed little besides the eyes, nose, and mouth. Metal helmets were lined with sponge or felt because of their weight.

Virtually the only weapons capable of penetrating a metal helmet were hammers or axes. No soldier's uniform was complete without a proper helmet.

A helmet was hot and uncomfortable, so a soldier would usually put it on when he faced impending danger.

A Roman soldier who lost his helmet was in danger of receiving severe head wounds. At the very least, a head wound could disorient him and render him ineffective. It would also leave him vulnerable to further injury.

The Helmet of Salvation — Spiritual

Paul is speaking to those who are already saved.

Therefore, he is not speaking here about attaining salvation.

Rather, Satan seeks to destroy a believer's assurance of salvation through doubt and discouragement.

Security is a fact. Assurance is a feeling that comes to the obedient Christian. The helmet protects the mind and sight.

Throughout Scripture, Satan is constantly trying to find a way to blind the believer. The blinding causes you to hear only what the deceiver wants you to hear. You have to stand firm in your salvation.

The enemy wants to attack the mind.

The enemy wants to attack what you see.

The enemy wants to make you doubt what God has already done.

Stand firm in your salvation.

The Sword of the Spirit — Verse 17 “And the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.”

The Roman Sword — Literal

The Roman sword was relatively short—almost like a dagger.

It was used for cutting and stabbing.

It was extremely difficult to approach a soldier who was well-trained in the use of this sword because it could be moved rapidly.

Because it was two-edged, the soldier could strike on either side without changing the position of the sword in his hand.

Its razor-sharp point could pierce armor.

Proper use of Scripture in spiritual warfare enables the Christian to stand against the wiles of the devil.

The Sword of the Spirit — Spiritual

This is the sword provided by the Spirit.

It is the Word of God.

To Protect Against

A life separated from God where you draw your strength from yourself. To Attack Against

The evil one.

What if instead of viewing the Bible as a storybook, we viewed it as a sword? What if we made sure to keep it sharp?

What if we prepared for battle by sharpening our minds and storing God's Word in our hearts? Hebrews 4:12

“For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword...” Our sword is much sharper and stronger because of who it is supplied from. The Word of God is Christ's living and active sword.

With this knowledge, there is no excuse not to pick it up and put it to use. This is the only piece of armor listed that is used for attacking rather than simply defending.

In order to do what Paul commands—to stand firm—we must attack in order to stand firm and take ground to hold.

The Christian is not only called to survive the battle. The Christian is called to advance. 2. The Prayer Warrior — Verses 18–24

Prayer and Supplication — Verse 18

“Praying at all times in the Spirit, with all prayer and supplication.”

Philippians 4:6

“Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.”

The important notes following all of these armor pieces bring awareness to the believer. There is a war being waged around us.

We must be fully equipped with the armor of God.

But the thing is, this armor is not crafted by us.

You are not in a smithy preparing your pieces.

You are not putting in hours upon hours of hard work stitching, melting, and bending metal. The Lord provides all of these pieces.

Paul refreshes us in this verse.

We need to be in constant prayer.

If the Lord is providing this armor, what happens when we become separated or distant from the One who provides it?

We lose sight of the armor that is to be used in this battle.

We need to be in constant prayer with the Lord:

● To remain close to our Provider.
● To remember.
● To be strengthened.
● To build up courage.
● To remain bold.
● To ensure victory.

What is the chance of a timid, non-confident soldier being victorious?

Slim to none.

But what are the chances of a strong and bold soldier being victorious?

Very likely.

Without constant prayer to our Father, we are susceptible to a gruesome defeat. On the contrary, when we remain close to God, we are always victorious through Him. Prayer in Every Circumstance

Prayer is now specifically commanded in a great variety of ways.

All prayer and supplication includes every form of prayer:

● Secret prayer.
● Spoken prayer.
● Domestic prayer.
● Social prayer.
● Congregational prayer.

And it is to happen at all seasons.

No period of life should be without prayer:

● Youth.
● Middle life.
● Old age.

No condition of life should be without prayer:

● Adversity.
● Prosperity.
● Sunshine.
● Desolation.
● Temptation.
● Important duty.
● Heavy trial.
● Personal circumstances.
● Social circumstances.
● Christian circumstances.

Every season requires prayer.

Pray for the Messenger — Verses 19–20

“And also for me, that words may be given to me in opening my mouth boldly to proclaim the mystery of the gospel...”

Paul asks the church to pray that he would have:

● The words to speak.
● The ability to speak.
● The boldness to speak.

Paul is asking for liberation—not from his imprisonment, but for his tongue. He wants freedom to speak.

The Mystery Revealed

Paul brings up the reason for his imprisonment again.

He has been teaching the gospel of Jesus Christ and the reason for His coming. Christ came to unite Jews and Gentiles.

Christ came for all.

This truth had caused Paul's imprisonment.

And yet, all Paul wants to do is continue teaching this truth boldly.

He is asking the church to be faithful in prayer.

Pray for his freedom of tongue.

Pray for his boldness to share the truth.

Ambassador

An ambassador is an official representative of a higher authority, such as a king or government. Paul is an ambassador for Christ.

He is representing the authority of Christ.

Even though he is imprisoned, he still has a message to deliver.

He is still called to speak.

He is still called to be bold.

Chains could imprison Paul's body, but they could not imprison the gospel.

Final Greetings — Verses 21–24

Who Is Tychicus?

Paul describes Tychicus as:

● A beloved brother.
● A faithful servant.
● A fellow bond-servant in the Lord.

Tychicus was sent to the Ephesians so they could know Paul's circumstances and so that he could encourage their hearts.

Paul did not simply send information.

He sent encouragement.

The Christian life is not meant to be lived alone.

The church needs faithful brothers and sisters who can:

● Encourage.
● Serve.
● Carry messages.
● Strengthen others.
● Remain faithful in the Lord.

The Battle Intensifies

As Paul closes Ephesians 6, the battle intensifies.

The Christian soldier must:

● Take up the shield of faith.
● Protect the mind with the helmet of salvation.
● Take up the sword of the Spirit.
● Pray at all times.
● Pray for other believers.
● Pray for boldness.
● Remain faithful.
● Encourage one another.

The armor is not designed to sit in a closet.

The sword is not designed to remain in its sheath.

The shield is not designed to be left on the ground.

The helmet is not designed to be carried under your arm.

Suit up.

Stand firm.

Take up your sword.

Pray.

Advance.

Reflection

● What can you do to know God's Word well enough to wield it as a weapon? ● How can you better prepare yourself for spiritual battle?
● Are you using the armor God has provided, or are you attempting to fight in your own strength?
● Are you praying at all times and remaining close to the One who provides the armor? Conclusion

The Christian life is a battle.

But we have not been sent into battle without protection.

God provides the armor.

God provides the strength.

God provides the weapon.

God provides the victory.

So take up the shield of faith.

Put on the helmet of salvation.

Take up the sword of the Spirit.

Pray at all times.

Stand firm.

And continue to advance the gospel of Jesus Christ. The Battle Intensifies.
