Point 2: Behold (v.4-7)

The sins: They seduced & controlled others through lust, power, wealth, religion, sorcery, & witchcraft

b. The judgment of God

1) He spoke out against them & all they stood for

2) He would expose Assyria’s naked & shameful behavior to the world 3) He would cover them with filth & contempt: Make them an unusual public display 4) He would destroy Nineveh (the Assyrian Empire): No one would mourn for her

LMW states, “Nineveh fell because of lust, idolatry, sorcery, and witchcraft. In the Bible, prostitution, harlotry, and adultery refer both to acts of illicit sex and to spiritual adultery. The citizens of Nineveh were guilty of committing both. Note the Scripture and outline: Since the days of Jonah, the Assyrians had turned away from the Lord and given themselves to false gods.

Their worship and devotion were focused upon the so-called gods of this world, the idols created in people’s imaginations. The Assyrians were also guilty of worshipping power and wealth. Politically, they used their power and wealth to seduce other nations into unholy relations or treaties with them. Once the nations had taken the bait and united with the Assyrians, they were doomed, for the Assyrian rulers subjected the nations to their own desires and lusts.

Furthermore, the Assyrians used their religion, in particular their sorcery and witchcraft, to attract and entice other nations. After all, the Assyrians gave credit for their power and wealth to their so-called gods. In their minds, it was their gods who blessed them with victory and wealth.”

Their sins made them attractive to other nations, God would use this against them as a public display to expose them.

Gods punishments came in 4 different manners,

1. The second time he told them that he was against them and everything he stood for.

2. God declared that He would expose Assyria’s naked and shameful behavior to the world.

3. He would fill them with filth, exposing them to the large -scale public humiliation. 4. He would destroy them and no one would mourn them.

Lord of Hosts will humiliate you . Once great, now pit of despair ( related to Jonah).Nineveh, once proud and feared, will be publicly exposed and humiliated.

The same city that shamed others will now be shamed.

Sin that is hidden now will be exposed later. What you cover, God will uncover, unless you bring it to Him first.

Point 3: Pride (v8-15)

Who is Thebes?

The immediate context speaks of the defeat of the Egyptian city of Thebes by Assyria, of which Nineveh was the capital. When Thebes was defeated by Assyria in 663 B.C., the detestable acts of Nahum 3:10 took place. The Assyrians sold people into captivity and killed infants (cf. Hosea 13:16). The infants were likely killed by the Assyrians as a gratuitous act of cruelty and because the infants could not be easily exiled.

It’s important to note that God did not condone this horrific action. In fact, Nahum mentions this account as justification for God’s condemnation of Assyria. God expresses His intent to soon judge Assyria by predicting the violent destruction of the Assyrian capital of Nineveh.

If they can't escape judgment, what makes you think you will? Pride blinds people to warning signs.

Pride says, It won’t happen to me, until it does. Don’t ignore the examples God has already given.

Consequently, what Assyria sowed, she would now reap. Her mistreatment of others would now be turned back upon the heads of her own people. The Assyrians would suffer the same punishment they had inflicted upon others.God would make them helpless from

Babylon's wrath, just as they did to other nations. Next there fortress would be taken easily as fruit falls from a fig tree. Continuing, Assyria’s troops would be like feeble women, completely unable to defend the nation or the gates of Nineveh or the other cities of the empire. In fact, the Assyrians would offer so little resistance it would be as though the gates of the land were thrown wide open to the invaders. Then showing them that their preparations would be useless. Then, Nineveh would be set ablaze and the people would be slaughtered, like grasshoppers.

This is a warning of pride and viewing ourselves as superior to others. We know all throughout scripture we are called to be humble and lowly. This should be a trait of every believer. To end This book,

Waylon Bailey says this in the New American Commentary:

Those who read the Book of Nahum should not miss the point that God cares for the weak and needy of the world and is working in history to correct oppression. Let us make sure that we recognize God’s work and seek to correct oppression and participate in the work of God in his world. For us who read the Book of Nahum today, a strong message comes through. We can easily stand with Nahum and point the finger of guilt at our enemies. We quickly volunteer for God’s army, hardly able to wait for his call to battle against the hated enemy. God calls us to another listening post as we read Nahum. He calls us to stand with the court of the king of Nineveh and listen to God’s description of who we are in his sight. He calls us to take off our battle uniforms and watch God at work. “We must never forget that the whole Book of Nahum is a celebration of divine, not human, action. Nahum leaves vengeance in the hands of God.” (Achtemeire, Nahum-Malachi, 29) So must we. When we listen to Nahum from this vantage point, something entirely unexpected happens. The hymn of hate is directed against us. We stand in the tension between the God who is full of wrath and yet good and slow to anger. We come to see that God is calling us to bear our cross, yes even to Calvary and death. We are not only “to resist evil, not only to correct it, but also sometimes simply to suffer it, confident in the assurance that God will finally cleanse his earth of all corruption.” Then for us Nahum becomes more than anything else a great call to repentance.*
