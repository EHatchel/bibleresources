Ephesians 5:22–24
Wives

Even though this passage is specifically addressing marriage, there is something for all of us to learn because the larger theme of this passage is not simply marriage. Paul’s broader theological concern is the Spirit-filled life.

The general principle is found in Ephesians 5:18:

“Be filled with the Spirit.”

Paul then explains several ways this Spirit-filled life should be lived out. One of those applications is how Christian husbands and wives are to relate to one another.

Dustin Kitchens writes:

“If we walk down the path God has laid for us—if we do just what He says—we experience the most loving and peaceful, the richest and fullest life imaginable. This is definitely true for husband and wife, for they have the companionship of each other as well as the Lord.”

Paul begins with the wife.

1. Submission Is a Spiritual Act of Worship

Verse 22

“Wives, submit to your own husbands, as to the Lord.”

The word submit means to surrender or willingly place oneself under another's authority.

However, submission does not mean inferiority. Submission does not mean silence. Submission does not mean that a woman is less intelligent, less valuable, or less capable than her husband.

Submission means placing oneself under a God-given mission.

David Guzik explains:

“Submission does not mean inferiority. As well, submission does not mean silence. Submission means ‘sub-mission.’ There is a mission for the Christian marriage, and that mission is obeying and glorifying God. The wife says, ‘I’m going to put myself under that mission. That mission is more important than my individual desires. I’m not putting myself below my husband, I’m putting myself below the mission God has for our marriage, for my life.’”

The purpose of Christian marriage is not simply for two people to get what they want. The purpose of Christian marriage is to glorify God.

Therefore, the wife is called to submit to her husband “as to the Lord.” That phrase is incredibly important.

The wife is not ultimately submitting because her husband is perfect. She is not submitting because he is always right. She is not submitting because she is less capable.

She is submitting because she loves Christ and desires to obey Christ.

David Guzik, quoting Martyn Lloyd-Jones, explains:

“It means: ‘Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands because it is a part of your duty to the Lord, because it is an expression of your submission to the Lord.’ Or, ‘Wives, submit yourselves to your own husbands; do it in this way, do it as a part of your submission to the Lord.’ In other words, you are not doing it only for the husband, you are doing it primarily for the Lord Himself… You are doing it for Christ’s sake, you are doing it because you know that He exhorts you to do it, because it is well-pleasing in His sight that you should be doing it. It is part of your Christian behaviour, it is a part of your discipleship.”

This means that Christian wives should not obey this command with resentment and bitterness. They submit out of love.

They love the Lord, and because they love the Lord, they desire to obey Him. They love their husbands, and because they love their husbands, they desire to serve them. Lilly Williams explains this personally:

Also, wives need to watch their resentment towards their husbands as well. We have the OPPORTUNITY to serve/submit our husbands. Yes, it is a command but it

is a blessing. It is so easy to get into the mindset of resenting the husband when being a trad wife. But so much blessing comes with it. I GET to cook my husband dinner, I GET to fix his plate, I GET to wash his clothes so he is able to go to work and provide for our family. When talking about submission, it is important to recognize feelings that come from our flesh and from the devil. We have a beautiful calling to serve and submit to our husbands out of obedience to the Lord. I serve/submit to my husband to serve the Lord.

This is an important distinction.

The Christian life is filled with things we are commanded to do, but that does not mean those things are burdens.

The commands of God are not meant to destroy our joy. They are meant to lead us into the life God designed us to live.

A Christian wife can say:

“I do not serve my husband because I am less valuable than him. I serve my husband because I love Christ.”

2. Submission Recognizes God's Order

Verse 23

“For the husband is the head of the wife even as Christ is the head of the church, his body, and is himself its Savior.”

The word head speaks of leadership and authority.

However, when God establishes the husband as the head of the wife, He is not saying that the husband is more valuable.

He is not saying that the husband is automatically more intelligent.

He is not saying that the husband is more capable in every area of life.

God is speaking about function and order.

Every organization needs leadership in order to function properly.

God has established order within His universe, His church, and His family.

This does not eliminate partnership.

Marriage is a partnership.

But every partnership requires leadership.

The husband is called to lead, and the wife is called to willingly submit to that leadership. This principle begins in Genesis.

Genesis 2:18 says:

“It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him.” God created Eve as a suitable helper for Adam.

Genesis 2:22–24 says:

“And the rib that the Lord God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man. Then the man said, ‘This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.’”

The man and woman were created to become one flesh.

Marriage is not a competition between two individuals trying to control one another. It is a God-ordained union.

Paul explains this order in 1 Corinthians 11:3:

“But I want you to understand that the head of every man is Christ, the head of a wife is her husband, and the head of Christ is God.”

Notice the order:

God → Christ → Husband → Wife

The husband is not the ultimate authority.

Christ is.

A Christian husband is not free to lead however he wants. He is under the authority of Christ. This is why a wife can submit to her husband without worshiping her husband. Her husband is not God.

He is a man under God.

The husband is called to be a protector and comforter of his wife.

Christ is the head of the church, and Christ sacrificed Himself for the church.

Therefore, a husband who desires to lead biblically must understand that biblical leadership is not selfish control.

It is sacrificial responsibility.

The husband is not called to say:

“You must do what I say because I am the man.”

He is called to say:

“I am responsible before God for how I lead my family.”

This is why the wife’s submission cannot be separated from the husband's responsibility. The wife is called to submit.

The husband is called to sacrifice.

The wife is called to respect.

The husband is called to love.

The wife is called to follow.

The husband is called to lead.

And both are called to obey Christ.

Submission Is Most Clearly Tested in Disagreement

It is easy to submit when you agree.

Everyone submits when they believe the person they are following is correct. The true test of submission comes when there is disagreement.

David Guzik says:

“Everyone submits to someone when they are in agreement. It's in disagreement that their submission is tested.”

This is where I want to share something personal from my wife, Lilly. Lilly Williams,”

When we received the counter offer back on this house, it was 5k more than we offered. I didn’t see an issue with it. It wouldn't have changed our loan amount and mortgage amount monthly by a significant amount. But, I did not personally care. In perspective to our loan amount already, what was another 5K? Ayden did not agree. Ayden put his phone down, and I argued with him completely and tried to push him to understand. He did not agree no matter what I said. He did not feel like it was right and that the sellers were trying to screw us over. I went home literally sad and upset and I didnt understand. We turned down their counter that was 5k higher than we were willing to take. It was hard to submit and understand. But understanding doesn't always have to come with submission. I even waited another day to let the realtor know that we turned down the offer because I was hoping he would change his mind. Two days after we turned down the offer and walked away from this house, the sellers called our realtor and told them that they would take our offer.”

This is an excellent picture of submission.

My wife did not necessarily agree with me.

She did not immediately understand my reasoning.

She even believed that I might be making the wrong decision.

But she eventually submitted to my leadership.

And then, two days later, the sellers accepted our original offer.

Now, I am not saying this story proves that I am always right.

I am not saying that every decision a husband makes will end up working out perfectly. The point is that understanding does not always have to come before submission. Sometimes you can say:

“I do not understand why you are making this decision.”

And yet still say:

“I trust God’s design for our marriage, and I will follow your leadership.”

This does not mean a wife is forbidden from giving advice.

It does not mean she cannot disagree.

It does not mean she cannot express her concerns.

A godly wife can speak.

A godly wife can advise.

A godly wife can disagree.

But there comes a point where the husband has the responsibility to lead, and the wife has the responsibility to submit to that leadership.

Submission is most clearly seen when agreement is absent.

3. Submission Trusts God's Design

Verse 24

“Now as the church submits to Christ, so also wives should submit in everything to their husbands.”

Paul gives the church as the example.

The church submits to Christ because Christ is her Lord.

In the same way, the wife is called to submit to her husband.

The phrase “in everything” is comprehensive.

However, it does not mean that a wife must obey her husband if he commands her to sin. God is always the highest authority.

A wife does not submit to her husband in sin.

A wife does not submit to abuse.

A wife does not submit to criminal behavior.

A wife does not submit to adultery.

A wife does not submit to anything that directly violates God's Word. The husband has authority under Christ.

He does not have authority above Christ.

This is why Scripture is so important in marriage.

The husband should not simply say:

“Because I said so.”

He should desire to lead according to the Word of God.

The wife should not simply say:

“I will never follow my husband.”

She should desire to honor God's design for marriage.

Both husband and wife must constantly ask:

“What does God want?”

God remains number one.

Matthew 6:21 says:

“For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.” We should treasure the Lord above everything.

But within marriage, we should also treasure the spouse God has given us. A wife should not view submission as a curse.

She should view it as an opportunity to serve the Lord.

A husband should not view leadership as a privilege to control his wife. He should view it as a responsibility to serve and protect her.

The Gospel Picture of Marriage

Paul is ultimately using marriage to point us toward Christ and the church. The church submits to Christ because Christ has given Himself for the church. The church follows Christ because Christ first loved the church.

This is why Ephesians 5 cannot be understood by only reading verses 22–24. The entire passage must be read together.

The wife is called to submit.

The husband is called to love sacrificially.

The wife is called to respect.

The husband is called to serve.

The wife is called to follow.

The husband is called to lead.

And both are called to submit themselves to Christ.

The ultimate goal is not for the husband to get his way.

The ultimate goal is not for the wife to get her way.

The ultimate goal is for Christ to be glorified through the marriage.

Main Point

Husbands and wives are called to walk in a spirit of submission and love, with the wife submitting to her husband’s God-given leadership and the husband leading under the authority of Christ.

Biblical submission is not inferiority.

It is not silence.

It is not mindless obedience.

It is not the absence of wisdom or counsel.

Biblical submission is a willing surrender to God's design for marriage.

And when a wife submits to her husband as unto the Lord, she is ultimately demonstrating her submission to Christ.

The question for every Christian marriage is not:

“How can I get my way?”

The question is:

“How can I best obey Christ?”

Because when the husband is submitted to Christ and the wife is submitted to Christ, both husband and wife are moving toward the same Lord.

And when both are moving toward Christ, they are ultimately moving closer to one another.
