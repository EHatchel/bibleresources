Ephesians 4:25–32
The Right Kind of Walking (Part 2)

Wrapping up chapter 4, we’re still in the commonly referred to as applicational half of Ephesians. Last time, we spent time going over Paul's elaboration of how unbelievers/gentiles walk, how that's not how Jesus showed us how to walk, then concluded by saying we need to put off your old self (or the old man) and put on the new.

First word we see is “Therefore”, if you’re new. Whenever we see, therefore it’s in reference to what was just said. It’s associated. Last thing we discussed last time was putting on the new man. Being made new in Christ.

In this section, Paul is now elaborating on how the BELIEVER walks. Last time unbeliever, this time believers.

Decided to break this down one by one as opposed to more grouped sections seeing as that each line is a different aspect of a believer's new “walk”.

1. Always Tell The Truth

25 Therefore, having put away falsehood, let each one of you speak the truth with his neighbor, for we are members one of another.

This sounds probably self-explanatory, just don’t lie.

Q: But what does it mean to lie? (Open)

Is it when you say something that is simply not true? Sure. But is it when you lie to protect someone's feelings? What about a little white lie here and there? When you say something not true about someone to make them look bad or even exaggerate to make something seem worse than it really is? What about cheating? Lying to get ahead in a video game? Agreeing to rules and not standing behind your word?

The answer is Yes.

Anything that you say or sometimes do that isn’t ‘True’ in any matter, is lying.

Proverbs 12:22 says

22 Lying lips are an abomination to the Lord, but those who act faithfully are his delight.

Paul, in 1 Peter 3:10, quoting Psalms 34:13 said

10 For “Whoever desires to love life and see good days, let him keep his tongue from evil and his lips from speaking deceit;”

Moreover lying can hurt others, violating the second greatest commandment. (Love your neighbor). Whether you lie TO them or AGAINST them, you hurt at minimum the trust you have between them, if not them emotionally or socially.

What I’d say is worst of all, is it hurts your reputation.

If you remember us going through Philippians, we covered why your reputation matters. How people see you matters. Lying can ruin all of that if it’s what you're known for.

Let me ask you, how is someone suppose to believe you when you say Jesus is the Son of God and Savior if all you ever do is lie? If I was them I’d add it to your pile.

But Imagine, if you never lie. Time and time again you’re tested before people to always tell the truth. Would they believe then? Maybe not. But it would get them thinking, if they examine your life and never can recall you lying they will at least know that you believe it to be true with your whole heart. And that will get them interested.

It’s hard for our lips to tell others THE truth if they barely can tell any truth.

So always tell the truth.

2. Be Angry But Don’t Sin

26 Be angry and do not sin; do not let the sun go down on your anger,

Q: What does this mean? How can one be angry and not Sin?

Many people believe the bible tells us to never be angry, yet the Bible has quite a few examples of God himself being angry.

We see it at the tree with Adam and Eve (Genesis)

In Exodus (14) we see God take out anger upon Pharaoh and his army for oppressing his people. Then after that we see God get angry enough with the Isrealites all throughout the old testament for not listening and keeping his commandments.

“What about Jesus? Jesus was never angry right?” Yep, him too!

Matthew 21:12-17 Jesus took a whip and whipped people, turned over tables, and dumped their money out on the ground for trading in the temple and said “My house shall be called a house of prayer,’ but you make it a den of thieves.”

So what are we seeing? Righteous indignation.

Anger stemming from a perception of injustice or moral wrongdoing

Martin Luther described it as a ‘Anger of Love’

A righteous anger against the wicked/sinful and the anger to stand up for those who are oppressed.

The anger the Bible speaks against is selfish anger. Mallace, jealousy, resentment, or outright hatred. The anger of pride, angry on one's own behalf to say “I can’t believe this was done to me”. In this regard, James 1:19 tells us

19 Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger.

Then we get to “don’t let the sun go down on your anger”

If your girlfriend/wife see’s this verse you will most likely hear:

“SEE! The Bible even says you can’t go to sleep mad at me!”

And while true marriage advice, overall it’s really saying to make it right as soon as possible.

If you’re angry at someone, go address it. No one’s promised tomorrow.

You never know when it could be a misunderstanding. Even if your anger has grounds, forgive them. It’s hard to reach people when you’re mad at each other.

3. Sin Can Let The Devil In

27 and give no opportunity to the devil.

Q: What does this mean?

27 really ties to 26

Leave no sin unconfessed, no anger unaddressed, and no room for the devil to wreak havoc in your life.

Letting thing’s linger far too long will lead to more sin.

If you're angry, fix it before it turns into anger.

If you’re lustful, confess and repent, before it turns into adultery or addiction

And so on.

Sooner you take it to God, the sooner he can take it away.

4. Do Honest Work

28 Let the thief no longer steal, but rather let him labor, doing honest work with his own hands, so that he may have something to share with anyone in need.

The Los Angeles Times, Forbess, Fox and many others wrote that statistically $112 Billion was lost to theft in 2022.

From Exodus (20) when God made the commandment “Thou Shall Not Steal” to 2022 theft’s been a problem.

Instead of working hard, laboring with their hands, doing honest work they took what was not theirs at no expense to themselves.

Often out of Greed, which they do for themselves.

The verse tells us to instead work hard for abundance that we may in return GIVE to those who are in need.

More importantly, work for what you have. Use the blessing of an able body to labor. Use the opportunity to reach people and acquire physical wealth to take care of others.

5. Don’t Gossip

29 Let no corrupting talk come out of your mouths, but only such as is good for building up, as fits the occasion, that it may give grace to those who hear.

Now the second point made in regards to the mouth/tongue. Goes to show how impactful and important words really can be.

If you had the opportunity to go to generally any public school you know exactly what Gossip is. Something happens to or with someone, everyone begins to speak negatively or mockingly about the person. If you were anything like me in highschool, you probably did it often. Eager to hear and discuss someone else's shortcomings. At Least till it was about yours.

But is this loving your neighbor? I’d argue it's almost the complete opposite. This ties in to some of the same concepts we’ve spoken on before. It can hurt your reputation and relationship with the person. Making it all the more difficult for them to see the love of God you are supposed to be presenting.

This refers to bad mouthing. Talking crap about someone. In highschool we called these “roasts” and “checking” one another. Just as gossip, it often seems funny till it’s your turn. It tears people down and mocks God's creation.

It can also refer to swearing. Using foul language with the same mouth you’re trying to witness to others about Jesus. How can you swear and worship with the same mouth? As we’ve said before, if you wouldn’t say it if God was standing in front of you face to face, you probably shouldn’t say it at all.

This is something everyone struggles with.

James 3:8

8 but no human being can tame the tongue. It is a restless evil, full of deadly poison.

NO HUMAN. Some may see this as an excuse, “It says no one can, so why do I even try?” but the same could be said for many other sins. No ones perfect, but that doesn’t mean we can’t try. We’re called to. Commanded.

1 John 2:6

6 Whoever says he abides in him ought to walk in the same way in which he walked.

All of this being said, what comes from your mouth matters. Especially with people. There’s something to be said about telling someone the harsh truth. But that gives you no right to be hateful or disrespectful to them. Instead it’s out of love. It’s saying “I love you enough to call you out on this to help you”. As the verse says it’s “for building up, as fits the occasion, to give grace”

Remember a time you messed up and everyone spoke negatively about you for it when all you needed was a little grace to not be perfect. Give it to someone next time they mess up. It’s what Jesus would have given them.

6. Change Required

30 And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption. 31 Let all bitterness and wrath and anger and clamor and slander be put away from you, along with all malice.

Q: What does this mean?

Definition of grieve in this context is distress. To give grief to.

The Holy Spirit is with you the day you’re saved. Working in you and your life, guiding you. Things you had no problem doing prior, gives you a sense of conviction now. That’s good, the Holy Spirit is trying to change you. What is not good is to ignore it or be, as we discussed last time, callous to it.

Romans 12:2

2 Do not be conformed to this world,[] but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect.

Refusing to change from your wicked ways grieve the Holy Spirit.

Many scholars and pastors point out that for the Spirit to grieve it’s not a force or an energy but a person. Discerning and acting as we do. Some people look at The Father and The Son (Jesus) as people, but the Spirit as some kind of Holy wizardry the other two use. This is wrong. The Spirit is part of the same trinity that makes up God, along with the father and the son. Can get confusing.

Point being, is don’t give the Holy Spirit grief when it tries to guide your life. Don’t be too stubborn or prideful in sin that you refuse to change your ways as we’re called to do when we acknowledge our salvation.

Again, Galatians 2:20

20 I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me. And the life I now live in the flesh I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.

Dying to yourself everyday is a long and daunting process, but Jesus left the Spirit here with us to help. Don’t work against it.

7. Forgive As You’ve Been Forgiven

32 Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you.

Not much to break down here.

Be kind to one another.

Be tenderhearted.

And most of all, forgive one another as God in Christ forgave you.

Not “forgive when it’s easy” or “forgive so long as it wasn’t anything too bad.”

But forgive as we’ve been forgiven.

How were we forgiven? Was it when we got our act together? Was it we paid Jesus back then we were even?

It was when he died for a bunch of people who betrayed him. You may think I mean then, but I’m talking about us now as well. He died for our sins before we were ever born.

Romans 5:8

8 but God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

He forgave all of us when we did nothing to deserve it.

This is how we’re called to forgive.

When they don’t deserve it and you have every right to hate them, love them and forgive them as you’ve been forgiven.

Main Point:

Paul elaborates to the Ephesians that believers are to walk contrary to non-believers by always telling the truth, having a righteous anger, leaving no room for the devil, doing honest work, using words to build others up, letting the Holy Spirit guide them, and forgive as they have been forgiven in Christ.

Application:

Walk like a new man of God, not the old man.
