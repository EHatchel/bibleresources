The Things That Truly Matter - 1 Timothy 6:17-21

Introduction:

The world measures success by what a person owns, how much money they make, what kind of house they live in, and what position they hold in society. Many people believe that if they can accumulate enough wealth, they will finally have security, peace, and happiness.

But the Word of God teaches us that our greatest treasure is not found in the things we possess. Our greatest treasure is found in the God we serve.

In 1 Timothy 6, the apostle Paul is writing to Timothy, a young pastor serving the church at Ephesus. Paul has been warning against false teaching, the love of money, and the danger of allowing worldly desires to control the heart. Earlier in the chapter, Paul declares that the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.

Now, as Paul brings this letter toward its conclusion, he gives Timothy instructions concerning those who are rich, those who desire to live faithfully, and those who have been entrusted with the truth of the gospel.

These verses remind us of three important responsibilities: trust God, use our blessings for His glory, and guard the truth He has entrusted to us.

Let us examine this passage verse by verse.

Point 1: Trust in the God Who Provides (1 Timothy 6:17)

Paul instructs Timothy to charge those who are rich in this present world not to be proud and not to put their hope in uncertain riches, but in God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy.

Wealth must never become a source of pride.

Paul does not say that every person who has money is wicked. Instead, he warns against the spiritual dangers that can accompany wealth.

Money can create the illusion that we are independent. When people have financial security, they may begin to believe that they no longer need anyone, including God.

But everything we possess ultimately comes from the Lord. Our abilities, opportunities, health, resources, and opportunities to work are all gifts of His providence.

Deuteronomy 8:18 reminds God's people that it is the Lord who gives them the ability to produce wealth.

Therefore, we should never look down on someone because they have less than we do. Nor should we believe that material prosperity makes us more important in the sight of God.

What God has allowed us to possess should produce gratitude, not arrogance.

Wealth is uncertain, but God is trustworthy.

Paul calls riches uncertain because earthly possessions can disappear. Businesses can fail, investments can lose value, jobs can be lost, and material possessions can be destroyed.

Even when money remains, it cannot purchase eternal life, guarantee good health, or satisfy every longing of the human heart.

The problem is not simply having money. The problem is placing our confidence in money.

Our bank accounts can change overnight, but God's character does not change. Our circumstances may become unstable, but the Lord remains faithful.

Proverbs 23:5 warns us that riches can take wings and disappear. Jesus also teaches in Matthew 6:19–21 that earthly treasures are temporary and that our hearts follow what we treasure.

What you treasure will capture your heart, shape your perspective, and determine whom you serve. Jesus calls us to wholehearted devotion to God rather than devotion to wealth.

Never place eternal confidence in something that cannot last eternally.

God provides good gifts according to His wisdom.

Paul describes God as the One who richly provides us with everything to enjoy.

Christianity does not teach that every material possession is evil or that believers must feel guilty whenever God blesses them. We can receive God's gifts with thanksgiving and enjoy them responsibly.

Food, family, friendship, work, rest, and the resources needed for daily life can all be received as expressions of God's goodness.

However, we must enjoy God's gifts without allowing those gifts to take God's place.

The question is not merely, "What has God given me?" We must also ask, "Am I honoring God with what He has given me?"

Application: Examine your heart. Is your peace rooted in what you own, or in the God who provides? If your possessions were reduced tomorrow, would your faith in God remain?

Point 2: Use Your Blessings to Bless Others (1 Timothy 6:18)

Paul tells Timothy to instruct the rich to do good, to be rich in good works, to be generous, and to be willing to share.

Notice that Paul moves from what believers must avoid to what they must actively pursue.

It is not enough to avoid pride. We must practice humility. It is not enough to avoid trusting money. We must use our resources in ways that honor God.

Do good with what God has given you.

Paul says that believers are to be rich in good works.

The world often defines riches by possessions, but God teaches us to measure our lives by faithfulness and service.

A person may have very little money and still be rich in compassion, generosity, encouragement, and service. Another person may have great wealth but be spiritually poor because they live only for themselves.

Good works do not purchase salvation. We are saved by God's grace through faith, as Ephesians 2:8–10 teaches. But the grace that saves us also produces a life that seeks to do good.

Practice generosity with a willing heart.

Paul says believers should be generous and ready to share.

Generosity is not limited to wealthy people. Every believer can learn to give according to the resources God has provided.

Some can give financially. Others can give time, attention, hospitality, practical assistance, or encouragement. The important issue is whether our hearts are willing to serve.

Generosity also reveals what rules our hearts. When we believe that everything belongs to God, we begin to see ourselves as stewards rather than owners.

A steward manages what belongs to someone else. In the same way, Christians are called to manage God's gifts faithfully.

Second Corinthians 9:7 teaches that giving should not be done reluctantly or under compulsion, but willingly and cheerfully.

Application:

Is there someone God has placed in my path whom I can help?

Does my generosity reflect my gratitude for God's grace?

The goal is not to impress others with what we give. The goal is to honor God through a life of openhearted service.

Point 3: Invest in What Lasts Forever (1 Timothy 6:19)

Paul explains that generosity enables believers to store up treasure for themselves as a good foundation for the future, so that they may take hold of what is truly life.

This verse connects our present choices with our eternal future.

Build a foundation that lasts.

Earthly wealth may provide temporary comfort, but it cannot serve as the foundation of eternal life.

Paul encourages believers to use their resources in ways that have lasting spiritual value.

When we help someone in need, support gospel ministry, care for our families, serve our church, and invest in the spiritual growth of others, we are using temporary resources for purposes that matter eternally.

Jesus teaches in Matthew 6:20 to lay up treasures in heaven rather than making earthly treasure our ultimate pursuit.

This does not mean that we can purchase heaven through charitable giving. Eternal life is God's gift through Jesus Christ. Rather, our generosity demonstrates that our hearts have been changed by God's grace.

Live for more than the present world.

Paul describes the future with the phrase "the life which is life indeed," or what is truly life.

The world often tells us that life consists of comfort, pleasure, recognition, and material success. But Scripture teaches that real life is found in knowing God and living according to His purposes.

A person can possess many things and still lack peace, purpose, and fellowship with God.

On the other hand, a believer who lives faithfully for Christ may not have great earthly wealth but possesses a hope that this world cannot take away.

Do not spend your entire life preparing for retirement while neglecting preparation for eternity.

Planning responsibly for the future is not wrong. The danger is allowing earthly security to become more important than spiritual faithfulness.

Application: Consider what your life is producing. Are you investing only in things that will eventually pass away, or are you also investing in people, ministry, godliness, and the work of the gospel?

Point 4: Guard the Truth God Has Entrusted to You (1 Timothy 6:20–21)

Paul now turns directly to Timothy and gives him a final, personal charge: "Keep that which is committed to thy trust."

The King James Version uses language that emphasizes the sacred responsibility Timothy has received. Paul is reminding him that the truth of the gospel is a treasure to protect, preserve, and faithfully proclaim.

Guard the truth of God's Word.

Timothy was not free to change the gospel to suit the culture, satisfy popular opinion, or please those who preferred a different message.

He had been entrusted with the truth of God's Word, and he was responsible for teaching it faithfully.

The same principle applies to the church today. We must not replace biblical truth with personal opinions, worldly philosophies, or teachings that sound impressive but contradict Scripture.

The church must remain committed to the authority of God's Word and the saving message of Jesus Christ.

Guarding the truth does not mean being harsh or argumentative. We should speak the truth with love, humility, patience, and courage.

The gospel is not ours to reinvent. It is ours to receive, believe, live out, and proclaim faithfully.

Reject teachings that lead people away from truth.

Paul warns Timothy to avoid profane and empty talk

Paul is warning against claims of knowledge that are empty, misleading, or opposed to the truth of the gospel.

False teaching can be attractive because it sounds intellectual, promises secret insight, or appeals to human pride. But something does not become true merely because it is popular, sophisticated, or confidently presented.

Believers must test what they hear against Scripture. We should be willing to examine ideas carefully while refusing to abandon biblical truth for teachings that contradict it.

Conclusion

As we bring this passage to a close, we see that Paul gives us a clear picture of faithful Christian living.

First, we must trust God rather than earthly riches. Money and possessions can change, but God remains faithful.

Second, we must use our blessings to serve others. God's gifts are opportunities to do good, practice generosity, and demonstrate the love of Christ.

Third, we must invest in what lasts forever. Our lives should be shaped by eternal priorities rather than temporary pleasures.

Finally, we must guard the truth of God's Word. The gospel is a sacred trust, and we must remain faithful to it in a world filled with competing voices.

These verses leave us with an important question: What are we doing with what God has entrusted to us?

God has entrusted us with time, abilities, opportunities, resources, relationships. We will one day give an account of how we have lived, not so that we can earn salvation, but because our lives belong to the One who saved us.
