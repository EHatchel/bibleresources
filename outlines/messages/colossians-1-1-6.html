Gotquestions.org writes:

“It is important to know God’s will. Jesus said that His true relations are those who know and do the Father’s will: “Whoever does God’s will is my brother and sister and mother” (Mark 3:35). At its most basic, the will of God is to repent of our sin and trust in Christ. If we have not taken that first step, then we have not yet accepted God’s will.

Romans 12:2
’Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect. ’

As his mind is renewed according to the things of God, then he can know God’s perfect will.As we seek God’s will, we should make sure what we are considering is not something the Bible forbids. He reveals to us a step at a time—each move a step of faith—and allows us to continue to trust Him. The important thing is that, as we wait for further direction, we are busy doing the good that we know to do (James 4:17).

One Essential Relation - Brotherhood

Believers must develop an abiding love for one another. To “love the brotherhood” does not mean that believers will always have loving feelings toward each other; instead, it means we will choose to sacrifice our interests for the interests of others (cf. Philippians 2:3).

Loving the brotherhood involves maintaining peace and unity with our brothers and sisters in Christ (see Ephesians 4:3). We are God’s family, indwelled by the Holy Spirit. In Ephesians 4:4–6, the apostle Paul writes, “There is one body and one spirit, just as you were called to one hope when you were called; one Lord, one faith, one baptism; one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all.” Loving the brotherhood is part of sustaining this “oneness.”

Why is that important?

We are called to love one another.

We are called to pray for one another.

We also know we get healing from brotherhood in James 5:16.

We are called to forgive one another shown in Ephesians 4:32.

We are called to encourage one another 1 Thessalonians 5:11.

We are called to submit to one another Ephesians 5:21.

And this continues all throughout scripture, we can go on and on, but the importance of brotherhood and community is imminent.

Timothy

Timothy - Disciple of Paul. Son in the faith. He probably heard and responded to the gospel when Paul came through the area of Derbe and Lystra on his first missionary journey, but we don’t know for sure. Timothy served as Paul’s representative to several churches (1 Corinthians 4:17; Philippians 2:19), and he was later a pastor in Ephesus (1 Timothy 1:3). Timothy is also mentioned as being with Paul when Paul wrote several New Testament letters—2 Corinthians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, and Philemon.

Paul says Timothy had a “genuine faith,” the same as that which lived in his mother and grandmother (2 Timothy 1:1–5). Eunice and Lois prepared Timothy’s heart to accept Christ by teaching Timothy the Old Testament Scriptures and preparing him “from infancy” to recognize the Messiah when He appeared (2 Timothy 3:15). When Paul came preaching Christ, all three accepted his teaching and committed their lives to the Savior.

Paul also told Timothy to “do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker who does not need to be ashamed and who correctly handles the word of truth” (2 Timothy 2:15). This advice is crucial for all Christians. “All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work” (2 Timothy 3:16–17). Paul counseled Timothy, his “dear son” (2 Timothy 1:2), from a heart of love, wanting Timothy to stand firm in his own faith and to lead the other believers well. Timothy certainly seems to have been faithful; we should follow his example.

V.3-5

Habitual foundation - Prayer

Paul starts by saying that he always gives thanks to God when he prays for them. There is something important about this. Paul doesn’t just pray for them when there is a problem. Prayer is habitual. It is something he continually does.

Prayer should not just be something we run to when life gets hard. Prayer should be the foundation of our relationship with God. Paul says “when we pray for you” because prayer was a regular part of his life.

Following the source of our future path - Faith, love, hope

The source of a great Christian life is the gospel.

The Future of a great Christian life is Hope.

The path to hope is Faith and Love through Jesus Christ.

Paul gives us three things here that really show the Christian life.

Faith - what we believe and trust in Christ.

Love - how that faith is displayed toward other believers.

Hope - what we are looking forward to because of Christ.

These three are connected. Faith looks to Christ, love works because of Christ, and hope looks forward to what Christ has promised.

Their faith was “in Christ Jesus.” Their love was “for all the saints.” Their hope was “laid up for them in heaven.”

This means their faith wasn’t just something they said they believed. Their faith was producing something in their life. Their faith in Christ was producing love for other believers, and their hope in heaven was giving them a reason to continue living faithfully.

A great Christian life is not just about what you believe. It is about what that belief produces.

W. H. G. Thomas gives us an excellent description of the interrelation of faith, hope and love:

“Faith rests on the past, love works in the present, and hope presses toward the future; or, faith looks backward and upward, love looks outward, and hope looks forward. These three constitute the true, complete Christian life and not one of them should be omitted or slighted.We are only too apt to emphasize faith and love and forget hope but, inasmuch as hope is invariably connected with the coming of the Lord, “that blessed hope” (Titus 2:13), it is a vital part of our Christian life. Faith accepts, hope expects; faith appropriates, hope anticipates; faith receives, hope realizes; faith is always and only concerned with the past and present, hope is always and only concerned with the future. We know that faith comes by hearing; we shall find that hope comes by experience. Faith is concerned with a person who promises, hope with the thing promised; and faith is the root of which hope is a fruit. (Hope Laid Up for You in Heaven - W. H. G. Thomas)

Laid Up as used here is different then we use it. The greek is keimai, “to lie,” and apo “off, away from.” Thus, the compound verb means “to be laid away, to be reserved, put to one side,” The hope that is reserved for believers. Stored up for the future eternal life that awaits us. As we know Heaven is where Christ is, so that’s where our hope is stored. Our Hope is not a place ,but rather a person.

Something else that stands out is that Paul says they had heard of this hope before. Their hope was not something they invented for themselves. It came through the Gospel. The Gospel gave them something to believe in, something to live for, and something to look forward to.

V.6

Paul is giving a further description of the gospel. Teaching it , but also hinting at the christians who have been deceived by Gnostics. From what I have looked through they believe they have a Holy spark inside of them. If they can find their true identity inside themselves then they are God of their life.

But notice what Paul says about the Gospel. He says it has come to them, as indeed in the whole world it is bearing fruit and increasing.

The Gospel is not stagnant.

The Gospel produces fruit.

The Gospel causes growth.

When the Gospel is truly received, it should begin to produce something in the life of the person who receives it.

Paul isn’t saying that the Colossians were perfect. He is saying that the Gospel was working in them. There was evidence that they had received the truth.

This goes back to our identity. The Gnostics were teaching that the answer was found inside of yourself. Find the Holy spark inside of you and you will discover who you really are.

Paul gives us the opposite.

You don’t find your identity by looking deeper inside yourself.

You find your identity through the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

They heard the Gospel, they understood the grace of God in truth, and the Gospel began bearing fruit in their lives.

The Gospel doesn’t just change what you believe, it changes how you live.

Barclay commenting on in all the world (cp Col 1:23, “all creation under heaven.”) observes that:

“The Gospel is universal. It is for all the world. It is not confined to any one race or nation, nor to any one class or condition. Very few things in this world are open to all men. A man’s mental caliber decides the studies he can undertake. A man’s social class decides the circle amidst which he will move. A man’s material wealth determines the possessions he can amass. A man’s particular gifts decide the things he can do. But the message of the gospel is open without exception to all men.”

There is no special class of Christian.

There is no secret level of Christianity.

There is no hidden knowledge that only certain people can discover.

The Gospel is for everyone.

And when the Gospel is received, it begins to bear fruit.

That fruit is evidence of the Gospel working in us.
