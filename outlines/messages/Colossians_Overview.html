COLOSSIANS OVERVIEW:

Colossians is the next book we are diving into over the next few months. If you can’t find it in your Bible, it is towards the back of your bible, sitting between Philippians and 1st Thessalonians. This is our 4th and final prison epistle written by Paul.

If you don’t know Paul, the apostle:

He was once a prosecutor of christians and was a highly respected pharisee. He was fully against Christians and their belief so much that he would kill them.

But later in his life we read he was encountered by Jesus and quickly converted to christianity. Shortly after his conversion he started his many missionary journeys spreading the gospel across hundreds of nations all across Asia. It is estimated that Paul roughly traveled 9,150 miles (14,725 km) in 14 years. He was so bold about spreading the gospel that he ended up imprisoned multiple times for his bold claims of the gospel. And during one of his imprisonments around 60 AD, he wrote 4 letters to churches and individuals in churches. Colossians is one of these 4 letters he wrote.

In the other letters we have discussed in this group, Paul was either the founder of the church or had at least met the people he is addressing the letter to. In this case he hadn't. Paul hasn’t visited or seen this church face to face. So how did this letter come about if he didn’t know about this church? We can thank Epaphras for this. Epaphras was believed to be the founder of the Colossian church, and we can infer from this that Epaphras was saved from hearing Paul’s ministry in Ephesus. We can see Paul highly respected Epaphras and his labor for the Lord as he calls him a “fellow servant,” “faithful minister,” and “servant of Christ Jesus” a few times in his letters. (Colossians 1:7). And according to old map routes, researchers say that he had traveled over 1,300 miles from his home in Colossae, all the way to Rome where Paul was imprisoned to meet with him. But why? Why travel half the length of the US to meet one guy? Well after a few years of the Colossian church being founded, Epaphras had noticed a heresy brewing that was threatening the church. What is a heresy?

A heresy is a ”a belief or opinion that is contrary to an already established and accepted doctrine” or “a false prophecy”. Although Paul doesn’t say specifically what that heresy was in this letter, he covers a few themes that are believed to be directed at the groups attacking the church with false doctrine. This heresy in particular was a mixture of lots of different historical beliefs that were trying to disqualify what Epaphras built the church up in. The NIV Study Bible Commentary states:

“An analysis of his refutation suggests that the heresy was diverse in nature. Some of the elements of its teachings were:

Ceremonialism (focus on or excessive emphasis on ceremony, rituals, and formal practices)

Asceticism. [as·cet·i·cism] (the rejection of the pleasures of the world/not drinking, smoking, eating sugar, seeing movies, using the internet, or owning a cell phone)

Angel worship.

Depreciation of Christ.

Secret knowledge.

Reliance on human wisdom and tradition (Gnosticism)

These elements seem to fall into two categories, Jewish and Gnostic. It is likely, therefore, that the Colossian heresy was a mixture of an extreme form of Judaism and an early stage of Gnosticism”

To summarize this into easier terms, Colossae was being attacked by false teachers, stating that the church's idea of Jesus and his superiority over all things is not true. They also believed in relying on personal human knowledge over the hidden wisdom of God. This must have been impacting the church heavily for Epaphras to run to Paul for help.

So Paul hearing this has a clear image of what he needs to address to the church of Colossae. And Precept Austin splits the four chapters into two overall themes. Chapters 1 and 2 go over “The Supremacy of Christ”, where in Chapter 1 Paul goes over who he is and what ministry is for the Lord, and expresses that God is the creator and highest authority of all things. We see him hit it on the nail in Colossians 1:15-16 saying “He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things were created through him and for him.”.

And in chapter 2 he goes over not being discouraged or influenced by these false teachers, by sticking to what they know is true. He says at the end of chapter 2 telling the church that: “'These have indeed an appearance of wisdom in promoting self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh.'

Then the second half of the letter, Chapters 3 and 4, go over “The Submission of Christ”. He goes through the importance of conducting themselves according to their new self in Christ, with Chapter 3 covering many things we see also in Ephesians about putting on their new self, sexual impurity, wives and husbands, etc. And chapter 4 takes on final instructions Paul has for the church and his farewell to them.

We have seen Paul address multiple church groups and individuals through these letters, and each letter has a set theme specifically for the church. For example, in Ephesians, he discusses the mystery of the gospel being revealed, the unity of the church, and the emphasis that the church is the body of Christ, with Christ being the head. And we will see here in Colossians that Paul focuses on telling them that Christ is the head of all things, and that Christ should be the center of all the ways they conduct themselves, so that they can not be influenced by the false doctrine that is attacking them.
