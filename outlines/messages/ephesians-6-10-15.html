Ephesians 6:10–15
Prepare for War

Introduction

The Christian life is a blessing. The Christian life is prosperous. The Christian life is abundant. The Christian life is peaceful. The Christian life is a battle.

The Ephesians have been taught this great and mysterious doctrine in chapters 1–3. The mystery revealed that Christ came to unite all things unto Himself—both Jews and Gentiles. It will all belong to Him. We are united as one.

Then, in chapters 4–6, Paul teaches us how to walk. Humility and submission have been crucial in our learning to walk.

Now—finally.

Hold up. Take heed. Proceed with caution.

Paul is now preparing the Christian for war.

Finally

This comes at the end of the letter—a letter in which Paul has carefully established our place in Jesus and then taught us the basics of the Christian walk. This is his last section dealing with that walk.

In light of:

● All that God has done for you.
● The glorious standing you have as a child of God.
● His great plan of the ages that God has made you part of.
● The plan for Christian maturity and growth He gives to you.
● The conduct God calls every believer to live.
● The filling of the Spirit and our walk in the Spirit.

In light of all this, there is a battle to fight in the Christian life.

Point 1: The Strength of the Soldier

Verse 10

“Finally, be strong in the Lord and in the strength of his might.”

Be Strong in the Lord

Where could we draw our strength without the Lord?

Strength is ability.

The only ability I have without the Lord is sin.

That is what my life looks like when I am not dependent on the ability of the Lord and when I am not drawing my strength from Him.

The Greek word translated “might” refers to strength and ability.

How strong do you have to be to be known for having strength that is mighty? This is the person we are reliant on.

How comforting is that?

Your General is undefeated. He is victorious over all.

God’s best soldiers are those who are conscious of their own weakness and ineffectiveness and who rely solely on Him.

It is the Lord who provides the strength. In your weakness, He makes you strong. 2 Corinthians 12:9–10

The Lord provides strength in our weakness.

Paul tells us that the power of Christ rests upon those who recognize their weakness. Without this armor, we would not have a chance to stand against the schemes of the devil. Are we beginning to understand the importance?

As Spurgeon used to say:

“Satan never kicks a dead horse!”

The enemy is fighting against those who are alive in Christ.

Point 2: The Enemy of the Soldier

Verse 11

“Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the schemes of the devil.”

The Devil Is a Schemer and Deceiver

The devil's power is limited, but his tactics are extremely powerful.

The word “wiles” refers to the deceits, craftiness, trickery, methods, and strategies that the devil uses to wage war against the believer.

He will do everything he can to deceive and capture the believer.

Satan's schemes appeal to:

● The lust of the eyes.
● The desires of the flesh.
● The pride of life.

Satan will see to it that something crosses the eyesight of the believer—something appealing to the flesh and pride.

He may use:

● Delicious food.
● An attractive person.
● Someone exposing their body.
● Possessions.
● Clothing.
● Land.
● Cars.
● Houses.
● Positions.
● Authority.
● Power.

Satan will present something to the eyes that is appealing to the flesh.

He will entice the believer to eat the second helping, take the second look, buy the unneeded possession, and selfishly seek more power and position.

He will use every strategy he can to appeal to the flesh and the pride of the believer. That You May Be Able To

The word “may” reminds us that without the armor, there could be a loss. The armor is necessary for the believer to stand.

Whole

Incomplete could mean failure.

Without the whole armor, you are vulnerable to attacks in certain areas of your life. The enemy is looking for weaknesses.

If you leave one area unprotected, the enemy has an opportunity to attack. Point 3: Know Who You Are Fighting

Verse 12

“For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood...”

Our Battle Is Not Physical

Our battle is not ultimately against flesh and blood.

It is spiritual.

Paul describes the devil's schemes and armies—spiritual forces, rulers, and authorities. These are the forces we are protecting ourselves against.

What is the point of putting on armor when you do not know who you are fighting? Imagine spending all your time preparing for a war in which you do not know your enemy.

You kill cows to get the hide for hide armor. You stitch it together and suit up. You grab your sword and shield. You feel better prepared because now you have armor.

You feel comfortable and confident.

Then a tank rolls over the hill.

You prepared—but you did not know who you were preparing to fight. This is why it is important to know who you are fighting.

That is why Paul is telling us about our enemy and his schemes.

The enemy has:

● Strong forces.
● Incredible numbers.
● A hierarchy within his army.
● Deceptive tactics.
● The ability to spread blindness throughout the world.

Look around you and see how many blinded people there are.

This enemy is not one to take lightly.

If you do not stand and put on the whole armor of God, you are susceptible to his attacks. Point 4: Prepare for the Evil Day

Verse 13

“Therefore take up the whole armor of God, that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand firm.”

Is the Evil Day Now?

Yes.

Until Christ returns, we are living in these evil days.

We are in a constant battle every day.

Having Done All

Put on every piece.

How many times does Paul reinstate this?

The repetition should be worthy of our attention.

The importance is obvious.

Imagine getting your armor on and preparing to charge into battle. You take one step—and your pants fall down.

Your legs are exposed.

You forgot a piece.

You get your armor on again. This time, you make sure you have everything. You feel secure.

You charge forward.

You see an enemy approaching.

You reach for your sword—

And grab an air sword.

Dead.

Every piece matters.

David's Failure to Prepare

One key teaching from King David is the danger of neglecting the battle.

David procrastinated.

He stayed home instead of going to war.

While staying home, he saw Bathsheba bathing on a roof. He invited her into his home. He slept with her. She became pregnant.

Then he sent her husband back to war, knowing that he would die, in an attempt to cover his sin.

David's failure began with neglecting his responsibility.

He was supposed to be at war.

Instead, he stayed home.

In the same sense, Paul is telling us to prepare.

Do not be lazy.

Do not procrastinate.

This is a call to battle.

Take urgency.

Be prepared quickly and without hesitation.

The command refers to the full preparation of a foot soldier for both offense and defense—the complete suit of armor.

Paul could certainly claim knowledge of the Roman soldier's armor, having been chained to one for years.

Point 5: Stand Firm in Truth and Righteousness

Verse 14

“Stand therefore, having fastened on the belt of truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness...”

Stand Firm

This is the third time Paul calls believers to stand firm.

The repetition emphasizes the need for immovable steadfastness in the face of a relentless and ruthless enemy.

The Belt of Truth

Truth can be translated from the Greek word alētheia.

It means what is true in any matter under consideration.

Truly. In truth. According to the truth.

John 14:6

“I am the way, and the truth, and the life.”

The greatest truth in the world is the gospel of Jesus Christ.

Romans 5:8

“But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” We have to believe it.

That is our only hope.

It is the truth we cling to in a broken world.

We must know the truth.

We must sit under the truth.

We must be fastened to the truth.

Fastened

Paul does not say simply to put on the belt.

He says to fasten it.

To fasten something is to tighten it down securely so that it cannot slip free.

The belt keeps the soldier ready. It helps keep the other pieces of armor in place. It allows the soldier to move freely.

It keeps things from getting in the way and causing the soldier to trip.

The enemy is always trying to make us trip and fall.

It is a common scheme of the devil.

Fasten the belt and stay ready.

The Breastplate of Righteousness

Righteousness

Right standing with God.

2 Timothy 3:16–17

Scripture teaches, corrects, trains, and equips the man of God for every good work.

The breastplate covered the body of the soldier from the neck to the thighs. It protected the heart.

The believer's heart is focused upon the Lord Jesus Christ and His righteousness. That focus must be protected.

The enemy wants to take out the heart of the believer.

The sign of the Christian soldier is righteousness.

When a man is saved, God imputes the righteousness of Jesus Christ to him. God counts him righteous.

This piece is given to us at the moment of salvation.

It protects our hearts from what looms ahead.

But we should continue to strive for righteousness.

Matthew 5:6

“Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied.”

God provides us different strengths and different pieces of armor to protect and equip us. Now we have:

● The belt of truth.
● The breastplate of righteousness.

Point 6: Be Ready to Advance with the Gospel

Verse 15

“And, as shoes for your feet, having put on the readiness given by the gospel of peace.”

The Shoes of the Gospel of Peace

Peace is:

● Everlasting.
● Unending.
● Given.
● Not earned.
● Not worked for.

Philippians 4:7

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.”

The sandals were a sign of readiness.

Readiness to march.

Readiness to do battle.

The Roman sandals were made with nails that gripped the ground firmly, even when the ground was sloping or slippery.

The sign of the Christian soldier is readiness.

A readiness to march.

A readiness to bear witness to the gospel.

Wherever the Christian soldier's feet take him, he shares the gospel.

The gospel firmly grounds a world reeling under the weight of desperate need and conflict. We are headed into enemy territory.

We are sharing the good news of Christ with all nations in these evil days. Repeated Emphasis

Throughout this passage, Paul repeatedly emphasizes the need to:

● Put on the armor.
● Put on the whole armor.
● Stand against the enemy.
● Stand firm.

The repetition is intentional.

The Christian life is not passive.

We are called to prepare.

We are called to stand.

We are called to fight.

We are called to remain faithful.

Conclusion

John MacArthur reminds us that the Christian's practice can be illustrated by a car. The first three chapters of Ephesians describe the car—its engine and its capabilities. The second three chapters are the road map the car is to follow.

The first three chapters define believers as high-powered individuals.

The second three chapters show them where to go with their power.

The ignition switch is represented by the strengthening of the Spirit in the inner man. As the Spirit fills and controls the believer, the power plant is turned on.

He can then begin to move out in obedience to follow the road map that God has given. The Christian life is a blessing.

The Christian life is prosperous.

The Christian life is abundant.

The Christian life is peaceful.

But the Christian life is also a battle.

Those who ignore the call and the battle raging around them are doomed to become casualties. So:

Be strong in the Lord.

Put on the whole armor of God.

Know your enemy.

Prepare for the evil day.

Fasten yourself to truth.

Protect your heart with righteousness. And be ready to advance with the gospel of peace.

Prepare for War.
