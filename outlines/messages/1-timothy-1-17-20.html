Standing Firm in the Faith

There was a time before GPS when ships navigated by charts, compasses, and lighthouses. A captain didn't intentionally run his ship onto the rocks. In fact, many shipwrecks happened with experienced sailors. All it took was a slight change in course—a few degrees off, an ignored warning, a neglected compass—and over miles of open water, that tiny deviation became catastrophic. The captain often didn't realize he was in danger until he heard the sound of waves crashing against the rocks beneath him. By then, it was too late.

Take the Titanic. They underestimated what a little iceberg could do until they met what was under the surface and by then it was too late.

Spiritually, that's how shipwreck happens.

Today Paul's covering exactly that. How losing focus and priority on the mission of the Gospel can shipwreck your faith.

Main Point: The glory of God compels believers to faithfully persevere in the fight of faith.

The King (v.17)

17 To the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever.[] Amen.

It’s been a while, but the first thing we see coming out of the previous verses where Paul elaborates and expresses the grace of God is a doxology!

Q: What is doxology?

A: It’s an abrupt expression of praise towards God.

We’ve seen Paul do this a couple of times in books like Ephesians (3:20-21) and Philippians (4:20). He does it a handful of times in other books like Romans and Galatians.

But why?... A Better question would be why not?!

Why wouldn’t Paul, formerly Saul a blasphemer, prosecutor, enemy of the church burst into praise over the mercy he’s received from v.16?

Notice Paul doesn't move from theology to duty without worship. Grace produces praise. The more Paul thinks about what Christ saved him from, the less he can contain his worship.

Paul begins in reference to four distinct attributes of God

King of the ages

Not just king over people, but time and history itself.

While we didn’t go too far, this may sound familiar to the “Ancient of Days” we went over in Daniel ch7. (Same guy btw.)

Every century, every empire, every kingdom, every ruler, every election, every civilization…

Every earthly king and kingdom dies and falls away, but God’s reign never ends.

Nothing exists or ever will exist outside His reign.

He’s not king of history, he’s king over history, and tomorrow. He’s sovereign over every age.

Immortal

Greek: ἀφθάρτῳ (aphthartō) (AF-thar-tos)

Meaning: incorruptible, imperishable, unable to decay.

That which is, incorruptible. He has no seed of corruption, no seed of aging, deterioration, or decay within His being. God cannot die. He alone has immortality

Invisible

Greek: ἀόρατος (ahoratos) (ah-or--at-os)

Meaning: Invisible, Unseen

No one has ever seen God in His full essence.

John 1:18

"No one has ever seen God..."

The Only God

Paul is attacking paganism without naming it.

Ephesus worshiped: Artemis, Caesar, countless idols

In a city full of competing gods and emperor worship, Paul reminds Timothy there is only one true God worthy of worship.

He is the only living and true God, the only God who possesses intelligence and wisdom, who can truthfully interact with the world and save people.

“be honor and glory forever and ever.[] Amen.”

Honor (timē)(tee-may'):

a valuing by which the price is fixed (meaning it never changes), honour which belongs or is shown to one

Assigning worth, showing respect, recognizing authority or dignity.

Glory (Doxa):

splendour, brightness, a most glorious condition, most exalted state.

Both of which God deserves above all else in our lives.

The Charge (vv.18-19a)

18 This charge I entrust to you, Timothy, my child, in accordance with the prophecies previously made about you, that by them you may wage the good warfare, 19 holding faith and a good conscience.

The language Paul is using here is militaristic. It’s a battle.

“This Charge” greek being parangelia: announcement, a proclaiming or giving a message to, a charge, a command.

Donald Guthrie says, “The ministry is not a matter to be trifled with, but an order from the commander-in-chief.”

It’s not over politics or cultural shift, it’s the battle to protect sound doctrine. That the Gospel is a battle. If this were not the case, Ephesians 6 would’ve called The Word “the high five of the spirit” not “The Sword of the spirit”. Ephesian 6 will also tell you about the armor we wear. Belt of truth, breastplate of righteousness, shoes of the Gospel, shield of faith, and helmet of salvation.

You don’t wear armor to play dates, you wear it when you expect to fight.

This isn’t Ephesians ch6 today so I’ll keep this short, but often believers forget the urgency and demand of what we’re called to when you put your faith in Christ. Whoever told you it was easy and you wouldn’t lose anything because of the Gospel lied to you. But what you gain is far more than you could ever lose.

“in accordance with the prophecies previously made about you”

Q: What is Paul talking about? What Prophecies could he be referring to?

Scholars debate this topic. However most like Knight, Mounce, Towner, MacArthur, etc, seem to agree off what we’re told later in the scripture.

1 Timothy 4:14

14 Do not neglect the gift you have, which was given you by prophecy when the council of elders laid their hands on you.

Bonus confirmation in 2 Timothy 1:6

"I remind you to fan into flame the gift of God, which is in you through the laying on of my hands."

These do not amount, compare, nor could even sit in the same category as the prophecies of Christ. These prophecies were descriptive, not determinative. This is an important distinction. The New Testament never portrays prophecy as replacing obedience. The prophecies didn't make Timothy faithful. They affirmed God's work already evident. Timothy still had to fight, endure, hold faith, and keep a good conscience. Even after receiving the prophecy, he still had responsibilities.

It’s no different than for me to look at *pick person* and say *encouraging ‘prophesy’*.

While we should be cautious about making direct comparisons with apostolic-era prophecy, the practical encouragement is similar: God's people publicly affirmed Timothy's gift and calling. We should do the same for each other.

It’s Paul, in a sense, saying people are counting on you and routing for you, don’t give up!

Looking deeper than this is rather speculative. But it does go to show what you say about to build up and encourage other believers matter more than you think.

“that by them you may wage the good warfare, 19 holding faith and a good conscience.”

Q: What does it mean to wage good warfare?

As we previously discussed, ministry isn’t one fight, it’s a life long campaign. What for? Truth. Not your truth, my truth, this truth, or that truth… but THE truth. The Word of God.

Hated by many, mocked and mimicked by even more. Critically acclaimed and debated. But only because it’s true. From God, brought to life through Jesus Christ, him crucified, and resurrected. But that’s why! People would love to never hear of this book again because of what it reveals, things about ourselves we don’t wanna admit. Here we’ve previously called it a mirror. Don’t like what you see? Neither do I. Good! That’s the point! Not everyone will see it that way though, hints… the battle. Some see it is judgemental, controlling, a joy killer… all the same things people call a good doctor who tells them their lifestyle is killing them.

Holding faith:

It is the faith that the minister holds in Christ, in the very teachings of Christ and of the Word of God. As ministers fight the spiritual battles of this life, they are to cling to the commands, instructions, and words of their commander-in-chief. They are not to turn away from the commands and words—not even for a minute. Notice Paul doesn't tell Timothy to win every battle. He tells him to keep holding on tightly to the message of Jesus Christ.

Good Conscience:

having an internal moral awareness of right and wrong that aligns with God's Word, free from guilt. It is powered by the Holy Spirit and maintained through faith in Jesus Christ, quick confession of sin, and sincere love for God and others.

A violated conscience ignored long enough eventually becomes a seared conscience. We’ll see this later in ch.4.

Acts 24:16 Paul says

"I always take pains to have a clear conscience toward both God and man."

It’s rather important that like Timothy, we heed this charge as well. In accordance with the “prophesies” upon us, waging good warfare, holding faith with good conscience. As believers, failing to do so could leave us like our last point we see here in the text…

The Shipwrecked (vv.19b-20)

By rejecting this, some have made shipwreck of their faith, 20 among whom are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I have handed over to Satan that they may learn not to blaspheme.

Top 10 most terrifying verses from Paul

Before we dive into that, first let’s discuss who Hymenaeus and Alexander are,

Hymenaeus

2 Timothy 2:16-18

16 But avoid irreverent babble, for it will lead people into more and more ungodliness, 17 and their talk will spread like gangrene. Among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus, 18 who have swerved from the truth, saying that the resurrection has already happened. They are upsetting the faith of some.

Alexander

2 Timothy 4:14-15

14 Alexander the coppersmith did me great harm; the Lord will repay him according to his deeds. 15 Beware of him yourself, for he strongly opposed our message.

We read that these men are among those who rejected the former from verses 18-19a. As such, Paul says they’ve made shipwrecked their faith.

Mind you, shipwreck is never the first decision. It’s the consequence of many decisions before.

The rejection Paul's referring to in the greek is apōtheō (ap-o-theh'-om-ahee)

Meaning to thrust away, push away, repel

Let's talk about slipping and back sliding for a moment. As we’ve discussed in the past, this often happens when you take Jesus off your priority list for various reasons. You get “busy” or you “just have a lot going on” and your disciplines of faith (reading, memorizing, studying, praying, fasting) begin to lack. Sin creeps in the cracks and spaces you leave open to attack in your life. You didn’t choose it, if anything you often think you’re far from it. Maybe it takes a good friend to tell you some hard truth for you to even realize it. A longer talk for another time, but the point is, it's unintentional. It's our flesh gaining the upper hand for a moment till we run back to Jesus when we realize.

This is not what Paul is referring to…

This is deliberate, a conscious choice.

LMW says

“The warning is frightening. Conscience can be rejected, and faith can be shipwrecked. Conscience says that something is wrong and should not be done, but conscience is ignored and subdued, turned away from, and denied. When we continue to push away or subdue our conscience, something terrible happens: our faith is shipwrecked—broken to pieces and destroyed. We must live as Scripture dictates: righteously and godly. If we do not live righteously and godly, then we weaken our faith and, soon thereafter, it is dashed upon the storms of evil, worldliness, and false doctrine. Our faith is shipwrecked— all because we pushed our conscience aside, refusing to listen to its call to live righteously and godly.”

I’m sure we all had someone come to mind.

Let’s go ahead and talk about the elephant in the room…

Does this verse point to, in a sense, loss of salvation? Why is Paul sending people to hell?

The topic itself has been debated amongst theologians and scholars for hundreds of years. Verses like the one we see today along with passages in Hebrews 6 and 10, John 15, 2 Peter, and more used to back the stance that salvation can be lost. A real rabbit hole of a topic, but not our point today..

However for the verse before us today,

I believe and I think you can see as well that this isn’t what Paul is referring to. Instead it points to the probability of discipline or at worse lack of born again life transforming faith to begin with.

Yes, there is excommunication. He sent them out into the world that Paul depicts in 1 Corinthaians 4 As belonging to Satan. He kicked them out of the church.

But Paul was just closing the door on these gentlemen, whipping his hands clean of them. Saying “ oh well, lost cause” then why at the end of verse 20 does it say, “that they may learn not to blaspheme.” ?

The disciplinary point of view is supported from the fact Paul in association has done this another time in scripture.

1 Corinthians 5:5 when Paul's addressing the man who has his fathers wife.

“you are to deliver this man to Satan for the destruction of the flesh, so that his spirit may be saved in the day of the Lord.”

LMW supports this point of view by saying, “The discipline means more than excommunication. It is the miraculous subjection of the person to the power of Satan, to be terrified or tormented by him, that they might repent and return to Christ. ▸ The idea is that Paul and the church prayed for some circumstance or difficulty to arise in their lives that would stir them to repent. Scripture teaches that God, just like an earthly father, disciplines us when we sin and fail to obey Him. In fact, it is impossible to sin and escape chastisement. People reap what they sow.”

God loves us deeply, but is sovereign enough to discipline us as his children for our own good.

If you think this isn’t scriptural, name a man in the bible.

David

Hunted by Saul.

Betrayed by friends.

Lost a child.

Faced rebellion from Absalom.

Jonah

Storm at sea.

Swallowed by a great fish.

Nebuchadnezzar

Lost his kingdom.

Lived like an animal for a period.

Job, Moses, Peter.

Christianity is not for the week.

If all this is so, would it not be right for prayer to reflect scripture?

Paul desiring to send people to hell would contradict Christ's mission. But Paul, desiring people to turn to Christ, even if it takes them suffering to the point of submission is something we don’t often see and talk about.

When we pray it’s usually a, “Oh God, Please help them know you.” or “I pray they begin to take your word seriously”.

Never “God I pray your word keeps them awake at night till they know you as King.” or “God, I pray you take their occupation away if that’s what they worship more than you.” it’s “Lord, if that's what it takes to bring them to repentance, take it away”

It’s not praying for harm out of anger or bitterness, it's praying God intervenes in someone's life to save them at all costs.

What about for yourself? Do you ever pray the hard stuff over your old life? God if the job I have now doesn’t honor you, move me” “God, if the relationship I’m in now doesn’t have you in the center, separate us.” “God, if I’m distracted by idols in my life, take them from me.”

God loves us deeply, but is sovereign enough to discipline us as his children for our own good.

I can tell you first hand, prayers like that work.

Many of you know by relationship history (it applies), few of you know about my dad.

He was the most evil man I ever knew. Alcoholic with multiple DUIs, drug abuser, divorced 3 times, 4 kids, 2 he wasn’t allowed to see, 1 he’s never met, then me. Laid hands on my mother, fought with me… he hurt people in ways than just words and actions. My mom tried 20 years of her life to change him. Chance, after chance, after chance. Till finally it got bad enough that no chances could be reconciled. My dad got what he wanted (in a sense), he was handed over to Satan.

But God used it for my dads good. The Lord saved my dad in the middle of the mess he made after all these years, my dad repented from all of his ways, and he’s the best dad I could’ve asked for. If I would’ve known now what I didn’t know then, maybe I wouldn’t have prayed for him to change. I would’ve prayed for him into the hands of Satan sooner.

Modern Christianity would tell you this theology is harsh, till you remember… it’s a battle.

Application:

Worship the King before you fight the battle.

Hold tightly to Christ before you slowly drift away.

Don't ignore a guilty conscience—respond to God's conviction quickly.

Fight for truth every day, because no one drifts closer to Jesus by accident.
