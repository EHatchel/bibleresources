Mentally and physically draining your strength. ** **This is the amount of conflict that Paul is having currently at this present time.

When was the last time you had a spiritual conflict over someone you haven't met? For Paul this seemed to be common.

Is he struggling because of his imprisonment? Or maybe even his age? Health concerns? It seems to be that the only times Paul has an internal struggle this grand is when he is dealing with people and the word of God. WHY?

To preach and spread the Gospel

To make the Mystery Revealed for all, that jews and gentiles are equal under Christ.

Your purpose in life is to spread this message and now it is being twisted and tainted. It is my belief that Paul has very good reason to be upset.

Deceive with plausible arguments - how do we combat this. This is a warning that will happen. He is not saying it in case it happens, but rather when it does. The way to avoid deceit is knowing the truth.

Spiritual illiteracy leads to deceit.

These people weren't taught deceit, they were deceived when they were taught. All of this from spiritual illiteracy. One man was spiritually illiterate who taught and deceived people and was passed down generation after generation.

This is the reason for the commonly talked about question i ask, What does believe mean?

Got questions put it this way, Don't just take it from me: Do you believe these things? If so, great, but believing the facts about Jesus is only part of the equation. Biblical faith/belief is far more than believing certain things to be true. Biblical saving faith is also trusting/relying on those facts.

A chair is a good illustration. You can look at a chair and believe it is made of materials strong enough to support your weight, and you can believe that it was assembled correctly. But that is not biblical faith. Biblical faith is sitting in the chair. It is actually relying on the chair to hold your weight off the ground.

Are you trusting that Jesus is your Savior? Are you relying on His death as the full payment for your sin debt? Are you depending on His resurrection as the guarantee that you, too, will be raised to eternal life after death? Not that it could ever happen, but if the “chair” of Jesus Christ were pulled out from beneath you, spiritually speaking, would you hit the ground, or are you also relying on things in addition to the chair?

If you understand and believe what the Bible says about Jesus, and if you are trusting in those truths as the basis for salvation—you are saved! You “believe in Jesus” in the biblical sense.

V.5

Colossians 2:5

'For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing to see your good order and the firmness of your faith in Christ. '

Absent in the body obviously is referring to Paul being in prison. In Spirit he is present. Through his prayer over the church and the people within it. He prays for them continually.

The word "order" (taxin) means to maintain military discipline, array, and arrangement; to hold a solid front (The Amplified New Testament); to hold the military line unbroken and intact (A.T. Robertson, Word Pictures in the New Testament, Vol.4, p.489).

The word "stedfastness" (stereōma) means to stand fast and persevere; to be immoveable, steady, and unyielding; to never crack, give in, or back up. This, too, is a military word. A.T. Robertson says that it is "the solid part of the line which can and does stand the attack" of the seduction.

Note that the believers of the Colossian church were being attacked by false teaching even as Paul was writing to them. But they were responding like a victorious army. They were maintaining their discipline and holding their order and standing fast. Note also the importance of the minister's encouragement: Paul says that he was with them in spirit, joying and watching them gain the victory over the false teachers.

William Barclay notes that "Faith begins with receptivity. It begins when a man is at least willing to listen to the message of the truth. It goes on to mental assent. A man first hears and then agrees that this is true. But mental assent need not issue in action. Many a man knows very well that something is true, but does not change his actions to meet that knowledge. The final stage is when this mental assent becomes total surrender. In full-fledged faith, a man hears the Christian message, agrees that it is true, and then casts himself upon it in a life of total yieldedness."

1. To Walk In Christ

6 Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, 7 rooted and built up in him and established in the faith, just as you were taught, abounding in thanksgiving.

Q: Who here has received Christ?

You received Christ Jesus, so walk in him.

If you were here for Ephesians, you should already know a lot when Paul says to walk in the Lord.

Q: What does it mean to walk in Christ?

With the word therefore building off the previous set of passages, Paul is saying to not be deluded and deceived, therefore walk. He addresses the problem and provides the solution, Christ. You believe, therefore walk.

“Therefore, as you received Christ” Well how did they receive him? What if they had the wrong idea from the start?

Epaphrus, who is believed to have received Christ during Paul's work in Ephesus, gave it to them. Like Paul, Epaphrus believed, received, then set out to deliver God’s word, great commission style. One could say it was Epaphrus who led the people astray, but if that was the case then why would Epaphrus be reaching out for help from Paul? It would be easier to believe, given that colosse was under roman control by 130 BCE, that their heresy was caused by Roman culture. So what am I getting at? The point I'm trying to highlight is based on what we know, we can infer that it was Epaphrus who brought Christ to the regions and his faithfulness even in the face of this conflict was true. They received him as savior, Lord above all, a way of true salvation through faith in him and his word. Not what they’ve blended Roman traditions with.

Therefore, Paul tells them to walk in such a manor.

As we’ve discussed before in Col 1:10 when Paul speaks on walking worthy, Walk (Peripateo) being presentence infers that it is a habitual and currently intentional action. It’s your lifestyle, how you conduct your life. But notice it says “in him”, not “with him in mind” or “in a way that people can kinda tell your Christian”, but IN HIM. His word, his way, and most importantly his will. Isaiah 55:8-9 The Lord says that his ways and thoughts are as the heavens high above our ways and thoughts. The people of Colosse ‘thought’ in their own will that they could mix or add to their version of Christ as opposed to walk in Him and his perfect word. They stopped growing.

Skip Heitzig quoted Charles Haddon Spurgeon:

“The Christian like is like climbing a hill of ice - you can’t slide up: if you want to know how to backslide: stop going forward - cease going upward and you’ll go downward out of necessity. You can never stand still!”

Then in 7, he elaborates to say being rooted and built up (once again) IN HIM, established in the faith.

We all may think of something different when we hear “rooted”, tree roots seem to be the most popular. It makes sense given the plant analogy seen throughout scripture like someone planted the seed of faith for those who hear it (Mark 4:26-29) (Luke 8:11), then God alone provides the growth (1 Corinthians 3:6-8), then now in Colossians similar back in Ephesians 3:17, like a well aged and tested tree were to be rooted and built up in him. As a tree with roots, buried deep in the ground of his word and strong in understanding of Christ’s identity.

Established in the faith. Unmovable faith. Faith in Christ so deep, we’re not swayed by what we hear and see. 2 Corinthians 5:7 “For we WALK by faith, not by sight” Always knowing the truth found in Christ and his Word.

Equally important but often forgotten, Paul adds to abound in thanksgiving.

Precept Austin listed 13 verses (Out of hundreds) on the topic of being thankful. Of those 13, Paul was the author (at least confirmed to be) 10 times. It’s obvious that across all the books, thanksgiving is crucial in each one. Paul wrote to the Philippians that he was thankful for them and they should be thankful towards the Lord, he wrote to the Ephesians to be thankful instead of pointless arguing, then now encourages the Colossians to be thankful that God really is above all.

Being thankful for Christ alone, but also what God has given you and his control has the power to overcome a lot of the issues we as believers can face. Especially fear, doubt, discouragement, and the feelings that possess us in troubling times.

Philippians 4:6-7:

6 do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God. 7 And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

The act of being thankful, even when it’s hard, is obedience to say my situation might not be good but God is. For the colossians, Paul encourages them to be thankful in order that they give recognition to what Christ has already done. Allowing Him to be what they can confidently cling to as the truth, not what the world or a society's culture tries to say it is. It’s to fix their gaze back on him, not the traditions they let creep in.

2. ** To Not Be Taken Captive**

8 See to it that no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit, according to human tradition, according to the elemental spirits[a] of the world, and not according to Christ. 9 For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, 10 and you have been filled in him, who is the head of all rule and authority.

8 See to it no one takes you captive by philosophy and empty deceit

Philosophy, greek word philosophia, meaning love (and pursuit) of wisdom.

In 2025 people call this finding “your truth”. What’s true to you, in your wisdom and understanding. The truth about who you say you are and want to do in life. What rules you desire to play by. Those who are trying to discover the “meaning of life”.

Then there’s empty deceit. If you’ve ever been truly deceived. You’ll remember believing what you were told despite there being obvious flaws you only acknowledged after the fact. That’s why empty (or some translations say ‘Vain’) deceit. It sounds and looks like the truth, but there is nothing inside.

Paul writes these things are according to two forums of heretic, human tradition and elemental spirits.

Q: What is tradition?

Human tradition, the things man started the habitual act of and not God.

9

But for him. (to recap 8) “See to it that no one takes you captive..” Why? For in him the WHOLE fulness of deity dwells bodily.

Preachers and commentaries address that part of the heresy spreading in Colossia at the time is that is the Gnostic belief that the fullness of God (Planoma - Fullness/Complete) was divided in different ranks and statures and Jesus was just one of them.

Paul's telling them that Christ was the whole thing. The complete fullness. The planoma. Not one of many, but just one.
