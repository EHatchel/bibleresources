Ephesians 3:14–21
An Astounding Prayer

Alan Redpath was once asked at a preachers' conference:

“How do you recognize a spiritual man?”

His answer was short and simple:

“Bent knees, wet eyes.”

This was a common posture for Paul.

Paul was a man of faith and purpose, leadership and truth. Throughout his letters to the churches, he continually prayed for them.

Paul understood something about spiritual power and posture.

In this passage, Paul prays for the churches to understand:

● The power of Christ.
● The love of Christ.
● Inner strength.
● Spiritual stability.
● Fulfillment.
● Gratitude.

We often pray for the things that are most important to us.

So ask yourself:

What do you pray for?

1. The Posture of Prayer

Ephesians 3:14–15

“For this reason I bow my knees before the Father…”

Paul bows his knees.

This shows us his posture of humility.

When I pray, I often say:

“We bow our heads beneath You because You are supreme and almighty. You are our guide and Savior.”

That is the heart posture Paul is showing here.

Kneeling and bowing are outward signs of humility and surrender.

Paul understood:

I am not in control. I am dependent on the Father.

What Are We Praying For?

Everyone cares about how they live and the people around them.

We pray for:

● Physical strength.
● Finances.
● Decisions.
● Relationships.
● Sickness.
● Wisdom.
● Insight.

But Paul understood that if the inner man is spiritually strengthened, the outer man can endure what life brings.

The spiritual condition of the inner man affects the way we live externally. This is partly why Paul rarely prays for himself.

And when he does, he often prays for his purpose in Christ.

So ask yourself:

How often do I search my spiritual life and understanding rather than only searching my personal and worldly circumstances?

I Only Kneel for God

There was a basketball player named Jonathan Isaac who played for the Orlando Magic.

During the COVID-19 era, his teammates and coaches decided to kneel during the national anthem as part of the Black Lives Matter movement.

Jonathan Isaac decided to remain standing.

He was nervous about his decision and spoke with his pastor.

His pastor told him:

“If you stand for Christ, He will stand for you.”

Jonathan Isaac decided to stand.

Afterward, the media criticized him.

When asked about his decision, he gave a simple answer:

“I only kneel for God.”

He had decided that he would not bow to the customs and pressures of the world. He would stand for Christ.

And he would only kneel before God.

That is a powerful picture of faith.

There are times when we must bow before God in humility.

And there are times when we must stand firm for Christ.

Both require the right posture.

Adopted by the Father

Ephesians 3:14–15

Paul says that every family in heaven and on earth is named from the Father. Through Christ, we have become partakers in the gospel.

We have been adopted by the Father into His family and household. We have the Spirit of adoption.

We belong to God.

Therefore, we can approach Him with confidence.

“Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need.”

— Hebrews 4:16

Because of Christ, we are allowed to draw near.

We have boldness.

We have access.

We are not outsiders.

We are children of God.

2. Paul's Five Prayers

Ephesians 3:16–19

Paul now becomes very specific in his prayer.

Prayer 1: Be Strengthened in the Inner Man Verse 16

Paul does not pray for the Ephesians to receive a miracle.

He does not pray for them to cast out demons.

He does not pray for them to heal the sick.

He does not pray for them to raise the dead.

He prays for their inner spirit.

He prays that they would become mature Christians.

This should teach us something:

Spiritual maturity begins with the inner man.

Our inner man should always be growing.

We practice this through self-examination.

We should evaluate:

● The choices we are making.
● The way we live.
● The way we speak.
● What we prioritize.
● How we spend our time.
● How we spend our money.
● Where we put our effort.

A study discussed by psychologist Tasha Eurich found that although many people believe they are self-aware, only a much smaller percentage are truly self-aware.

Self-awareness is connected to better performance, leadership, and decision-making.

Paul understood the importance of looking inward nearly 2,000 years before modern research discussed it.

We must ask:

What is happening inside of me?

Prayer 2: That Christ Would Dwell in Your Heart Verse 17

Paul prays that Christ would dwell in their hearts through faith.

Christ desires fellowship with each of us.

At salvation, Christ enters our lives.

The heart is the center of our spiritual life.

Christ is not merely someone we know about.

He is someone who lives within us and works in us.

One commentary summarizes the progression this way:

The Father strengthens believers through the Spirit so that Christ may be completely at home in their hearts.

Paul has now brought us through the Trinity:

● The Father is addressed.
● The Spirit strengthens us.
● Christ dwells in our hearts.

The more we are strengthened by the Spirit, the more we become like Jesus. The more we become like Jesus, the more He is at home in our hearts.

So ask yourself:

Does Christ feel at home in my heart?

Prayer 3: Rooted and Grounded in Love

Verse 17

Paul uses two powerful images.

Grounded

The word means:

● To lay a foundation.
● To make stable.
● To establish.

A foundation provides stability.

“Righteousness and justice are the foundation of your throne.”

— Psalm 89:14

“For no one can lay any foundation other than what has been laid down. That foundation is Jesus Christ.”

— 1 Corinthians 3:11

The love of Christ is a foundation that makes our walk stable.

Electrical Grounding

I recently had electrical training at work for eleven hours.

One thing that stood out to me was the importance of grounding.

Proper grounding protects an electrical system during a power surge or lightning strike. It prevents electricity from traveling where it should not go.

It protects equipment from damage.

The ground provides safety and stability.

The same is true spiritually.

When your life is grounded in Christ:

● You have stability.
● You have protection.
● You have a foundation.
● You have direction.

Christ is the foundation that protects us from being destroyed by every outside force.

Rooted

The word means:

● To strike roots.
● To strengthen with roots.
● To make firm.
● To establish.

Think about a tree.

The roots hold the tree in place.

They keep the trunk, branches, and fruit connected to the tree.

The deeper the roots, the stronger the tree.

The same is true of our relationship with Christ.

If we are rooted in the love of Christ, we become stable in Him.

Prayer 4: To Understand the Love of Christ

Ephesians 3:18–19

Paul prays that believers would have the strength to comprehend the width, length, depth, and height of Christ's love.

These dimensions may describe the vastness of God's love.

One way to understand them is:

The Width

The width of God's grace reaches across the division between Jews and Gentiles. God's grace is wide enough to save all who come to Christ.

The Length

The length of God's love stretches from eternity to eternity.

God's love existed before we did and continues forever.

The Depth

The depth of God's love is seen in Ephesians 2.

We were dead in sin.

We were spiritually separated from God.

Christ came down into the depths of our sin and corruption to save us.

The Height

The height is seen in Ephesians 2:6.

We were not only made alive with Christ.

We were raised with Him and seated with Him in the heavenly places. These dimensions are so vast that they are beyond our ability to fully comprehend.

To Know the Love That Surpasses Knowledge

Paul prays that we would know the love of Christ even though it surpasses knowledge. This seems like a contradiction.

How can we know something that surpasses knowledge?

Paul is talking about more than simply knowing facts about Jesus. We are not merely supposed to know about His love.

We are supposed to know His love personally.

This requires relationship.

Someone can know facts about you without actually knowing you. The same is true of Jesus.

You can know facts about Christ without truly knowing Christ.

But the love of Christ is experienced through relationship with Him. This settles the question of unconditional love.

We need someone who knows everything about us and still refuses to reject us. There is only one who can do that:

Jesus Christ.

Love Is the Foundation of the Christian Life

Jesus said:

“You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.”

And:

“You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”

The greatest commandments are centered on love.

Love is central to the Christian life.

The fruit of the Spirit is connected to love:

● Joy is love singing.
● Peace is love resting.
● Patience is love enduring.
● Kindness is love's touch.
● Goodness is love's character.
● Faithfulness is love's habit.
● Gentleness is love's self-forgetfulness.
● Self-control is love holding the reins.

Without love, there is no true evidence of spiritual maturity.

The love of Christ is:

● Unknowable in its fullness.
● Unsearchable in its riches.
● Unspeakable in its joy.
● Untrackable in its ways.
● Unexplainable in its grace.
● Unfathomable in its peace.

Christ Himself is unsurpassable.

Prayer 5: To Be Filled With the Fullness of God

Verse 19

Paul prays that the Ephesians would be:

“Filled with all the fullness of God.”

To be filled means to be completely controlled or dominated by something. If someone is filled with anger, anger dominates them.

If someone is filled with bitterness, bitterness controls them.

Paul prays that believers would be filled with the fullness of God.

That God would dominate our thoughts, actions, desires, priorities, and lives.

The Doxology of Paul

Verse 20

“Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think…” A doxology is a short expression of praise to God.

Paul has just spent several verses praying.

Then he begins praising God.

Why?

Because God is able to do more than we can ask or imagine.

The power that works within us is the power of God.

The Holy Spirit works in us continually.

Even when we are blinded, confused, or unable to understand, God continues working in us.

He is shaping our lives to look more like Christ. God can do more than we can ask.

God can do more than we can think.

God can do more than we can imagine.

Gratitude

Verse 21

God is worthy of praise and honor.

He is the Creator of all things.

He is the Lord of love.

We are His image-bearers.

We have been blessed beyond what we deserve. We have been saved from our trespasses and sins. We have been adopted into God's family. We have access to the Father.

We have the Holy Spirit.

We have Christ dwelling within us.

We have every reason to be grateful.

So ask yourself:

Am I a complainer or am I grateful?

Praise belongs to God through every generation. Forever and ever.

Amen.

Main Point

Paul opens this passage in prayer.

He prays for five things:

1. That believers would be strengthened by the power of the Spirit in the inner man. 2. That Christ would dwell in their hearts through faith.

3. That they would have strength to understand the vastness of God's love. 4. That they would know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge. 5. That they would be filled with the fullness of God.

Application: How Should We Pray?

A — Adoration

Praise God for who He is.

C — Confession

Admit your sin and your need for God.

T — Thanksgiving

Thank God for what He has done.

S — Supplication

Bring your needs and requests before God.

But remember what Paul teaches us in Ephesians 3:

Do not only pray for what is happening around you. Pray for what is happening within you.

A strong spiritual life begins with the inner man.

A believer who is strengthened inwardly can stand when life becomes difficult outwardly.

Bent knees. Wet eyes. A strengthened inner man. A heart rooted and grounded in the love of Christ.
