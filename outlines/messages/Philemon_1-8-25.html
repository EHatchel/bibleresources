V.8-22 Pauls Imitation

V.8-10

Accordingly is translated in Greek to therefore, it is therefore because of Philemon’s faithfulness of refreshing souls through the gospel that he would rather reach out an appeal through love rather than commanding Philemon.

Paul has the boldness in Christ to have the confidence to command people to follow Christ ,but his friendship and love for philemon lead him to ask instead of tell.

The appeal , Sending onesimus back and appealing to let him just be a brother in Christ. An old man - see precept austin.

Father to son . Paul calls himself a father of many people throughout scripture , Philemon, Timothy, and onesimus, as well as the entirety of the Church in Corinth. This is not a literal father, but rather a figurative and spiritual guide in Christianity.

V.11-12

Formerly he was useless to you, but now he is indeed useful to you and to me.)” Slaves were seen as nothing/useless without a master in Roman culture. Formerly useless = nonbeliever

Now he is indeed useful = believer

Onesimus’ name meant profitable, useful.

Paul is expressing that Onesimus was once useless to Philemon in his new goal of growing the kingdom of heaven. But now through Christ he is useful to Phielmon and has been useful to Paul. We see in verse 13 Paul saying that Onesimus was so useful he would have been glad to keep him. (see verse 13)

Philemon would host church groups in his home to contribute to the Lord after his conversion. Paul shows that Onesimus now can be useful in Philemon’s goal of advancing the gospel in Colasse.

We before Christ were the same way. If we continued down our path of sin and disobedience to God, who are we more than flesh and bones living on earth? We are dead, nothing, and useless.

With Christ in our lives, we become alive, son/daughter of God, and useful for the kingdom. You then have a new purpose for your life.

“For this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.’ And they began to celebrate.” - Luke 15:24

V.12

“I am sending him back to you”

Paul is sending Onesimus back with this letter.

We know Paul can’t deliver this letter as he is on house arrest/imprisonment. “-sending my very heart”

The CSB translation says “I am sending him back to you as a part of myself.”

Very heart or splagchnon

(https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g4698/esv/mgnt/0-1/)(splangkh’-non) = bowels, intestines, (the heart, lungs, liver, etc.)

Paul is using this expression/figure of speech to show how much he cares about Onesimus.

He is not literally sending his heart, but he is figuratively saying that he is delivering himself through Onesimus.

V.13-14

The affection Paul had for Philemon was so great he was fair. Imagine this scenario. You have someone helping you to serve the lord. You find out you know his owner very closely. You have a few options. Don’t say anything to him and continue to serve the kingdom faithfully and could count that as good. On the other hand , doing a favor to Philemon out of love for him to tell him what was going on while Onesimus has been away.

Acts 9:15-16

’But the Lord said to him, “Go, for he is a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles and kings and the children of Israel. For I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name.” ’

Paul is reminded of what Christ did for him and what he does for us. God does not want us to follow out of compulsion but rather out of love for him. This is a lesson on being an imitator of Christ which Paul follows so closely.

“Each one must give as he has decided in his heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.” - 2 Corinthians 9:7

This is THE way a believer should be for their fellow saints.

How hard is it to do something required after being forced to do it?

How much easier is it to do something required out of your own will/heart?

V.15-16

Perhaps this is why he has parted from you, Paul is extending his knowledge of how the Lord works in our lives. Not everything God’s doing in your life is comfortable or happy days, sometimes it is rough and rugged. He is moving you to a place of growth and understanding the will for your life.

This is the case for Onesimus, look at how hard it was deciding to run away from Philemon, as he was a loving and faithful servant of Christ. The Lord used his journey to lead him to Paul to meet Jesus and his need for a savior. Becoming a beloved-brother in Christ to serve the gospel faithfully. Then forever, return to Philemon and serve him and the Lord in ministry.

“Paul refers to the separation as though it were God’s act, brought about, or at least overruled, by him for the lasting benefit of Philemon and Onesimus alike, and indeed for Paul’s benefit too. But for the separation, Onesimus would have remained with Philemon as his slave until their relationship was terminated in some way or other - by emancipation, redemption, resale, or

death. Then the relationship would exist no more. But, thanks to the separation, Onesimus was now a member of Christ, as Philemon already was, and a new and deeper relationship was thus established which would never come to an end.” Pre- Austin

V.17-19

Referring back to verse 12 , sending my very heart. It is just like sending myself. In the same way, receive him as you would me. Adding to that, receive me as you would receive Christ. As a partner in Christ, a fellow-labor, a fellow-worker, a bondservant, fellow-soldier, saint, brother, son-father. Receive him this way as an equal in Christ, just as Jesus came to teach and fulfill.

The gravity of this verse (V.18) is best explained with what we learned in verses 1-7. Agape love. The sin Paul carried was heavy, and his account very large. The Lord met him where he was and wiped it clean. Paul now uses this grace to place grace on Onesimus. Everything he has done past, present, and future, add it to my account. Paul’s accountant must have a rough job.

The severity of Paul’s words of expression here is a perfected picture of the gospel. This small price to pay for Paul has no comparison on what Christ did for all of us. He put everyone’s price on himself and took it to death, covering it with his blood.

Romans 3:23-25

“For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, whom God put forward as a propitiation by his blood, to be received by faith. This was to show God’s righteousness, because in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins.

Propitiation - an expiatory sacrifice ( having power to atone )

Paul has yet again another opportunity to be an imitator of Christ showing love, grace, and sacrificial love. Knowing that the penalty for Onesimus’ sin is death, Paul does not hesitate to say put it on my account and fully live that out.

Verse 19 Being the legal promissory note for Paul to be legally held responsible for the debt he’s claiming to inquire on Onesimus behalf. Giving Philemon not only his reputable word in Christ, but also legal documentation backing it. Back half in reference to the debt Philemon owes Paul from receiving the Gospel through his efforts in the book of Acts.

V.20-22

Refresh - anapauō (Key) Pronunciation : an-ap-ow’-o

1 to cause or permit one to cease from any movement or labour in order to recover and collect his strength.

Paul will greatly benefit from Philemon’s continued work for the Lord, especially if he takes in onesimus as a fellow bondservant of Christ. Easing the mind of Paul in knowing the acceptance of Philemon and the work they can do for the kingdom. Giving him encouragement in knowing he is not alone in his mission of the gospel allowing rest and rebuilding of strength and refreshment of his spirit.

John Macarthur,

You remember to the Philippians in chapter 2, those very wonderful words, chapter 2 verse 2? “Make my joy complete?” How can we do that, Paul? How can we give you joy? “By being of the same mind, maintaining the same love, united in spirit, intent on one purpose, not being selfish or conceited, but humble regarding others more important, and looking on the things of others and not your own.” In other words, Philemon, if you will humble yourself and consider Onesimus more important than yourself, and seek unity, and love, and fellowship, and therefore forgive that man, you will bring me joy.

And so, he’s saying you should be motivated by wanting to be a blessing to others. You should be motivated to forgive because it will rejoice the heart of another. “So,” he says, “let me benefit from you in the Lord.” And then adds, “Refresh my heart in Christ,” again in that spiritual sphere. Bless me, refresh me. You refreshed everybody else back in verse 7; now it’s my turn.

V.21

Paul is confident in Philemon to be obedient to the Lord. He listed Philemon’s characteristics in verses 4-7. We know he is godly, faithful, loving. With the listing of these we can assume that he would be obedient. Paul therefore is still not commanding him,but instead showing his trust and faith that Philemon will obey the Lord’s commands on forgiveness and reconciliation.

V.22

Paul is stating that he plans on returning to Philemon. He is still imprisoned at this point and he is saying that through Philemon’s prayers that he might be delivered to him; to be there through his progress and growth in Christ.

This verse, along with the broader apostolic teaching about the new family God has created in Christ Jesus, shows us that while Scripture never explicitly commands believers to free their slaves, it does create an environment in which owning slaves eventually becomes unthinkable. If we truly understand that other Christians are joint heirs with us in Christ Jesus, full members of the household of God and as valuable as we are in His sight (John 9:1–13; Eph. 3:6), how can we put them below us through buying and selling them as if they were some kind of disposable commodity? Philemon, perhaps more clearly than any other epistle, shows us the radical implications of what it means to live as the community of God’s children in this world.
