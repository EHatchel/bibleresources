Colossians 1:24–29
Faithful in the Calling

1. The Calling To Suffer

24 Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake, and in my flesh I am filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions for the sake of his body, that is, the church, 25 of which I became a minister according to the stewardship from God that was given to me for you, to make the word of God fully known,

What in the world is Paul saying? He rejoices in his suffering?

Go back to Paul's Conversion Acts 9:10-19

“16 For I will show him how much he must suffer for the sake of my name.” Suffer he has, as we’ve seen in 2 Corinthians 11:26-27.

I’m sure many of you have heard me say, in reference to John 15:18, if you’re not being mocked, shunned, isolated by peers, tested by man, emotionally or physically hurt, if you’re NOT SUFFERING, you’re most likely doing it wrong. Jesus said it clear as day in John 16:33: “In the world you will have tribulation.” Not maybe; not only if you live in a bad area like Memphis will you have tribulation. “YOU WILL HAVE TRIBULATION”.

Then Christ says, “BUT!....take heart; I have overcome the world.”

Other translations say “be of good cheer”.

Remember Matthew 5:10: “Blessed are those who are persecuted because of righteousness,

for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.”

This is Paul’s signature style; he must have liked John 16:33 in the way he always has: joy in his suffering. We know this to be true after we went through Philippians. Notice he says “for your sake.” We’ll come back to that in a moment.

“and in my flesh I am filling up what is lacking in Christ's afflictions for the sake of his body, that is, the church,” did anyone else feel a kind of way when they read “I am filling up what is lacking in Christ”?

Q: What did y’all think when reading Paul is “lacking in Christ’s afflictions? I almost felt offended for Christ, as if Paul was saying it wasn’t enough. I immediately had to go to commentaries, and John MacArthur's explanation helped me the most:

"The enemies of Christ were never satisfied with what they did to Jesus. They hated Jesus with an insatiable hate. They wanted to add to His suffering. So, as soon as Jesus ascended back into heaven and wasn't around any more, who did the world attack? The church! (Ed: See Acts 8:1-4+ - Note: although persecuted, they continued preaching!) They began to persecute the church--whipping them, burning them at the stake, throwing them to the lions. Why? Was it because they hated them individually? No, it was because the church stood in the place of Christ. And since Christ wasn't around to hate, they hated the people who stood in His place." (Read Jn 15:18-25)

Paul is NOT saying that the redemptive suffering of Christ, His death on the Cross, is in any way lacking. With His dying breath, Jesus clearly testified, "Tetelestai (It is Finished; Paid in Full)." The afflictions of believers add nothing to Christ's finished work of redemption. However, we will suffer afflictions as we make known Christ's finished work of redemption.”

Now I see… It wasn’t Christ who was lacking; it was the enemies of Christ who were lacking someone to take their hate out on since Christ defeated the grave. Paul says he’s filling up. I’d translate to filling in. It’s sacrificial. Rome wanted to make an example; Paul wanted to be the example. He’s more pleased to suffer so the Church may live and have confidence than to be afraid of his circumstances. How Christlike Paul is to be so sacrificial for God’s people… Notice his priorities in the matter.

There’s one problem though, Paul’s not here anymore. “Lacking in Christ's affliction for the sake of “THE BODY.” Who’s the body again? (Us)

Think about what they did to his body. Lashes, beating him to the point where blood was supposed to be. Stabbing him. Deprived him. Forced him to carry a cross uphill… and we’re called “The body.” They beat his body; now they want the body. You and I. Again, I want it to be clear there will be suffering if you’re truly proclaiming Christ.

25

25 of which I became a minister according to the stewardship from God that was given to me for you, to make the word of God fully known,

This is, at its simplest, what Paul’s calling was. To be a minister, providing stewardship from God for God to make the word of God known.

Q: Define ministry

Q: Who here’s called to ministry? (Make sure literally everyone who’s saved raises their hand)

I thought Skip Heitzig covered this topic really well, so I will use a lot of what he said to break down this verse.

Stewardship. A concept often brought up all throughout the Bible, from Genesis with Abraham and Eliazar all the way to Matthew, where Jesus spoke parables about stewards. A steward, someone who serves to manage one's assets and belongings, is commissioned. Paul is saying, as we know, that he was commissioned by God to serve a purpose.

We are all called to serve a purpose. We are all ministers of God’s word, stewards of God’s will. If you weren’t called to serve, none of us would still be here. If the goal was just to get us into heaven and genuinely nothing else, the moment you gave your life to Christ, you would drop dead. But we are commissioned, a great commission at that. You were born at a certain time, in a certain place, by certain parents, for a certain purpose. Ephesians 2:10: “10 For we are God’s handywork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do.” You have a purpose only you can fulfill. It’s your job to discover that purpose and be faithful. Two purposes we all have as believers are to build up those in the Church and reach unsaved people. That alone will keep you on this earth till the end.

2. The Mystery

26 The mystery hidden for ages and generations but now revealed to his saints. 27 To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Paul really cut to the point this time. Ephesians, he really drew out the mystery to explain it to them.

Q: (Ask newer people) What do they think the mystery is?

The “Mystery” hidden for ages and generations, as some of you remember, is salvation available to all, both jew and gentile.

Mystērion being the Greek word, John MacArthur adds that this word does not have the same connotation as the word we use in English, like a mystery novel of sorts. This Greek word refers to something that was always there and not revealed (or only partially), but only now being revealed.

We could talk about this for hours, but as some may know, just like the New Testament, the Old Testament is pointing to Christ. And we see Christ referred to and directly mentioned throughout it, but almost as if they’re speaking in mysterion. Proverbs 30:3-4: “3 I have not learned wisdom, nor have I knowledge of the Holy One. 4 Who has ascended to heaven and come down? Who has gathered the wind in his fists? Who has wrapped up the waters in a garment? Who has established all the ends of the earth? What is his name, and what is his son's name? Surely you know!”

Similarly, the Old Testament put up a lot of walls on who could be with God directly.

(Tell the story about the person asking how to get in the inner sanctuary to be with God. The veil being torn.)

What was once hidden and reserved for a specific group, of a specific family, of a specific tribe, and a specific area is now available to all. Christ and the hope of glory were a mystery hidden away but now made public on the cross.

3. The Presentation

28 Him we proclaim, warning everyone and teaching everyone with all wisdom, that we may present everyone mature in Christ. 29 For this I toil, struggling with all his energy that he powerfully works within me.

Q: Who is ‘we’?

Those three things in 28 summarize Paul's ministry. To preach, warn, and teach. Preaching is not always teaching, nor is teaching always preaching. Preaching is letting the Word be known; teaching is showing them how to live it. One can be done on a street corner, reaching untouched ears; the other is personal. It happens in personal messages, devotionals, hard conversations. It’s discipleship.

Paul definitely did his fair share of proclaiming Christ. He traveled as far as he could manage before being captured by Rome.

But he also warned about false preaching. We alone have covered it in Philippians 3, Ephesians 4, and even now Paul is writing almost the whole letter in address to the false teachings and ways of the church. Christ even warned us in Matthew 7:15, “15 “Watch out for false prophets. They come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ferocious wolves.”

Then there's teaching, arguably the most valuable of the three. Paul sure thought so and executed accordingly. Teaching with the idea of allowing someone to learn, not just hear words. We have a lot of that today. Preachers on platforms, hosting what Skip called "Spiritual Pep rallies. Christian-themed but not Christ-focused.

This is why expository preaching and discipleship are advantageous for both the believer and the church. Personal wisdom is taught applicably in our lives to not just be hearers of the word (James 1:22) but doers. I’d go so far as to say someone can proclaim and warn you day and night, but if you never truly learn how badly you need Christ and how to change, then it was all for nothing with regard to the soul of the one who heard but did not listen.

Remember, Christ didn’t say convert all nations; he said make disciples. You're either growing or dying, and through discipleship you can learn how to grow. You can mature. Again, Paul says all this, “that we may present everyone mature in Christ.” In 1 Corinthians, Paul calls new believers (and/or worldly believers) infants, babies. He refers to them as the immature. They heard! They were warned! But have learned little in regard to living it. And oftentimes this fails to present Christ in the way it should. This is where church hurt can come from. Someone who wears the “Christian” on a shirt or a necklace but not on their heart is wreaking havoc on the impressions of unbelievers. Understand that we are not made perfect till we’re in glory, but that's the goal. It’s better to aim for the stars and land on the moon than nothing at all.

Again, we should proclaim Christ! We should warn people like Christ did! But we have to teach people how to live Christ in a world where little is taught.

29

For this…Paul toiled.

He labored, struggling. Struggling in this verse is often replaced with striving. The Greek word is “agōnizomai” (ag-o-nid'-zom-ahee). Which means to contend with adversaries; fight metaph. to contend, struggle with difficulties and dangers to endeavour with strenuous zeal; strive: to obtain something.

Paul’s not struggling to commit; he’s struggling because he labored above his maximum capacity. He put literally all he add every moment into this Gospel. Leaving no limitations on himself so that God could work powerfully through him. How it should be, and he knew it too. 1 Corinthians 15:10 “I worked harder than all of them—yet not I, but the grace of God that was with me.”

Spurgeon is quoted for saying “If you plan to be lazy, there are plenty of places where you will not be wanted. But above all, you are not wanted in Christian ministry. The man who finds ministry an easy life will also find it will bring a hard death.” Paul's saying that he puts in great effort, but it’s not his effort that accomplishes it all. It’s Gods. It’s 1% in proportion to God’s 99% but Paul gives the whole percent so that the Lord can work through him.

Philippians 2:12-13

“...continue to work out your salvation with fear and trembling, 13 for it is God who works in you to will and to act in order to fulfill his good purpose.”

This is where burnout happens. When you’re trying to do it all by yourself and not relying on the Lord, that's when you will come up short every single time. With God, nothing is impossible. And I mean truly nothing.

Main Point and Application

Paul introduces to the Church the calling God has placed on his life and how he is a steward for the Lord of his Church. Serving wholeheartedly to the mystery revealed in Christ.

Find your stewardship and serve faithfully in all your capacity.

Group Applications:

Even in times of suffering we can be filled with joy.

We’re tasked with teaching, be bold, be a mirror.

Rejoice in suffering.

Be a steward to Gods will.

Be willing to go through struggles for sake of others and Christ. God gave you a purpose to reach people and grow the body.

Mystery revealed, Jesus’s Christ saving grace is for everyone.

Rejoice, share, lean on him.

Trust God, spread the word.

Reflection:

Am I striving for spiritual maturity for myself and others?

Am I striving to be faithful in my service to the body of Christ?

How can I fully commit to my calling God has given me even though it involves suffering and sacrifice so that I can help others know Christ more deeply?
